# Provisional yearly party-access loop

Status: temporary design sketch for discussion and playtesting. Nothing here is a committed rule or decision.

## Purpose

Replace the current Leverage, Bluff, opening-bid, counterbid, contest-reveal, and Revolving Door structure with a sequential yearly loop built entirely around Operation cards, party access, collection, and closure.

The game still lasts twelve years and holds an Election after years 4, 8, and 12.

The intended tension is:

- Operations change the board now but remain at their party for another player to collect.
- Collecting secures future resources but costs a limited Collection counter and puts the cards out of use for the rest of the current year.
- A party's opening firm controls when that party closes and receives any cards left there at closure.
- A strict majority of opened parties can end the year before a player with a large hand uses every card.

## Removed systems and components

- Remove Leverage cards.
- Remove Bluff cards.
- Remove opening bids, counterbids, bid ranks, equal-bid cancellation, concealed bid stacks, contest reveal, and bid transfer.
- Remove the Pecking Order unless a separate purpose is found for it.
- Remove gifts of Operation cards and points. The new loop does not need a gift system for its first tests.
- Replace each standard player's three interchangeable firm identity cards with one opening Firm card and two Collection counters.

The fixed agendas, district map, Support, operations, party bonuses, coalitions, and Election scoring remain unless separately revised.

Bellweather Centre gains the Capital scoring rule described below. It remains absent from the ordinary district objectives and Election draw.

## Player supplies

| Human players | Firms controlled per player | Party openings per player | Collection counters per player | Operation-card supply |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2–3 | 2 | 2 | 4 | Double |
| 4–6 | 1 | 1 | 2 | Standard |

Each controlled firm supplies one Firm card and one party opening. Collection counters belong to the human player rather than to a particular firm.

At two players, use ABBA party-opening order. At three players, use ABCCBA. These snake orders apply only to Establish Access. Lobbying uses ordinary clockwise human-player turns even when a player controls two firms.

## Year structure

Working phase names:

1. Establish Access
2. Lobby
3. Year End

"Party Openings", "Actions", and "Cleanup" remain usable prototype terms. Establish Access is intended to avoid describing the Firm cards as bids.

### 1. Establish Access

Starting with the Early Bird and proceeding clockwise, each player places a Firm card on a party that does not already have one. At two players, use ABBA order. At three players, use ABCCBA. The turn-around player therefore makes two consecutive party openings.

A party with a Firm card is open. A party without one is unavailable for the current year. Only an open party can receive Operation cards or be the target of Collect.

The player who placed the Firm card is that party's opening player for the year. Only that player may voluntarily Close it. Whenever the party closes for any reason, that player receives all Operation cards still there.

### 2. Lobby

Starting with the Early Bird and proceeding clockwise, players repeatedly take one action. A player whose own party has closed continues taking turns while another party remains open.

#### Operate

Play and resolve 1–3 Operation cards from hand at one open party. Resolve the cards one at a time in the order chosen by the player, then leave them in a single Operation pile at that party.

All cards in one Operate action target the same party. A player cannot include an Operation card that has no legal resolution.

An Operate action can claim at most one party bonus, even when it plays two or three Operation cards. Each of a party's bonuses can still be claimed at most once during the year. Collecting cards does not reset its bonuses.

#### Collect

Spend one available Collection counter and take the complete Operation pile from one open party. The pile must contain at least one card.

Put the cards and spent counter in a discard or New Year area in front of the player. The cards cannot be used again during the current year. The party remains open, and its bonus state does not reset.

The pile does not need to be fanned, sorted, or kept fully readable. Players may remember or track its contents, but the tabletop procedure does not preserve perfect visibility.

"Revolve" remains an alternative thematic name for this action, with "Revolving Door counters" as the corresponding component name. Collect and Collection counter are the clearer working terms.

#### Close

A player cannot Close on their first Lobby turn of the year, even if their first action was Pass.

Return one of the player's Firm cards from its party and close that party. Put every Operation card still at the party into the opening player's New Year area. The cards cannot be used again during the current year.

No player can Operate at or Collect from a closed party for the rest of the year.

#### Pass

Take no action. Any later Operate, Collect, or Close action resets the sequence of consecutive passes.

If every player passes consecutively, immediately close every remaining open party. Each Operation pile goes to the player whose Firm card opened that party.

### Majority closure

Count only the parties opened during the current year. Unavailable parties do not count as already closed.

When a strict majority of this year's opened parties have closed, immediately close every remaining open party. Each remaining Operation pile goes to the player whose Firm card opened that party.

Immediate closure avoids extra mandatory turns, impossible Close actions for players whose parties have already closed, and additional harvesting by players who closed early.

The year can therefore end partway through an orbit. Later players do not receive a compensating turn in the current year. The player who triggered the end becomes the next Early Bird, and ordinary turn order resumes from that player in the next year.

### 3. Year End

When Lobbying ends, finish closing every open party and awarding its Operation pile before proceeding.

1. Finish the action that ended Lobbying, including all automatic closures and pile awards.
2. Move every player's New Year pile into their hand. Unplayed cards already in hand remain there.
3. Return all Firm cards and Collection counters to their owners.
4. Reset every party bonus.
5. Give the Early Bird to the player whose action ended the year. This is the player whose Close triggered strict-majority closure or whose Pass completed the consecutive-pass sequence.
6. After years 4, 8, and 12, hold the Election using the current map and Court state. Resolve ordinary district scoring, relative-seat adjustments, Capital scoring, and Election cleanup in that order.
7. Advance to the next year and reopen no party until its Establish Access phase. After the Election in year 12, the game ends instead.

Collect remains available in year 12. The design accepts that its future-resource value disappears at the end of the game rather than adding a final-year exception.

## Capital scoring

Bellweather Centre has a capacity of three and retains its current connections to Crown Road, Canal Ward, and Old Quarter. It does not hold an Election: do not randomize, draw, return, or otherwise clear its Support on Election Day.

At four to six players, each player's scoring card for the current Election is also their Capital card.

At two or three players, the first scoring card drawn for each compatible pair is automatically its Capital card. Keep it in the pair's first or left-hand position so its identity remains physically fixed without another marker or secret choice. Both cards still provide their normal district objectives; only the first card provides Capital parties.

On Election Day, count how many different parties printed on the player's Capital card are present in Bellweather Centre:

| Printed parties present | Capital points |
| ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 |

Only the exact printed parties count. Coalition-partner Support does not satisfy a printed Capital party. Multiple Support belonging to one party still count as only one party, so duplicate Support can occupy the Centre without improving the Capital set.

Capital points are not part of the player's base district score. Calculate base district scores and apply positive and negative relative-seat references first, then add Capital points to the result. A single matching party scores nothing.

Support in Bellweather Centre remains in place after scoring, preserving its composition as a route, staging area, or blocker for the next Election period.

## Tabletop handling

- Operation cards resolve immediately and then form one pile at their party.
- Collected and closure-awarded cards move as a pile to the receiving player's discard or New Year area.
- The player does not need to display or organize the pile's full contents.
- Cards in a New Year area are unavailable until Year End moves them into hand.
- Firm cards and spent Collection counters can sit with the New Year pile to show that they are unavailable.

## Operation timing consequences

- Party bonuses reset once per year rather than once per contest.
- Operation cards resolve during the action that plays them; there is no later party-resolution phase.
- The existing party bonuses otherwise retain their current effects. Night Shift is the only timing exception: replace its delayed effect provisionally with **Double Rally:** after its Night Parliament Rally resolves, immediately repeat that Rally in the same district against the updated board. The second Rally does not require another card and does not claim another bonus.
- Removing the Pecking Order makes year-end timing determine the Early Bird. The player whose action ends the year becomes the next Early Bird.

## Expected strategic effects

- Opening a party grants a closing right and a claim on its residual pile, not exclusive access to operate there.
- Players can use operations on their own party and try to reclaim them by closing, but every opponent acts between successive turns and may Collect the pile first.
- Collect can intercept a valuable pile without closing the party, allowing another pile to accumulate later in the year.
- Early closure can deny a useful party to rivals.
- Strict-majority closure lets enough opening players end the year despite opposition from a player with a large hand.
- Hidden agendas remain the main source of uncertainty and misdirection after Bluff cards disappear.
- Bellweather Centre rewards assembling exact three-party sets while duplicate Support can deny the final matching party a space.

## Risks to test

1. **Whole-pile concentration:** One Collection counter can still acquire an arbitrarily large pile. The yearly cooldown and limited counters may be sufficient costs, but reserve sizes should be recorded.
2. **Action volume:** A player with many cards can take more effective turns. Measure whether majority closure and exposed Operation piles provide enough counterplay.
3. **Seat-order pile sniping:** The player acting immediately after an Operate action gets the first opportunity to Collect its pile. Test whether this feels tactical or overly determined by seating.
4. **Opening and first-action advantage:** The Early Bird chooses a party first and takes the first Lobby action. Earning the marker by ending a year may or may not distribute both advantages fairly.
5. **Early denial:** An opening player can Close from their second Lobby turn onward. Test whether one full table orbit provides enough opportunity to use the party.
6. **Bonus exhaustion:** Once-per-year bonuses may make early Operate actions disproportionately valuable.
7. **Final-year Collect:** Collect has little or no material value in year 12. Leave it unchanged for the first test and observe whether it creates confusion or empty delay.
8. **Low-player scaling:** Doubled openings, counters, and cards preserve component ratios but may create different closure coalitions and action lengths.
9. **Capital incentives:** One matching party scores nothing, while a complete set scores three points. Record whether this produces contested set-building or makes partial Capital positions too unrewarding.
10. **Persistent Capital lock:** The Centre does not thin at Elections. Record whether a full Centre remains interactive through Organise and Smear or becomes an ignored obstruction for multiple Election periods.

## First playtest

Test at four players before resolving low-player details. Record for every year:

- Operation cards played by each player.
- Cards gained through each Collect and Close.
- Largest party pile and largest player hand.
- Which end condition fired: consecutive passes or strict-majority closure.
- Number of Lobby turns and consecutive effective turns by each player.
- Party bonuses claimed and the turn on which each was claimed.
- Capital parties present, Capital points scored, and whether its Support changed during the following Election period.
- Whether players could explain why they operated, collected, closed, or passed.

The first test should determine whether public pile timing and majority closure create enough interaction to replace competitive bidding, rather than attempt to balance every Operation family immediately.
