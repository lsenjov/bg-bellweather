Worked examples

Only the middle region scores

Ada’s named parties (including coalition partners) have 4 Urban, 6 Mixed and 7 Outlying elected Support. Her base score is 6. With 4, 6, 6 she also scores 6. Add Gain/Lose references and Capital afterward.

Two cards score separately

Bert’s two cards score regional totals 4/4/0 and 0/4/4. Their middle scores are 4 and 4, so his base is 8. Do not combine them into 4/8/4 and score 4. Ignore Gain/Lose at two or three players and use the first card’s parties for Capital.

Off-home Court precedes a printed Court

Ada plays Honeycomb’s Common Cause at open Foxglove. First, Foxglove Courts Honeycomb, adding one Court Support in each direction and updating both Targets. Then resolve the card’s printed Court at Foxglove toward its selected target, followed by the move. If either required part cannot resolve, undo the whole card, including the automatic Court, and leave it in hand.

ABBA applies only to Party Openings

State: Ada is Early Bird in a two-player game. Each player must open two different parties.

Resolution: Party Openings occur Ada, Bert, Bert, Ada. When the Lobby begins, turns simply continue clockwise from Ada: Ada, Bert, Ada, Bert, and so on.

Why: The snake order gives later players a fair opening choice without changing the ordinary action orbit.

Cards resolve before the next card is chosen

State: Ada holds Riverworks’ Public Works and Canal Network Bonus cards and begins an Operate action at Riverworks with an ordinary Rally.

Resolution: Ada chooses and resolves the Rally, leaving it in Riverworks’ pile. She may then finish or play either Bonus card against the changed board. If she plays Public Works successfully, it resolves as a complete standalone Rally action and returns immediately to its Riverworks home. Ada may still finish or play a third ordinary card at Riverworks; the third ends the action automatically.

Why: Every card is chosen sequentially, while the first card locks the open party for the complete Operate action.

Quiet Hours occupies an empty district

State: Ada holds Night Parliament’s Quiet Hours Bonus card and operates at open Night Parliament, selecting Harbormouth for its printed Rally and empty Meadow for its additional effect.

Resolution: Resolve the Rally, then add one Night Parliament Support to Meadow. Both placements must remain legal together; otherwise the whole card is illegal and stays in Ada’s hand. On success, Quiet Hours returns immediately to its home at Night Parliament.

Why: A Bonus card is one atomic standalone action. Quiet Hours may also select an empty Bellweather Centre.

Collect takes a pile out of circulation

State: Riverworks is open with four Operation cards in its pile, Public Works is at its Riverworks home, and Bert has a Collection counter available.

Resolution: Bert spends one counter and moves the entire pile to his New Year area. He may also move Public Works there, or leave it home. Riverworks remains open and may receive more Operations later this year. Bert cannot use any collected card until Cleanup releases it to his private hand.

Why: Collection harvests the current pile without reserving or closing the party.

Collect may take an empty pile

State: Foxglove is open with no Operations in its pile. Spin is at Foxglove, and Ada has a Collection counter.

Resolution: Ada may spend the counter to Collect Foxglove. She may take Spin into New Year or decline it; either way, the empty Operation pile remains empty and Foxglove stays open. The Collect would also be legal if no Bonus card were there.

Why: An open party, not the contents available there, determines Collect eligibility. Spending a counter for no cards is allowed.

Only the opener may Close

State: Ada opened Honeycomb. On Bert’s turn, Honeycomb holds a valuable pile. Ada has already taken one Lobby turn this year.

Resolution: Bert cannot Close Honeycomb, although he may Collect its pile. On Ada’s next turn she may Close it, take its current pile into her New Year area, optionally take one Bonus card still home there, and return her Firm marker face up. This Bonus choice is available even if Honeycomb’s pile is empty. Honeycomb remains closed unless a later card opens it again.

Why: Closure and automatic closure award the pile to the current owner of the Firm marker on that party.

A strict majority ends the Lobby at turn end

State: Six parties opened. Two are already closed. Ada legally closes a third.

Resolution: Three returned Firm markers among six openings is not more than half, so play continues. If Bert closes a fourth and returns its marker, finish that Close, then end the Lobby before the next player acts. Unclosed party piles are awarded to their current Firm owners; later players do not finish the orbit.

Why: The end trigger is a strict majority of returned Firm markers, checked at the end of every Lobby turn rather than between cards in an Operate action.

Pass waits for every personal Closure

State: In a two-player game, Ada opened Honeycomb and Riverworks. She has closed Honeycomb, while Riverworks remains open.

Resolution: Ada cannot Pass because one of her two Firm markers is still on a party. After a later Close returns her Riverworks marker, she may Pass on future turns. Her Pass changes no Firm markers; apply the ordinary end-of-turn majority check, then continue if the year remains active.

Why: Pass becomes available only after every Firm marker that player controls has returned. The returned-Firm majority remains the sole Lobby-ending state.

Institutional Memory may resolve only part of a card

State: SC-01 is face up from an earlier Election. Honeycomb can move into a free Urban district and Old Shell into a free Mixed district, but no Outlying destination is free for Foxglove.

Resolution: Ada may choose only the Honeycomb objective, only the Old Shell objective, or both. She cannot choose the blocked Foxglove objective. At least one objective must be chosen.

Why: Institutional Memory explicitly permits a non-empty legal subset; “for each chosen objective” does not require all three.

Shell Firm can move an opponent’s Firm to a closed party

State: Ada operates at Foxglove, whose Firm belongs to Bert and whose pile contains two cards. Old Shell closed earlier and currently has no Firm marker.

Resolution: Ada plays Shell Firm and moves Bert’s Foxglove Firm marker and both pile cards to Old Shell. Foxglove now has no marker; Old Shell is open under Bert’s Firm ownership. Ada’s Lobby action ends immediately. The number of returned markers has not changed.

Why: Shell Firm moves the physical marker and pile, not their owner, and a closed party satisfies “without a Firm marker.”

Mass Transit vacates its first district

State: Orchard contains Honeycomb Support, Crown Road contains Old Shell Support, Canal Ward contains Foxglove Support, and Northgate has a free spot. Those four districts form a path in that order.

Resolution: Move Honeycomb from Orchard to Crown Road, Old Shell from Crown Road to Canal Ward, and Foxglove from Canal Ward to Northgate. Resolve the shifts together: Orchard loses one Support, the intermediate districts keep the same occupancy, and the route stops in Northgate.

Why: The chosen free spot is the endpoint, not the start of a loop.

Midnight Session changes the end-of-turn count

State: Four Firm markers were placed during Party Openings and three are currently returned. On Ada’s turn, she plays Midnight Session at an eligible acting party. Old Shell is closed and Ada has a returned Firm marker.

Resolution: Ada puts her returned marker on Old Shell, which opens empty under her ownership. Only two of the four opening markers are now returned, so finishing the Operate action does not end the year. If Old Shell closes later, its pile and another optional Bonus card can be awarded again.

Why: The majority is checked after the complete turn using current marker locations, and earlier Closure awards are not undone.

Cleanup precedes Election scoring

State: Year 2 has ended and Ada collected a Rally card during that year.

Resolution: Complete Cleanup first: close remaining parties and resolve their optional Bonus-card awards, return Firm markers, release all New Year cards such as Ada’s Rally to private hands, return Collection counters, and move Early Bird. Then reveal Election 1 agendas and score. The released Rally cannot affect that Election because there is no Lobby action between Cleanup and scoring.

Why: Elections occur after the ordinary year boundary, leaving Cleanup uniform in all six years. The thematic midterm boundaries after odd years do not alter Cleanup.

The first card fixes low-player Capital scoring

State: Bert’s compatible Election pair is SC-05 followed by SC-06. Bellweather Centre contains two parties named on SC-05 and all three parties named on SC-06.

Resolution: Add each card’s independently calculated middle-region score. Only SC-05 supplies Capital parties, so Bert gains 1 Capital point for having two of its three parties present. He cannot choose SC-06 for 3 points.

Why: The first card in a compatible pair is locked as its Capital card when the pair is formed.

Final card ties take the higher occupied rank

State: After Year 6 Cleanup, four players hold 5, 8, 8, and 10 cards. Each total combines ordinary Operation cards and held Bonus cards; Bonus cards at their party homes are excluded.

Resolution: The player with 5 cards scores 0. Both players with 8 score 2 because two other players have totals less than or equal to theirs. The player with 10 scores 3. Add 0, 2, 2, and 3 respectively after ordinary Election 3 scoring.

Why: Ranking slots run from 0 through one less than the player count, and a tie uses its highest occupied slot. In a low-player game, count each human player’s one combined hand rather than separate Firm supplies.
