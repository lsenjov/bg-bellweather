Policy cards

Policy component prototype · 2026-10-18

Components/Policy cards

Thirty unique policies: every ordered pair of six issues appears once. Each proposal face shows its name, + and − issues, local flavour text, and six ordered party votes. The enacted face repeats the name and issues, with the existing ability name, full rules text, explicit Party or Global scope, and consequence flavour. There are 26 Party cards and 4 Global cards. The + row shows three supporting parties; the − row shows three opposing parties. Every party tile uses its established colour, emblem watermark and name, with explicit For/Against labels. Print the 30-card deck.

Setup and regional votes

The physical map uses Urban (18 Support / 9 votes), Industrial Belt (14 / 7), Outlying (16 / 8) and Centre (3 / 3, no thinning). The app and current rulebook use the same board specification.

Shuffle once. Deal one policy face up to each of Urban, Industrial Belt, Outlying and Bellwether Centre during setup, then after Elections 1 and 2. Keep all four visible during the following two years. Deal without replacement; failed policies stay discarded. Only twelve cards are used in a game.

After Years 2, 4 and 6 Cleanup, thin each non-Centre district randomly to half its capacity: retain 3, 2 or 1 Support in capacity-6, -4 or -2 districts, or all Support if fewer are present. Centre is a fourth region with one capacity-3 district: retain all its Support.

Each surviving Support casts one vote on its region’s policy, following its party’s printed position. More For than Against passes; ties, including 0–0, fail. Count all four votes before activating any newly passed effects. Previously enacted laws remain active.

Party laws are permanent abilities of the party that supplied the most surviving For votes in that policy’s region when it passed; among tied supporters, the leftmost printed party wins. Parties voting Against are ineligible. Flip passed policies to their enacted face. Keep each Party policy enacted face up beside its recipient’s party board, separate from Bonus cards and Operation piles. The award never transfers when Support, Firm access or ownership changes. Global laws stay in a shared display and affect every player. Laws accumulate without repeal. Opposite policies can both pass. There are at most eight laws during ongoing play and twelve after the final election. Do not deal new policies after Election 3.

These prototype orders are fixed and public. A party votes For exactly when + ranks above −. The three opposite pairs are Honeycomb/Foxglove, Old Shell/Riverworks and Many Wings/Night Parliament. Within each vote row, parties are printed from largest absolute gap between the + and − issue ranks to smallest, then by whichever ranks the + issue higher. Law resolution

Apply a Party law only when its recipient is the acting party, whoever is playing the card. This includes off-home party cards whose baseline in the party-card Operation table matches the relevant Operation, even though their card text prints effects directly. Moving another party’s Support does not activate that party’s laws; standalone Bonus effects follow their own text. Standalone movement or placement is governed by its own card. Each distinct law effect applies at most once per card action, even if two enacted policies print it or two Support move. An Operate turn may resolve several separate card actions.

Combine applicable movement permissions: a law may change where Organise can go while Political Exchange permits a swap. With Carpooling, each of two arriving Support needs a free spot or a rival swapped back to the source. Choose the rivals; both arrivals use the same source and destination.

Resolve the baseline Operation, then all triggered extras, including the Bonus extra and law follow-ups, in the acting firm’s chosen order. Law extra placements and movements are mandatory when possible and otherwise skipped; an unavailable law extra does not block the original Operation. Text offering an alternative remains a choice.

Follow-ups trigger only from the original Operation, never from other follow-ups. Bridge Campaign and Cross-Region Campaigns compare the original source and destination regions, including Centre. Crossing a region boundary triggers both when awarded to the acting party: Bridge Campaign adds at the destination and Cross-Region Campaigns at the source. A move within one region triggers neither, even if its route passes through another region. Carpooling does not double extras. No loops.

Identical effects do not stack for the same acting party; copies awarded to different parties work independently. Different applicable effects combine. Duplicate Global scoring effects apply only once. All normal capacity limits and the map’s adjacency rules still apply unless a law explicitly changes the relevant movement or targeting permission.

Final scoring

Each firm reveals its one secret issue-priority card after the final election. Every player scores every enacted policy, whether Party or Global and regardless of its recipient: gain the + issue’s value and lose the − issue’s value. Failed and undealt policies score nothing. Global scoring laws from the final election affect scoring, including policies enacted earlier or simultaneously. Party laws awarded at the final election have no later actions to modify, but their policies still score.

Muted Priorities changes the printed 6-point issue to 4; Broad Interests changes the printed 1-point issue to 3. They do not reorder priorities or change party votes. Both gains and losses use the adjusted values. Laws that double an issue’s score are excluded from this prototype.

Add the retained final hand-rank bonus: count other human players with fewer than or the same number of ordinary Operation and held Bonus cards after Year 6 Cleanup. Score one point per such player. Home Bonus cards, policy cards and scoring cards do not count. At 2–3 players rank each human’s one combined hand. Final totals may be negative; highest wins, with shared victory on a tie. There is no Capital or regional agenda scoring.

Fifteen effects, two copies each

1. Grassroots Expansion · Party

Rally may place its Support in a neighboring district instead of its source district.

6. Fresh Start · Party

When Organise or Rally restores a party absent from the map, place two Support instead of one, together or separately. Place both if possible; otherwise place one.

7. Carpooling · Party

Organise may move two Support together from the same source to the same destination.

9. Established Networks · Party

Organise may move between any two districts already containing acting-party Support, regardless of distance.

10. Political Exchange · Party

Organise may swap moving Support with rival Support at the destination. Each arrival needs a free spot or a rival to swap back to the source.

11. Long-Reach Campaigns · Party

Smear may target a district up to two adjacency steps from acting-party Support.

14. Displacement · Party

Instead of removing its target, Smear may move that Support to a free spot in a neighboring district.

17. Cross-Region Campaigns · Party

After Organise moves Support across a region boundary, add one acting-party Support in the source district.

19. Muted Priorities · Global

At final scoring, each firm treats its printed 6-point issue as worth 4 for both gains and losses. Party votes do not change.

20. Broad Interests · Global

At final scoring, each firm treats its printed 1-point issue as worth 3 for both gains and losses. Party votes do not change.

22. New Constituencies · Party

Rally may place its Support anywhere in its source region, provided the destination has no acting-party Support.

28. Chain Migration · Party

After Organise moves Support, move a different acting-party Support from a neighboring district into a spot vacated in the source district.

29. Bridge Campaign · Party

After Organise moves Support across a region boundary, add one acting-party Support at its destination.

30. Regional Express · Party

Organise may move to any district in its source region, regardless of distance.

35. Crowded Airwaves · Party

Smear may target any full district, regardless of acting-party presence or distance.

Physical card faces

Thirty 63 × 88 mm cards. The proposal face is displayed during voting; inspect the reverse whenever needed. Passed policies flip to their enacted face. Failed policies are discarded with no effect or score. The flavour on either face adds local context, never extra rules. Shuffle and draw with both faces concealed so the unique reverse does not identify the next policy.

The printable deck has four pairs of A4 portrait sheets, fronts followed by their matching backs. Print at 100%, double-sided, flipping on the long edge. Back columns are mirrored, including empty slots on the last sheet. Check alignment on the first sheet pair before printing the rest.

Bonus interactions

The firm freely orders the Bonus extra and all law extras together. Required Bonus extras must succeed or the entire card action is illegal. Midnight Leak skips if no matching rival is available in a neighboring district. Dig In refills its source if possible and otherwise skips. With Displacement, resolve Smear Bonuses normally against the same rival and original target district: Spin fills the vacated spot; Stonewall removes another matching rival there; Midnight Leak targets a neighbor of the original district.
