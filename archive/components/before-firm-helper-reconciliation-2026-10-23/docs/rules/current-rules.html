Index/Current rules

Across six years, rival lobbying firms establish party access, perform political Operations, collect public card piles for later years, and close their own contacts before three Elections decide Bellwether’s future.

Decided

Players, duration, and objective

The game supports two to six human players. It lasts six years. Hold an Election after Years 2, 4, and 6; after the third Election’s final card rank bonus, every player tied for the highest cumulative point balance shares the win.

Years 1, 3, and 5 are midterm years. Hecate can place Election prophecies only in these years, predicting the following Election.

Influence the four regions to pass policies that suit your secret issue priorities. Each enacted policy gains its + issue value and loses its − issue value at game end. Preserve cards for the final hand-ranking bonus.

Working title: Ladder Bidding. The auction mechanic has been removed; the title remains open to change.

Decided

Components

  • One fifteen-district Bellwether map with fifty-one Support spaces.
  • Six parties, unlimited Support for each, public areas for yearly Firm markers, Operation piles, and three Party-card spaces per party.
  • Six supernatural Firm identities with one yearly opening marker each (two per player at two or three players), their agents and firm cards; Fiddler favour tokens and six Hecate reward cards.
  • Common cards: Organise, Rally, and Smear; plus eighteen unique collectible party cards. Firm cards and Hecate reward cards are separate categories. See the print inventory for the current prototype specifications.
  • Two Collection counters per player, doubled to four at two or three players.
  • Thirty policy cards, twelve secret issue scoring cards, one public sheet, one private sheet, one privacy shield, one single-sided A5 landscape firm reference and one double-sided A5 portrait shared reference, four reversible vote markers and four reversible range markers per human player, one covered New Year area per player with its total count visible, an Early Bird marker, a Year/Election tracker, and a final score ledger. Physical Collection counters use cubes.

Leverage cards, Bluff cards, gifts, bid markers, counterbid pieces, and the Pecking Order are not used. Their former specification is retained in the Ruleset 14 component archive.

Decided

Public and private information

Exact cards in hand and issue scoring cards until the final Election are private. Placed prophecies, agents, portrait states, favours and Hecate’s reward supply are public. Card backs identify common cards, each party’s cards, each Firm’s cards and Hecate reward cards separately. All common cards share one back; each party and Firm has its own shared back; all six Hecate reward cards share a distinct back. Combined hand size, combined New Year area size, Operation cards in party piles, Party cards at their home parties, Firm markers on open parties or returned to players, Collection counters, points, Support, public party rankings, pending policies, enacted laws and discarded policies are public.

When you Collect or receive cards from a Closure, place them in your New Year area. They cannot be used during the current year. The exact contents are private, though the total card count remains public.

Decided

The six-year structure

  1. Party resurgence: restore parties absent at the start of the year.
  2. Party Openings: establish access to different parties.
  3. Fiddler’s opening burst: spend last year’s favours; there are none in Year 1.
  4. Lobby: take clockwise turns from Early Bird.
  5. Year-end: resolve remaining Closures, then the scheduled Election, then refresh counters and prepare the next year.
  6. New Year: release New Year cards into hands, then begin the next year with party resurgence and Party Openings.
Decided

Party resurgence

At the start of each year, before Party Openings, identify every party with no Support on the board. Keep this list fixed for the whole resurgence. Starting with Early Bird, take one clockwise trip around the players. Each player places one Support of each identified party, in their chosen order, before the next player places theirs. Each placement may use any free district capacity slot, regardless of region or agent locations. Each human places once per party, including at two or three players. Skip placements if no capacity remains.

Ordinary absent-party recovery during the year is unchanged. A party recovered before the next year starts does not qualify for resurgence. There is no new year or resurgence after the final Election.

Decided

1. Party Openings

The Fiddler, if present, makes the first opening. Remaining players open in clockwise order starting with Early Bird and skipping the Fiddler. Without the Fiddler, start with Early Bird. Place an unused opening marker on a party without one; its owner receives that party’s Closure. A party with a marker is open to every player; without one it is unavailable for common and party cards.

At two or three players, reverse the first-round order for a second opening round. The Fiddler’s primary opening must be a different party in each of the six years; its second opening is unrestricted. Mark its primary openings with permanent history tokens on those parties, separate from spendable favours. After openings and the Fiddler’s burst, Early Bird takes the first regular Lobby turn. Lobby turns always proceed clockwise.

Decided

2. Lobby actions

On your turn, take exactly one action. Then play passes clockwise unless that action ends the year.

Operate

Play one to three cards sequentially from hand. Choose each card and its complete parameters against the updated board, resolve it fully, then choose whether to continue. Each card can use a different acting party; the acting party remains fixed within that card. Common and party cards require an open acting party. Firm cards require none. You cannot change Lobby action after starting Operate.

A common card resolves its Operation through an eligible agent, then enters the acting party’s public pile once. A party card occupies one slot, resolves without agent-location requirements, then returns to its home party, even if home is closed. It can be played off-home and held indefinitely. Firm cards normally enter their owner’s New Year area; placed prophecies remain placed until resolved or withdrawn. Hecate reward cards require an open acting party, fixed for the entire card, resolve an agent-authorised Operation, then return to her reward supply.

Party cards print their effects directly and never require an agent at the effect’s location. Their Operation column below still identifies which baseline receives the chosen acting party’s laws: Waggle Route and Dig In use Organise; Public Works, Scatter the Flock and Quiet Hours use Rally; Stonewall, Spin and Midnight Leak use Smear. The first move, addition or removal is that baseline; the remaining printed effect is its party-card extra. Organise cards retain voluntary agent transport. Cards marked Unbound, including Whisper Network, follow their own text and do not trigger Operation laws. Choose an open acting party for every party card; where the card names a chosen party or moves or adds one party’s Support, that is the acting party. Mixed-party cards follow their own printed selection rules.

Resolve each card atomically. The firm freely orders all triggered extras together, including the party-card extra and law follow-ups. Law extras are mandatory when possible and otherwise skipped. Required party-card extras must succeed or the entire play is illegal. An effect that says “up to” must still move, add or remove at least one Support; Institutional Memory may choose any subset of matching parties, at most one Support each, all in the chosen district. Whisper Network swaps both Support simultaneously and needs no free spot; choose the acting party’s Support and a different party’s Support in a neighbouring district. Dig In skips a full source; Midnight Leak skips when no matching rival is available in a neighboring district. Displacement leaves Smear party cards referring to the same rival and original target district. A successful party card returns home immediately, even if home is closed; no retroactive award occurs.

Collect

For Collect, Close and automatic Closure, take exactly one Party card when any are available at that party, even if its Operation pile is empty. You choose which card. Take none only when none are available.

Spend one of your Collection counters and choose any open party. Take its complete Operation pile into your New Year area and take one Party card from that party if available. The pile may be empty, and the action remains legal when no Party card is available. The party remains open and can receive new Operations. Any player may Collect any party, including one they opened or previously collected.

Close

Choose an open party bearing one of your Firm markers. Take its complete Operation pile into your New Year area, take one Party card from that party if available, then return the Firm marker face up to your play area. The Party choice remains available even when the Operation pile is empty. Without its marker, the party is closed and accepts no more Lobby actions unless a card opens it again. A returned marker is normally unavailable until the next year, but Midnight Session can place one on a closed party.

You cannot Close during your first Lobby turn of the year. Only the player who currently owns the Firm marker on a party can Close it. At the end of every Lobby turn, count the Firm markers placed during Party Openings that are currently returned. If strictly more than half are returned, the year ends. Do not check between cards in an Operate action. Resolve automatic Closures in Firm-number order, 1–6, skipping absent Firms. At two or three players each owner resolves both Closures together in their chosen order, including all prophecy effects.

Pass

You may Pass only after every Firm marker you placed during Party Openings has returned. Do nothing, then apply the ordinary end-of-turn check and continue clockwise if the year remains active.

Who becomes Early Bird?

The player whose Lobby turn ends with a strict majority of Firm markers returned becomes the next Early Bird.

Decided

Agents and card categories

Each player’s Firm has its own agents. Agents occupy no Support spaces and have no general district limit. Common cards are Organise, Rally and Smear cards played into party piles. Party cards are the eighteen former Bonus cards. Firm cards are the personal movement and ability cards belonging to each Firm, including Hecate’s prophecies. Hecate reward cards are her separate six-card Operation supply. An Operation effect does not change a card’s category.

For a common or Hecate reward Operation, an eligible agent must be in Organise’s source district, Rally’s placement district, or Smear’s attacking district containing acting-party Support. Smear can target that district or a neighbour. If the acting party has no Support anywhere, the eligible agent instead occupies the placement or removal district; recovery Organise has no source and transports no agents. Laws grant their extras without additional agent presence.

When resolving Organise, the active player may voluntarily carry any number of their own movable agents in its source district with the Support to its destination. Waggle Route and Dig In also resolve Organise and may transport agents despite printing their effects directly. Other Support-moving effects, including Whisper Network’s swap, do not transport agents. Portraits, the puppeteer and marionettes never ride. Agents remain where they are through Elections and New Year. There is no forced Pass exception: players cannot Pass while any of their opening markers remains on a party.

Set up agents after initial Support and policies are revealed, in Firm order 1–6, skipping absent Firms. Each player completes their Firm’s setup before the next. All Firm cards start in hand. Firm cards and Hecate reward cards are excluded from final hand ranking.

Decided

1. First Chair Consulting — The Fiddler

He used to stand at crossroads, but now prefers the air-conditioned comfort of hallways. An excellent, unfashionable suit, comfortable shoes and a backwards visitor’s badge; a briefcase in one hand and a fiddle case over his shoulder. His shadow branches down both hallways.

Place his single agent in any district first. Start with zero Favours Owed; all favours must be earned during play. His firm cards are two identical Just Passing Through cards (move one neighbouring district) and two identical I Can Wait cards (do nothing); each uses a slot and goes to New Year.

One More Thing: cards played in one turn must have different names. If the third card is common or movement, resolve it once, then optionally a second time if legal against the updated board. A common card uses the same acting party both times, enters its pile only once, and receives each law at most once for the whole card; choose an eligible resolution for its extra. Resolve extras before proceeding. The agent may independently accompany each Organise, permitting two steps, or moving two Support from one source while staying until the second movement or throughout. Party cards are never doubled.

A third-slot common or party card places one favour on its acting party, at most one per party, even if the repeat is declined or impossible. Movement and I Can Wait do not place favours.

A Few Calls to Make: after Party Openings, before regular Lobby turns, spend or discard every favour earned last year, in any chosen order. There are no favours to spend in Year 1; the first possible burst is at the start of Year 2, using favours earned in Year 1. Each buys either a one-step agent move or a normal common Operation through the agent for that favour’s party. Operations require an open party and receive laws once per favour action; favours on closed parties can buy movement only. These standalone actions use no card, trigger neither One More Thing nor new favours, and cannot be saved. Year 6 favours have no later burst.

Decided

2. Second Opinion Advisory — Doppelgängers

Place two interchangeable agents in any two distinct districts. They can never occupy the same district. Four identical firm movement cards each move one agent one neighbouring district, then optionally move the other one neighbouring district if legal. Choose the first agent freely and resolve the moves sequentially; neither may enter the other’s current district. Neighbouring agents are allowed. Other players’ agents do not restrict movement or mirroring.

Mirror the pile: only the first common card you play on each acting party in a turn can be mirrored. Before resolving it, note the top common card already in that party’s pile, if any. Choose one agent to resolve the played Operation first. The other agent may then resolve the noted card’s Operation using the same acting party. Check the copied Operation’s legality and choose its parameters against the board when it resolves; it need not have been legal before the first Operation. Each agent performs at most one of these two Operations. Each Smear may choose its own legal rival party.

The played card uses one card slot and enters the acting party’s pile once, after resolution, covering the card noted for mirroring. The copied card stays in the pile. Copy only its Operation: the copy costs no extra card or slot, is not a card play, cannot trigger another mirror, and does not count as playing that Operation for a prophecy. Both Operations require normal agent positioning and obey the acting party’s laws. Resolve them sequentially, including voluntary Organise transport, while keeping the agents in distinct districts. Each law triggers at most once for the whole played card; choose an eligible resolution.

If the pile was empty, or the copy is declined or illegal, that party’s first-card opportunity is still spent for this turn. Every later common card you play on that party resolves normally through one agent. Each party has its own opportunity, reset at the start of your next turn, so three different parties may each provide a mirror in one turn. Party and firm cards do not use these opportunities and are not mirrored by this ability. Party cards resolve once and bypass agents. No mirroring declaration or isolation requirement applies.

Decided

3. Third Party Interests — Hecate

Place three interchangeable sisters in any districts, sharing allowed. Each authorises all common Operations. Her seventeen firm cards comprise three movement cards (move one sister one neighbouring district) and fourteen prophecies below. All seventeen firm cards start in hand. Her separate public reward supply begins with two Organise, two Rally and two Smear reward cards, none in hand.

All prophecies are public and can be placed remotely: at most one prophecy on each individual region, district, party and Firm. There is no overall cap. A prophecy can only be the third card of an Operate action, following any two non-prophecy cards. Hecate cannot fulfil a prophecy during a turn when she plays one; if she has already fulfilled one that turn she cannot play one. A prophecy stays placed across years until it resolves or she sends it to New Year, for free at any time. There is no additional tracked once-per-year restriction; a returned card can be replayed when otherwise legal.

On success choose one available Hecate reward card and take it directly into hand for use on a subsequent turn; it cannot be played on the turn it is earned. If none is available, lose that reward. Independently, optionally move one sister one neighbouring district for free. Chains are permitted without a special exception. Resolve rewards after the triggering card finishes and before the next card; where timing has no effect, take them as soon as the result is clear. Reward cards use ordinary slots and normal agent requirements, return directly to the supply after use, and do not enter party piles or New Year.

  • Five Election prophecies: place on a region in a midterm year (1, 3 or 5) only, predicting its next Election’s final For-minus-Against margin: +5 or greater, +1 to +4, 0, −4 to −1, or −5 or lower. Ties still fail the policy. On success gain the full prophecy reward twice, resolving each reward separately: choose an available reward card and optionally move a sister each time. The same sister may move twice, or two sisters once each; an empty card supply does not prevent either movement. This increased Election reward is awaiting playtesting. Resolve after thinning and voting; success or failure sends the card to New Year, before that Election’s New Year release. Returned cards may be reused within a valid midterm placement window.
  • Three pile prophecies: predict 0–2, 3–4 or 5–6 cards when the named party’s pile is next taken through Collect, Close or automatic Closure. Count the pile, excluding the separately awarded party card. Another player taking the matching count succeeds; a different count or Hecate taking the pile fails. Either result sends the prophecy to New Year.
  • Three Firm prophecies: name Organise, Rally or Smear on another Firm. Wait for its next turn containing common-card play; fulfil immediately on the matching common card, otherwise fail when that turn ends. Earlier nonmatching common cards do not fail it mid-turn. Turns without common cards leave it pending. Firm, party and reward cards do not count even if they resolve those Operations. Success and failure send it to New Year; there is no penalty to the target.
  • Three district-entry traps: place in any district other than Bellwether Centre, with at most one of her traps placed per region. This limit is separate from Election prophecies; a region may have both an Election prophecy and a trap. Another player’s agent entering the named district triggers it, including Organise transport but not Support movement alone. That player chooses one common card from hand to send to their New Year area, or pays nothing if they have none. Hecate receives the standard success reward and movement even if nothing was paid. Resolve once per entry event even if several agents enter, then send the trap to New Year.
Decided

4. Four Familiar Faces — The Witch

Place witch, crow, cat and toad together in one chosen district. The witch cannot authorise common Operations. Crow authorises Organise, cat Rally and toad Smear. Immediately before resolving its assigned Operation, that familiar may move one neighbouring district; it must then legally perform that Operation, never using a common card merely to move. The step is before, not after, the Operation. Laws need no additional familiar presence. Party cards bypass positioning and grant no free familiar step.

Three firm cards—Crow, Cat and Toad—each teleport either the witch to that familiar or that familiar to the witch, anywhere on the map. Before or after teleporting, you may move the named familiar to a neighbouring district once, regardless of which agent teleports. The teleport is required; the familiar’s step is optional. Then send the card to New Year. All her agents follow the general voluntary Organise transport rule.

Decided

5. Five Strings Attached — The Puppeteer

Choose the puppeteer’s starting district, then move four times to neighbouring districts, leaving one of four marionettes in each departure district. Revisited districts and sharing are allowed. Only marionettes authorise common Operations, each able to Organise, Rally or Smear.

After resolving an entire common card, including law effects, retrieve the marionette that authorised it, move the puppeteer exactly one neighbouring district, and leave that marionette in the departure district. This cycle is mandatory and happens once per card. Neither puppeteer nor marionettes accompanies Organise; the used marionette follows this cycle rather than the moved Support.

Three identical personal movement cards each retrieve any marionette, move the puppeteer exactly one neighbouring district, then leave it in the departure district. No legal Operation or local Support is required; several can be played within the three-card limit. Party cards do not trigger the cycle. Puppeteer and marionettes can share districts without limit.

Decided

6. Six Figure Consulting — The Haunted Gallery

Place six portraits in six distinct districts and choose three awake. Exactly three are always awake and three asleep. Awake portraits authorise common Operations in the usual location. Party cards use any awake portrait regardless of their effect’s location. After the complete common or party card resolves, including laws, the used portrait sleeps and a portrait that was asleep before that card wakes. Resolve the network change after the card, never during it.

Two identical Overnight Shipping firm cards each move one portrait, awake or asleep, to any district on the map without changing its state. Portraits never accompany Organise. Sharing districts later is allowed.

Districts containing awake portraits are adjacent for all effects. The gallery uses this adjacency freely. Whenever another player uses it, pay one common or party card from hand to the gallery’s hand before using the connection; firm and Hecate reward cards cannot pay. One toll covers all uses within one played card; a separate effect using adjacency pays separately. No eligible payment means no access. Normal geographic adjacency stays free. This applies to Support movement, Smear, agent movement and every other neighbour-based effect, not just agent travel.

Policies and laws

Each party has a fixed public priority order. It votes For when a policy’s + issue outranks its − issue, otherwise Against. Each policy prints all six votes in coloured party tiles. Opposing priority pairs ensure three parties on each side.

Party laws are permanent abilities of the party that supplied the most surviving For votes in that policy’s region when it passed; among tied supporters, the leftmost printed party wins. Parties voting Against are ineligible. Keep each Party policy face up beside its recipient’s party board, separate from Party cards and Operation piles. The award never transfers when Support, Firm access or ownership changes. Global laws stay in a shared display and affect every player. Apply a Party law only when its recipient is the acting party, whoever is playing the card. This includes off-home Party cards whose Operation column identifies the relevant baseline, even though their card text prints the effect directly. Moving another party’s Support does not activate that party’s laws; standalone Party effects follow their own text. All thirteen Operation effects are Party; Muted Priorities and Broad Interests are Global. Different applicable effects combine; duplicate effects do not stack for the same acting party, and duplicate Global effects apply only once. Within each vote row, parties are printed from largest absolute gap between the + and − issue ranks to smallest, then by whichever ranks the + issue higher. Use the printed For row to break recipient ties; no calculation is needed during play. Each law triggers at most once per card action (or per Fiddler favour Operation), only from the original Operation, never another extra. Movement permissions combine; Carpooling can move two together while Political Exchange swaps up to two rivals back to the source. Bridge Campaign triggers when Organise crosses a region boundary, including Centre. It adds Support at the original destination; Cross-Region Campaigns adds at the original source. Each triggers once when awarded to the acting party, including on long-distance moves. Moves within one region trigger neither.

Decided

3. Year-end and New Year

  1. Close all remaining open parties in Firm-number order. Award each pile and one available party card to its marker’s owner, return the marker, and resolve all resulting effects. A player with two openings chooses their Closure order.
  2. After Years 2, 4 and 6, hold the Election below, including Election prophecies. Elections precede New Year.
  3. Return Collection counters and opening markers; assign Early Bird to the player whose turn ended the year. Complete preparation for the next year.
  4. Release all New Year cards into their owners’ hands. Agents stay in their districts throughout year changes and Elections.
  5. Begin the next year with party resurgence, then Party Openings, then the Fiddler’s burst, then regular Lobby turns. After Year 6, release cards for final hand ranking without starting another playable year.
Decided

4. Election

  1. After Closures and before New Year, randomly retain up to half capacity in each non-Centre district: 3, 2 or 1 Support in capacity-6, -4 or -2 districts. Retain all when fewer are present. Centre retains all its Support.
  2. Each surviving Support votes on its region’s pending policy using its party’s printed position. More For than Against passes; ties, including 0–0, fail.
  3. Count all four votes and determine Party recipients before activating new laws. Award each passed Party policy to its largest surviving For contributor in that region, breaking ties by the leftmost tied party in the For row. Keep it beside that party’s board; keep passed Global policies in the shared display. Discard failed policies without awarding an effect. Opposite policies can both pass.
  4. After Elections 1 and 2 reveal a new policy for each region. No scoring or scoring-card reveal occurs yet. Continue year-end preparation and release New Year cards before beginning the next year.
  5. After Election 3, reveal every player’s scoring card. For each enacted policy, Party or Global and regardless of its recipient, gain its + issue value and lose its − issue value. Party effects awarded at Election 3 have no future actions to modify. Apply all Global scoring laws, including newly enacted laws: Muted Priorities changes printed 6 to 4; Broad Interests changes printed 1 to 3. Both gains and losses change, without reordering priorities or changing votes.
  6. Add final hand rank: after releasing New Year cards following Election 3, count other human players holding fewer than or the same number of common and party cards. Firm cards and Hecate reward cards never count. Score one point for each. Home Party cards, policies and scoring cards do not count. At two or three players count each human’s combined hand once.

Final totals may be negative. Highest total wins; tied players share victory. There is no Capital score or regional agenda score. Deal no policies after the final Election. At most eight laws are active during play, and twelve policies can score at game end.
