Playtest guide

One question, the smallest prototype, and evidence before repair.

Index/Playtest guide

The first Bellweather session is not a game night. It is a short experiment to find out whether district vacancies, first refusal on party bonuses, and Revolving Door exposure make an influence bid interesting.

Before the first test

  1. Choose one structural question from the open-question queue.
  2. Write a falsifiable expectation: “If this works, players will…”
  3. Build only the pieces needed to repeat the decision five times.
  4. Write one page of rules another person can follow.
  5. Copy the session template to a dated HTML file.
Suggested next test

District Support loop

Use the sixteen named districts, an unlimited supply of borrowed cubes for district and Court Support, all six party sheets, and the Pecking Order board and party tokens. At four to six players, give each human 10 Leverage, two Organise, four Rally, two Smear, two Court, and one firm kit. At two or three, double those resources and give each human two firm kits. Start each player at 5 public points, doubled to 10 at two or three players. Repeat Organise, Rally, Smear, and Court until players deliberately create and claim vacancies; include movement through a Crownwater causeway, at least one supportless-party recovery, a tied Court lead, one contest claiming both bonuses, one multi-bid Pecking Order auction, and one Night Parliament contest with a delayed operation.

Use the committed setup. Put one Support from each party in Harbormouth, Grand Market, and Ironwood, leave every Court space empty and every Coalition Target unpointed, and randomize the starting Pecking Order. Added Support is always available; only printed district capacity limits map placement.

First complete-game test

Election Day clock

When the shorter Support loop is usable, play twelve total Campaign Rounds in three sets of four with the complete twenty-four-card scoring deck. Hold Election Days after rounds 4, 8, and 12. Do not shorten a Campaign Round because fewer contests were funded. Deal new hidden cards after the first and second Election Days, then end after the third.

Before each district draw, record how much own-party Support and coalition-partner Support each revealed objective could score. Record every Court Support count before cleanup, the shared draw, coalition state, points contributed across each coalition, every base district score, both seat-reference adjustments, the cumulative totals, and elapsed time for the campaign period. After cleanup, record which first Court placements preserve or replace the carried Coalition Targets. This separates map control, Court competition, coalition timing, election variance, social scoring, and pacing.

Superseded test

Initial ladder sketch

The original six-space position-and-prize test is retained in design history. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced the player-position ladder before that test was run.

During a test

  • Record choices and outcomes before asking for opinions.
  • Mark pauses, rule lookups, accidental reveals, and arithmetic delays.
  • Record each atomic gift of Leverage, Bluff, or operation cards or points, confirm every gifted component was eligible, and record every promise that was later broken.
  • Ask players to explain what they believed another player wanted.
  • Let a broken result finish unless continuing has no information value.
  • Avoid proposing a fix while players are still describing the problem.

Questions after a test

  1. Which influence bid was hardest, and what made it hard?
  2. When did an available party bonus or the Pecking Order change your choice?
  3. Which free district spot mattered most, and who created it?
  4. Did a party without Support feel vulnerable, flexible, or both?
  5. Which bonus was claimed first, declined, or left unusable?
  6. Could everyone track Closing Argument and After-Hours Deal until they resolved?
  7. Did your hidden scoring card or compatible low-player pair guide your actions without making your intentions obvious?
  8. Did the shared Support draws, surviving drawn Support, coalition matches, and seat references reward or obscure what you changed on the map?
  9. Did thinning only agenda-named districts create enough room while leaving Bellweather Centre as an interesting persistent chokepoint?
  10. Did atomic gifts and socially enforced promises create useful negotiation without causing timing or enforcement disputes?
  11. Did each Election Day arrive before, after, or near the point when the campaign state felt ready to score?
  12. When was earlier access worth exposing more Leverage or operation cards?
  13. Could you predict every destination in the Revolving Door?
  14. What political outcome did you expect that the current party rules did not support?

After the table clears

  1. Separate observed behavior from player interpretation.
  2. Update the feedback summary only when a pattern appears.
  3. Make one coherent change, not a bundle of speculative fixes.
  4. Update rules, components, the decision log, and the changelog together.
  5. Archive replaced material under archive/.
