Playtest guide

One question, the smallest prototype, and evidence before repair.

Index/Playtest guide

The first Bellwether session is not a game night. It is a short experiment to find out whether district vacancies, first refusal on party bonuses, and Revolving Door exposure make an influence bid interesting.

Before the first test

  1. Choose one structural question from the open-question queue.
  2. Write a falsifiable expectation: “If this works, players will…”
  3. Build only the pieces needed to repeat the decision five times.
  4. Write one page of rules another person can follow.
  5. Copy the session template to a dated HTML file.
Suggested next test

District Support loop

Use the sixteen named districts, borrowed cubes for Support, all six party sheets, the Pecking Order board and party tokens, 20 Clout per human player, and one minimal identical operation token set per human player. Give each player one lobbying house kit at four to six players and two kits at two or three players. Repeat Organise, Rally, and Smear until players deliberately create and claim vacancies; include movement through a Crownwater causeway, at least one supportless-party recovery, one contest claiming both bonuses, one multi-bid Pecking Order auction, and one Night Parliament contest with a delayed operation.

Keep setup hypotheses separate from map rules. The named territories, shared-border topology, printed capacities, and 4/4/4/2 operation-token supply are current prototype values. Support quantities, starting positions, and starting overtures remain test values until selected and recorded.

First complete-game test

Election Day clock

When the shorter Support loop is usable, play three twelve-round campaign periods with the complete twenty-four-card scoring deck. Do not shorten a Campaign Round because fewer contests were funded. Deal new hidden cards after the first and second Election Days, then end after the third.

Before each district draw, record how many matching Support each revealed objective could score. Record the shared draw, every base district score, both seat-reference adjustments, the cumulative totals, and elapsed time for the campaign period. This separates map control, election variance, social scoring, and pacing.

Superseded test

Initial ladder sketch

The original six-space position-and-prize test is retained in design history. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced the player-position ladder before that test was run.

During a test

  • Record choices and outcomes before asking for opinions.
  • Mark pauses, rule lookups, accidental reveals, and arithmetic delays.
  • Ask players to explain what they believed another player wanted.
  • Let a broken result finish unless continuing has no information value.
  • Avoid proposing a fix while players are still describing the problem.

Questions after a test

  1. Which influence bid was hardest, and what made it hard?
  2. When did an available party bonus or the Pecking Order change your choice?
  3. Which free district spot mattered most, and who created it?
  4. Did a party without Support feel vulnerable, flexible, or both?
  5. Which bonus was claimed first, declined, or left unusable?
  6. Could everyone track Closing Argument and After-Hours Deal until they resolved?
  7. Did your hidden scoring card guide your actions without making your intentions obvious?
  8. Did the shared Support draws and seat references reward or obscure what you changed on the map?
  9. Did each Election Day arrive before, after, or near the point when the campaign state felt ready to score?
  10. When was earlier access worth exposing more Clout or operation tokens?
  11. Could you predict every destination in the Revolving Door?
  12. What political outcome did you expect that the current party rules did not support?

After the table clears

  1. Separate observed behavior from player interpretation.
  2. Update the feedback summary only when a pattern appears.
  3. Make one coherent change, not a bundle of speculative fixes.
  4. Update rules, components, the decision log, and the changelog together.
  5. Archive replaced material under archive/.
