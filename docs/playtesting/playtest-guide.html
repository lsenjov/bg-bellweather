Playtest guide

One question, the smallest prototype, and evidence before repair.

Index/Playtest guide

The first Bellwether session is not a game night. It is a short experiment to find out whether district vacancies, first refusal on party bonuses, and Revolving Door exposure make an influence bid interesting.

Before the first test

  1. Choose one structural question from the open-question queue.
  2. Write a falsifiable expectation: “If this works, players will…”
  3. Build only the pieces needed to repeat the decision five times.
  4. Write one page of rules another person can follow.
  5. Copy the session template to a dated HTML file.
Suggested next test

District Support loop

Use a small temporary map with equal district capacities, borrowed cubes for Support, Honeycomb and Old Shell party sheets, 20 Clout per lobbying house, and a minimal identical operative set. Repeat Organise, Rally, and Smear until players deliberately create and claim vacancies; include at least one supportless-party recovery and one contest offering both decided bonuses.

Do not infer rules from this sketch. The temporary map, Support quantities and starting positions, operative quantities, starting overtures, and the other eight bonuses are test values until selected and recorded.

Superseded test

Initial ladder sketch

The original six-space position-and-prize test is retained in design history. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced the player-position ladder before that test was run.

During a test

  • Record choices and outcomes before asking for opinions.
  • Mark pauses, rule lookups, accidental reveals, and arithmetic delays.
  • Ask players to explain what they believed another player wanted.
  • Let a broken result finish unless continuing has no information value.
  • Avoid proposing a fix while players are still describing the problem.

Questions after a test

  1. Which influence bid was hardest, and what made it hard?
  2. When did an available party bonus or the Pecking Order change your choice?
  3. Which free district spot mattered most, and who created it?
  4. Did a party without Support feel vulnerable, flexible, or both?
  5. When was earlier access worth exposing more Clout or operatives?
  6. Could you predict every destination in the Revolving Door?
  7. What political outcome did you expect that the current party rules did not support?

After the table clears

  1. Separate observed behavior from player interpretation.
  2. Update the feedback summary only when a pattern appears.
  3. Make one coherent change, not a bundle of speculative fixes.
  4. Update rules, components, the decision log, and the changelog together.
  5. Archive replaced material under archive/.
