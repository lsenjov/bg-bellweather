Five-player learning extension

Five persistent identities, five complete Ruleset-15 games, twenty-five postmortems, and cumulative learning after every game.

Index/Playtesting/Five-player learning extension
Completed 15 August 2026

Session and method

This five-game extension continued the twenty-game four-player learning league. Horizon, Vex, Accord, and Cache retained their final numeric policies, and all five players received the complete 326-lesson shared dossier before Game 21. Orbit joined as a scoring-floor route architect initialized from that same dossier. After every game, each postmortem contributed selected lessons to the common dossier and each player's proposed numeric adjustments updated its own next policy; the dossier held 411 lessons after Game 25.

The games ran through the authenticated game API with observers allowed, no timer, and no facilitator-imposed behavioral shortcuts. Every game continued through the third Election. Seat order rotated once per game, so each identity occupied each of the five seats exactly once. The server supplied random agendas and Election draws, and every invite below remains valid for joining the completed replay as an observer.

A strategic executor refreshed after each Operation card, enumerated every legal Operation and immediate party bonus, and applied each identity's current policy. Its shared overlay tightened the runaway threshold from a two-point to a one-point lead; the ending dossier raised the penalty for feeding a referenced rival from 1.20 to 1.25.

Most impactful learnings

  • Five-player directional references produced decisive mirrored swings. Four of five winners had less direct base than the runner-up. In Game 22 Election 2, Orbit's five base and Accord's zero gave Vex -5 and Horizon +5 despite equal four-point bases. The same pair in Game 25 gave Vex -6 and Horizon +6. Public leader denial is therefore an inadequate proxy for the two identities named on a private scoring card.
  • One player's strong base can feed a rival. Cache won Game 24 by seven despite losing direct base to Horizon 7–13: Cache's modifiers were +13 while Horizon's were -1. Horizon's final six-point base itself became the positive half of Cache's +5 modifier. Reference externalities must be scored alongside self-construction.
  • Four intervening opponents make thin support fragile. Vex made at least four late Riverworks placements or moves at Harbormouth in Game 21 and drew none there. Correct support needs late timing, redundancy above the draw/removal threshold, and a reserved response after the last plausible disruption.
  • The Centre has a fifth hidden beneficiary. Cache's final Honeycomb move in Game 21 improved its own partial set but completed Orbit's exact trio, supplying the three points in a 23–22 win. Every Centre mutation should be checked against all five trios, and duplicate parties should be treated as occupied blockers rather than extra matches.
  • Most zero-base failures were execution errors, not inevitable five-player erosion. Recurrent losses came from routing the wrong party to an objective, erasing one's own correct support, saturating low-capacity districts, and overvaluing generic Smear or bonuses. Exact party–district fit, self-protection, and weakest-objective coverage need to be hard constraints rather than scalar preferences.
  • Activity remained a poor proxy for conversion. The extension used 1,013 Operate actions for 1,854 cards, averaging 1.83; bundle lengths were 461 one-card, 263 two-card, and 289 three-card actions. Horizon led Game 24 with 92 cards and lost, then cut to 66 in Game 25 and won. Across the series, 379 Collections moved 1,497 cards.
  • Terminal recovery and premature Closure still destroyed live winning chances. Game 21 Horizon recovered ten cards with a final Closure and triggered scoring before a one-action Centre denial. Game 23 Accord recovered eleven terminal cards and lost by one; Game 25 Orbit had earlier closed a party needed for the last agenda and lost by two. Recovered cards have no value after the final Election.
  • Capital stayed rare but consequential. Fourteen of 75 player-Election results scored Capital, including two exact triples, for 18 points total. Orbit earned half of all Capital points and won its debut through an exact three-point set, but Centre focus could not rescue its two-point Game 24 collapse.

HORIZON: lost

I lost by one because ORBIT's final +10 overtook my +5. I achieved a six-point SC-13 base, but took -1 modifier and zero Capital; ORBIT combined base four, modifier +3, and a complete +3 Capital. The Centre's Old Shell, Honeycomb, and Foxglove matched ORBIT's SC-21 while giving me only Honeycomb. Next lessons: After reaching maximum base, all remaining tempo should pivot to Capital and modifier denial. With five private trios, an uncontrolled Centre has more opponent exposure.

VEX: Fifth: ORBIT 23, HORIZON 22, CACHE 21, ACCORD 15, VEX 12.

VEX lost across every component in the five-player field. VEX scored 6 base, +1 modifier, and 0 Capital against ORBIT's 10, +5, and 3; those differences exactly produce the eleven-point margin. The extra opponent window made early support fragile, while five distinct seats increased reference and Centre threats.

Next lessons: In five-player games, reinforce exact objectives in the last year because four opponents act between turns. Track each distinct relative seat and recurring reference opponent across all three cards.

ACCORD: loss

I lost 23–15: my direct bases totaled three to ORBIT’s ten, and Capital was 0–3. Five-player interleaving gave four opponents time to alter the board between my turns, punishing diffuse construction. Yet my +7 modifier exceeded ORBIT’s +5, so references were not the cause. I spent all five Year 12 cards away from SC-24’s objectives, then passed twice.

Next lessons: With four intervening opponents, late support needs exact targeting and redundancy. Model distinct second-neighbor references, but modifiers cannot replace base.

CACHE: lost: second place, 21 points; ORBIT won with 23

CACHE lost by two: one fewer base and two fewer Capital points than ORBIT, offset by a one-point modifier advantage. CACHE led 16-13, but ORBIT won the final Election 10-5. A fifth player added another rival Centre trio and disruption window.

Next lessons: Five-player Centre moves require checking four rival trios, not three. A self-improving partial Capital move can worsen relative standings.

ORBIT: won

I won 23–22 by recovering from a zero-base opening with a nine-point middle Election and a ten-point final Election. My controllable strength was SC-16's six-base completion; the finish combined four base, +3 modifier, and exact Capital. This was not a dominant direct-scoring win: HORIZON led my base 14–10, while my +2 modifier advantage and +3 Capital supplied the five-point offset.

Next lessons: A win cannot excuse a zero-base first Election. Audit every Centre move against all five Capital cards; opponent assistance is not a reliable route.

Interpretation limits

  • This was an adaptive continuation, not five independent fixed-policy trials. All players shared each game's findings before the next game.
  • Orbit had the same accumulated knowledge but a newly initialized policy; the other four continued policies shaped by twenty prior games.
  • A strategic executor applied the numeric policies to exhaustive legal API choices. The player agents shaped policies and analyzed outcomes but did not deliberate afresh on each command.
  • Private agendas stayed private during play. Opponent agendas and exact reference effects became fully visible through completed replay.
  • No table talk or negotiated promises occurred. Court coalitions were evaluated from board utility rather than human bargaining.
  • The single five-game seat rotation balances identity exposure but gives only one observation per identity-position pair. Seat summaries are not estimates of a causal position advantage.

Audit

Verified: five completed games, 60 years, 15 Elections, 75 player-Election results, 25 postmortems, every identity in every seat once, Ruleset 15 throughout, and zero rejected API commands. The scoring ledger balances: 125 initial points + 210 base + 50 modifiers + 18 Capital = 403 final points. Fifty-six years ended by majority Closure and four through consecutive Passes.
