Main questionHow does the current yearly Lobby behave at four players when Operate length is chosen from updated board value rather than fixed by profile?

Method

Each controller merged its private hand and all three fixed agendas into the public state, refreshed after every resolved card, and considered every legal Operation and available party bonus. Current-Election objectives received the greatest weight, while later agendas retained smaller planning weights. After the first and second cards of an Operate action, the controller continued only when the best updated-board choice cleared its profile's opportunity-cost threshold.

The profiles independently valued constructive Support, Smear, Court coalitions, or collecting rival piles. No bundle length, annual Operation count, Collection count, Closure time, or deliberation period was imposed by the facilitator. The game completed with zero rejected commands.

This is one deterministic agent game. The collection strategy used a deliberately low two-card threshold, opponents did not negotiate or coordinate denial, and the relative-seat system can amplify a single seating and agenda assignment. Findings identify repeat-test targets rather than settled balance conclusions.

End-of-game findings

  • Collector runaway is a credible failure mode: the low-threshold collector converted early piles into a compounding hand advantage, captured the whole deck, suppressed opponents' agency, and won by five points. Repeat with human denial and alternative Collection thresholds before treating it as inevitable.
  • Late-game activity collapsed with card access: yearly Operation counts were 24, 12, 9, 7, 8, 9, 6, 4, 6, 6, 3, and 2. The decline followed hand concentration much more closely than map saturation.
  • Pass remained relevant: the game recorded 42 Passes, and Year 8 ended by passes. This contrasts with the five-player session, where every year ended through majority Closure.
  • Dynamic bundles were mostly short: 49 of 69 Operate actions stopped after one card. Seven reached the automatic three-card limit.
  • Party access was selective: Old Shell opened in all twelve years and Foxglove in eleven, while Riverworks and Many Wings opened only four times each. Old Shell received 32 Operations; Many Wings received three.
  • Operation families skewed constructive: Organise appeared 41 times, Rally 31, Smear 13, and Court 11. The coalition specialist formed a final reciprocal Honeycomb–Foxglove coalition, but Court remained the least-used family.
  • Nine party powers resolved forty times: Common Cause, Scatter the Flock, and Midnight Leak were never claimed. Common Cause and Scatter the Flock also went unused in the five-player game.
  • Capital scoring occurred but stayed small: two of twelve player-Elections scored one Capital point; no player achieved all three named parties.
  • Relative modifiers remained volatile: individual modifiers ranged from -3 to +3. Cache's win was nevertheless supported by the highest cumulative base score, twelve, rather than modifiers alone.
