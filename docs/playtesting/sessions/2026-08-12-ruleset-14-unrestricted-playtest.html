Main questionWhat happens when four strategic players retain full control of every opening, counterbid, operation, and deliberation period through all twelve rounds?

Method

Each player knew one fixed scoring card for each Election from the start and pursued its own strategy. The facilitator did not cap bid size, counterbid count, operation bundles, legal choices, or deliberation time. A stalled controller received only a state reminder or process recovery; no reminder prescribed a move. Players continued counterbidding through rounds 9–12, including zero-Leverage operation-only bids, and every pending operation was resolved or failed only under the engine's normal legality rules. The replay contains 97 non-null counterbid submissions for 96 available slots: all four players filled both slots in all twelve rounds, with one later slot update.

This makes the full result suitable as strategic playtest evidence. It remains one four-player agent game with one agenda and seat assignment, so balance conclusions are provisional.

End-of-game findings

  • Counterbidding remained central: all players continued active counterbidding in the final period. Zero-Leverage operation-only counterbids were strategically useful for transfer position and resource recovery.
  • Operation starvation was not universal in this session: final reserves still contained mixed operation families, including three Organise for Northstar. The constrained game's Ember-specific drought is invalid as systemic evidence because facilitator restrictions curtailed its counterbid-based recovery opportunities.
  • Fixed agendas created long-horizon play: players built routes and party positions several Elections ahead, while whole-map thinning and Smear made those plans contestable.
  • Coalitions mattered: reciprocal Court targets altered scoring, and ties preserving an incumbent target made coalition restoration less automatic than raw Court counts suggested.
  • Centre has both promised roles: the Canal Ward–Centre–Old Quarter route scored at Election 2, while three persistent Many Wings Support filled the Centre at game end.
  • Large packages are real friction: ten-to-seventeen-card bundles lengthened resolution and required careful per-card state refreshes, but exposed genuine resource concentration that a cap would have hidden.
  • Party bonus availability is contest-wide: a higher bid can consume a favored bonus before later bids act. Clients and agents must not treat bonus readiness as bid-local.
  • Canonical state must be reread after each card: one player twice inferred that a bundle had completed when legal cards remained. The API state was correct and allowed continuation.

API observations

  • The public projection can list unfavoured operation families under availableBonusOperations, even though the engine correctly rejects those bonus claims. The field should reflect actual claim legality.
  • Night Shift exposes a pending delayed operation but does not project the least-occupied legal district list, leaving every client to derive it from the map.
  • Rejected operation parameters preserved the pending decision for a corrected retry. Version conflicts were also recoverable by rereading state.

Facilitation learning confirmed

Subagents are useful only when parallel work improves orchestration. A playtest with no time limit must optimize for valid decisions, not wall-clock speed. Never instruct a player to skip counterbids, cap a legal bundle, shorten deliberation, or choose a move for pacing. If a controller stalls, restore the controller, provide a neutral state reminder, wait, or mark the session incomplete.
