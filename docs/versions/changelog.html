Unversioned draft

  • 2026-07-28: Added the first complete Election Day prototype and an exact twenty-four-card hidden scoring deck. Every card combines pairwise non-neighboring 6-, 4-, and 2-capacity districts with three different parties, plus positive and negative relative-seat references for games with four or more players. Election Day uses one shared 3/2/1 Support draw per named district, returns Support unchanged, awards base points for matching parties, then applies seat references using base district scores only. Scores may be negative. Test Election Day after every twelve complete Campaign Rounds, deal fresh cards between scoring periods, and end after the third Election Day with highest cumulative score winning. Archived the replaced unresolved Election Day rule; timing values, card format, coalition consequences, and tie-breakers remain open.
  • 2026-07-28: Added six printable party tracking boards on three A4 landscape sheets, with two long boards per sheet. Each board combines one opening-bid area, both full-text party bonuses with coverable Ready circles, an overture tracker, and coalition guidance; Old Shell tracks its Shell marker and Night Parliament points delayed markers to the claiming bids. Support reserves remain separate.
  • 2026-07-28: Replaced the node-and-line district diagram with sixteen named territories sharing geographic borders around Crownwater. Northreach, Reedwater, and Westgate connect through Crown Road, Canal Ward, and Old Quarter to Bellwether Centre; the other outer districts are Cloverfield, Harbormouth, Millbank, Sunmeadow, Grand Market, Red Orchard, Mossfield, Ironwood, and High Pastures. Shared border segments now define adjacency. Archived the node-map presentation while retaining its topology and capacities.
  • 2026-07-28: Added the first printable district map: sixteen districts in a twelve-district outer ring and a three-armed inner cross, with eighteen connections and fifty-seven Support spots. Three evenly spaced grey ring districts hold six Support, their ring neighbors hold four, the center holds three, and all remaining districts hold two. Support supply, starting occupation, and district names remain open.
  • 2026-07-28: Completed all twelve party bonuses. Foxglove and Riverworks exchanged Organise and Rally while preserving every unique operation pair. Foxglove gained global relocation and Smear conversion; Riverworks gained connected Rally growth and Smear attrition; Many Wings gained a second Organise and Support alongside a courted party; Night Parliament gained delayed Rally and Court operations that resolve after ordinary operatives and before the Revolving Door. Archived the replaced pairing rule and icon specification.
  • 2026-07-28: Added a limited-capacity district Support map and replaced Campaign, Expansion, Platform, and Coalition with concrete Organise, Rally, Smear, and Court baselines. A party without Support may re-enter any free district through Organise or Rally and may Smear anywhere. Added directional overtures that form coalitions only when reciprocal. Defined Honeycomb’s Leave a Cell Behind and Swarm bonuses plus Old Shell’s Stonewall and Binding Pact bonuses; the other eight party bonuses remain open. Archived the replaced operation-family rule and card specification.
  • 2026-07-28: Replaced party-operation compatibility restrictions with universal operation baselines. Every operation may now be used at every party. Each party retains its unique pair as favored operations and offers one bonus for each; resolving bids receive first refusal on each still-available bonus from highest rank to lowest. A bid may claim both bonuses when able, while bonuses it cannot or chooses not to use remain available to lower bids. Archived the replaced rule and component specification.
  • 2026-07-28: Set the game in the fictional Republic of Bellwether, with rival lobbying houses influencing animal political parties. Added six party targets—Honeycomb Cooperative, Old Shell Union, Foxglove League, Riverworks Party, Many Wings Coalition, and Night Parliament—covering every unique pair of Campaign, Expansion, Platform, and Coalition operations. Themed reusable currency as Clout, command cards as operatives, the Priority Order as the media Pecking Order at the Press Perch, and circular transfer as the Revolving Door. Party effects, operative quantities, constituencies, Election Day, government formation, scoring, and the end trigger remain open.
  • 2026-07-28: Added the first auction core and selected four players with 20 currency each, represented in denominations of 1 and 5, identical category-based starting command sets, a fixed starting Priority Order, and a 90-second counterbid timer for the first prototype. At two or three players, each player opens up to two funded auctions and receives four additional bids; at four or more, each opens up to one funded auction and receives two. The core uses a fully closed economy with no automatic between-round resource changes or direct reserve scoring, concealed currency and command reserves, physical bid escrow, mandatory public opening bids when affordable, a full recovery-bid allowance during partial insolvency, timed counterbids whose hidden contents and public locations remain revisable until expiry, withdrawal of held or ambiguous bids at the buzzer, unrestricted nonbinding negotiation, auction-local tie cancellation, simultaneous reveal, uncapped command bundles with flexible parameters at execution, circular bid transfers, a persistent player-contestable Priority Order area changed by adjacent swaps, and locked resolution when commands or targets become invalid. Updated the card and board component specifications and archived their superseded ladder-era prompts. Flagged opening-target collisions, last-second sniping, negotiation pressure, self-bid insurance, and large command bursts for further design work.
  • 2026-07-28: Created the Ladder Bidding design archive, rules shell, component prompts, and playtest workflow. No game mechanics committed.

Version naming

Begin with v0.1 when a complete loop can be set up, taught, played, and ended. Use short labels such as v0.2 for coherent playtest builds; reserve v1.0 for a stable ruleset.
