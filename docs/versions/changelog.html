Unversioned draft

  • 2026-08-05: Delayed equal-counterbid cancellation and refunds until the affected contest becomes current. Future contests now remain covered and escrowed, and both public and owner-private projections omit cancellation and transfer outcomes until reveal. Corrected the physical rules to reveal each contest as a group and archived the replaced global-reveal procedure.
  • 2026-08-05: Made a player’s own filing rows selectable during counterbidding. Clicking or keyboard-activating a placed counterbid now selects its exact identity card and hydrates its persisted draft for editing, while opponent rows remain inert and unsaved edits must still be applied or reset before switching.
  • 2026-08-05: Reshaped the live party rail around coalition status. Court Support now appears as party emblems with counts, a one-way Coalition Target appears as a muted right-edge emblem, and reciprocal targets receive a restrained coalition treatment while the source party remains visually dominant. Added accessible state labels and narrow-layout handling.
  • 2026-08-05: Changed operation resolution to reject illegal player-selected parameters without consuming the pending decision or card. Pending decisions now include only operation families with a legal current choice; when no remaining family is legal, every remaining card in that bid fails automatically and resolution advances, including impossible delayed Night Parliament baselines. Updated the engine, HTTP error coverage, and web form/map legality constraints as ruleset version 11. Archived the replaced illegal-selection failure rule.
  • 2026-08-05: Doubled each player’s Court supply to two cards at four to six players and four cards at two or three players. Advanced the closed economy to ruleset version 10, expanded the shared six-player print inventory from six to twelve Court cards and from 138 to 144 total bid cards, filled all nine zero-gutter sheets, and updated setup rules, player kits, playtest guidance, and print assets. Archived the replaced single-Court standard setup and six-card Court print supply.
  • 2026-08-05: Changed low-player opening bids to separate Early Bird-relative snake turns: ABBA at two players and ABCCBA at three, including consecutive turns for the turn-around player. Every turn places one opening when affordable or passes with no Leverage, with affordability checked again after any intervening gift. Four-to-six-player order remains one clockwise turn each. Updated the ruleset 9 engine and replay state, action protocol, public server projection, example agent, web filing form and visible progress ledger, physical rules, player aid, glossary, worked example, and playtest API. Archived the replaced batched low-player procedure.
  • 2026-08-05: Gave every player two compatible scoring cards at two or three players. A second card that repeats any district on the first is discarded face down and redrawn until the player has six distinct district objectives; both cards score and all Gain/Lose references remain ignored. Four-to-six-player games retain one card. All twenty-four cards are reshuffled before each campaign period. Updated the engine, replay state, private/public projections, web folio and Election map, physical rules, component specifications, printable firm boards, and playtest guide as ruleset version 8. Archived the replaced single-card rule and firm-board text.
  • 2026-08-05: Removed the fixed player-count choice from web and API lobby creation. Every lobby now admits up to six players, reports live occupancy, and lets the host start with any group of two through six; the actual occupied seats determine game setup. Updated the example agent to retain a validated local automatic-start target for multi-agent playtests without sending that target as lobby configuration.
  • 2026-08-05: Removed all prelaunch application compatibility paths. Ruleset version 7 is now the only executable engine behavior; the server rejects older game records and snapshots, and the browser requires complete current projections instead of fabricating missing operation counts or retaining unknown contests. Existing obsolete local databases are left untouched but their games must be recreated. Historical rules, component archives, and changelog entries remain available as design history.
  • 2026-08-04: Moved Night Parliament’s delayed Closing Argument and After-Hours Deal bonuses from the end of its own contest to the end of the complete contest sequence, after every Revolving Door. This timing is ruleset version 7’s only supported behavior. Updated the printable Night Parliament and lobbying-firm boards and archived the replaced rule and board text.
  • 2026-08-04: Changed Organise map selection to arm the optional source district first by default, including when switching from another operation. After a source is chosen, the map automatically advances to the required destination; players may still arm Destination directly to skip Source.
  • 2026-08-04: Fixed resolution sequencing so the engine rebuilds the pending party-contest sequence after the Pecking Order auction completes. Adjacent swaps now determine which party contest resolves next in the same round, as required by the existing current-order rule. The temporary prelaunch version gate introduced with this fix was removed when ruleset version 7 became the only supported application behavior.
  • 2026-08-04: Removed party-bonus controls from the resolution form after an earlier bid claims that operation’s once-per-contest bonus. Any stale browser selection is cleared, while the engine continues to reject unavailable bonus claims authoritatively.
  • 2026-08-04: Moved resolution actions directly beneath the web map, replaced the operation-card dropdown with remaining-count radios such as “1 O” and “3 R,” and added armed, non-overwriting district-map selection for source, destination, operation, bonus, and repeated-operation fields. Bid contents now reveal by contest: current and completed contests are public during resolution, future contests remain covered, and all contests remain revealed afterward.
  • 2026-08-04: Added explicit filing progress during app resolution. The current filing is outlined, completed filings receive a grey wash and “Resolved” stamp even when they had no operations, waiting filings remain unchanged, and cancelled filings retain their separate crossed-out treatment.
  • 2026-08-04: Sorted the app’s live contest filings, counterbid target list, and replay contests by the current Pecking Order. Pecking Order remains first, and party contests now move immediately with resolved adjacent swaps.
  • 2026-08-04: Added each party’s established colour to its web-app contest frame while keeping firm colours scoped to the filings inside it. The Pecking Order contest remains neutral.
  • 2026-08-04: Assigned one firm to each player in the web application at every player count while retaining doubled reserves, two opening bids, and four counterbid cards at two or three players. The physical board game continues to give those players two firms’ components. Added each digital firm’s colour and emblem to the public points tracker, which now reports every player as Ready or Waiting during counterbid and Election readiness.
  • 2026-08-04: Made each placed bid’s total transferable-card count public. The count includes Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards and excludes the firm identity card; covered family quantities and composition remain hidden until reveal. The web table now displays every positive total instead of “Contents concealed” and omits count text for an empty stack. Archived the replaced fully concealed stack-size rule.
  • 2026-08-04: Unified party emblems across the web application and physical prototype. The app now uses the printed Old Shell, Foxglove, Riverworks, Many Wings, and Night Parliament artwork; printed Honeycomb components now use the app’s single outlined cell instead of the former five-cell cluster. Updated party boards, Pecking Order pieces, and scoring-card watermarks, and archived the replaced Honeycomb cluster.
  • 2026-07-31: Removed the shared backs from transferable bid cards and closed the 2 mm gaps between adjacent cards. The unchanged 138-card supply now prints as nine front-only A4 sheets in zero-gutter 4 × 4 grids instead of eighteen duplex pages. Archived the replaced backs and guttered layout.
  • 2026-07-31: Restored Court as the operation/card keyword and reserved Coalition for the reciprocal relationship. Court now places temporary acting-party Support on prospective partners’ Court spaces; a unique leader moves the persistent Coalition Target, while a tied lead and Election Day cleanup leave it in place. Court Support clears after every Election Day. Binding Pact now places a second Old Shell Support on the selected Court space, and Local Chapters uses Many Wings’ current target after Court resolves. Migrated the engine, protocol, server, web table, example agent, rules, and components to breaking ruleset version 5 with no legacy aliases. Archived the direct-redirection rule and replaced track, card, and Shell-marker specification.
  • 2026-07-31: Removed the large “Classified” label from the shared transferable bid-card back while retaining its Bellweather register, Influence Filing seal, navy security pattern, borders, and Revolving Door registry footer. Archived the replaced labelled back.
  • 2026-07-31: Renamed Bellwether to Bellweather, Clout to Leverage, Court to Coalition, and directional Overtures to Coalition Targets across the live rules, components, print files, web application, API, engine state, package scopes, and internal identifiers. The clean ruleset-version-4 migration intentionally provides no alias or saved-game compatibility because no web-playtest saves exist. Redesigned all transferable bid-card fronts with unique L/B/O/R/S/C upper-left and rotated lower-right indices while preserving their full names, central glyphs, effects, colour families, card backs, and quantities. Archived the replaced terminology and unindexed card-face specification.
  • 2026-07-31: Removed the campaign and score trackers from the A3 district map and restored the district diagram from 76% to 88% scale without changing its topology, labels, or fifty-seven Support spots. Added a separate A4 landscape sheet with 27 mm spaces: a 3 × 5 campaign grid and a 7 × 6 score snake spanning 0–40. Archived the replaced map-integrated trackers.
  • 2026-07-31: Changed Election Day from a non-mutating sample to a partial map reset. Each agenda-named district now keeps its shared random 3/2/1 Support draw and returns all undrawn Support there to party supplies; districts absent from every revealed agenda remain unchanged. Bellwether Centre remains a persistent chokepoint because the scoring deck never names it. Archived the replaced non-mutating draw rule.
  • 2026-07-31: Increased the standard player supply from two Rally cards to four, doubled to eight at two or three players, while leaving Organise, Smear, and Court quantities unchanged. Expanded the six-player printable supply to twenty-four Rally cards and 138 transferable bid cards across eighteen duplex pages. Archived the replaced equal Rally and Smear supply.
  • 2026-07-31: Moved the campaign tracker into a right-side 3 × 5 grid, with four Campaign Rounds and an Election Day in each column. Expanded the 0–20 score track into a two-row snake whose approximately 26 mm spaces fit the existing 25 mm firm score markers, and scaled the district diagram around the trackers without changing its Support spots or connections. Archived the replaced compact map trackers.

Removed the functional labels, enlarged the firm emblems for distance readability, and retained the separate 25 mm score markers. Archived the replaced firm bid identifiers.

  • 2026-07-30: Applied physical playtest feedback across the prototype. Replaced Clout pieces, operation tokens, and opaque counterbid covers with a shared-back 40 × 61 mm card economy: each standard player receives ten one-Clout, four inert Bluff, two Organise, two Rally, two Smear, and one Court card, doubled at two or three players. Opening Clout stays face up while opening operation and Bluff cards remain face down; firm A/B Counterbid Cover cards top concealed stacks and do not transfer, while every reusable card beneath them Revolves. Renamed the opening-priority component the Early Bird marker. Redesigned party overtures with one token and target-coloured positions, simplified firm boards around an exact-size agenda and party-power reminders, consolidated the Pecking Order and shared markers onto one sheet, and added twelve-round/three-election and 0–20 score tracks to the capacity-label-free map. The server persists authenticated games and complete replay streams in SQLite, enforces seat-private information, schedules optional counterbid deadlines, retains revealed rounds and Election bulletins, and serves the responsive Bellwether Register browser table. Added public chat, atomic one-way gifts, Election-result readiness, an HTTP/WebSocket agent contract, and a runnable conservative example agent.
  • 2026-07-30: Shortened the game to twelve total Campaign Rounds in three sets of four, with Election Days after rounds 4, 8, and 12. Players now begin with 10 Clout, two Organise, two Rally, two Smear, one Court, and 5 public transferable points; two- and three-player games double that complete supply, including points and bid pieces. Support is unlimited, with every party starting once in Harbormouth, Grand Market, and Ironwood; all overtures begin unpointed and the Pecking Order begins randomized. Replaced exchanges and rules-enforced immediate deals with atomic one-way gifts of eligible Clout, operation tokens, and points; all agreements are socially enforced. Made the counterbid timer optional and configurable, with unanimous readiness ending the phase early or acting as the sole trigger when the timer is off. Tied highest final point balances share the win. Reduced the maximum required operation-token supply to forty-two. Archived the replaced campaign setup and trading rules and eighty-four-token component specification.

Repurposed the shared opening-priority marker to track the role. Archived the replaced simultaneous-opening procedure and optional-marker specification.

Version naming

Begin with v0.1 when a complete loop can be set up, taught, played, and ended. Use short labels such as v0.2 for coherent playtest builds; reserve v1.0 for a stable ruleset.
