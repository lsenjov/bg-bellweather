Player aids

References that preserve decision time and remove memory tests.

Index/Player aids

Do not make an aid until the same question interrupts multiple turns. The interruption is evidence; the first aid can be an index card.

Prototype component

Party tracking boards

The six printable party boards combine contest placement and public party state. Print two long boards per A4 landscape sheet.

  • One opening-bid area anchors each party’s influence contest.
  • Both favored-operation bonuses appear in full with small Ready circles that can be covered after use.
  • Five target-coloured Court spaces hold acting-party Support; one source-party token marks the persistent Coalition Target and moves when a unique leader emerges.
  • Return Court Support after Election Day without moving the Coalition Target token; reciprocal targets show a coalition.
  • Night Parliament reminds players to mark the bid that claimed Night Shift.
  • Support supply is unlimited, so track only Support on the map and in the five Court spaces rather than a separate reserve.
Prototype component

Lobbying firm player boards

The six lobbying firm kits each include one printable player board. The board identifies the firm without relying on color, holds three ordered exact-size face-down agenda positions with room for stacked low-player pairs, distinguishes the concealed player reserve from public commitments, summarizes all four operation baselines, and gives terse reminders for both powers of every party.

At two or three players, one human takes two boards and combines their bid pieces. The player still has one reserve, one three-slot agenda allocation of three registered compatible pairs, and one score.

Shared reference component

Pecking Order board

The one-sheet printable Pecking Order keeps the persistent party sequence visible. Its cut-away lower panel supplies six movable party tokens, the Early Bird marker, and the round marker.

  • The auction is mandatory every Campaign Round and has no opener or opening bid.
  • Equal-Leverage counterbids cancel normally.
  • Operatives execute from the lowest active bid to the highest, so the highest-Leverage bid acts last.
  • The human player who placed the highest active bid becomes the first opener next round; the Revolving Door does not transfer the role. If no active bid remains, keep the existing first opener.
  • From the Early Bird, use ABBA opening order at two players, ABCCBA at three, or one clockwise turn each at four to six. Each turn places one funded opening or passes with no Leverage.
  • Every operation card swaps one adjacent pair of party tokens; Bluff cards do nothing.
  • The Revolving Door retains the normal high-to-low bid ranking.

Remaining reference needs

The party boards answer the recurring bonus, Court Support, Coalition Target, coalition, and Night Shift marker questions. The lobbying firm boards cover the broad contest sequence and bid ownership. The remaining likely needs are:

  • District adjacency and free Support spots.
  • Detailed Organise, Rally, Smear, and Court legality, including district recovery, tied Court Support, and Election Day cleanup.
  • Election Day district draws, retained drawn Support, undrawn Support returning to supply, reciprocal coalition matching, cumulative points, and shared wins at tied highest totals.
  • Atomic one-way gifts of eligible Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards and points; ineligible committed bid components; and socially enforced deals.
Superseded prompts

Ladder-era reference needs

  • Contest sequence and reveal timing.
  • What winning, tying, passing, and losing did.
  • How position changed a bid or result.
  • When bid resources were spent, returned, or refreshed.
  • End trigger, scoring, and tie-break order.

Quality bar

A player aid should answer a timing or consequence question faster than the rulebook. It should not contain strategy advice, hidden clarifications, or rules absent from the current rules document.
