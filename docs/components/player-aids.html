Player aids

References that preserve decision time and remove memory tests.

Index/Player aids

Do not make an aid until the same question interrupts multiple turns. The interruption is evidence; the first aid can be an index card.

Prototype component

Party tracking boards

The six printable party boards combine contest placement and public party state. Print two long boards per A4 landscape sheet.

  • One opening-bid area anchors each party’s influence contest.
  • Both favored-operation bonuses appear in full with small Ready circles that can be covered after use.
  • The overture track lists every other party and a setup-only unpointed state; reciprocal markers show a coalition.
  • Old Shell has a Shell marker position for a reinforced overture.
  • Night Parliament reminds players to mark the bids that claimed delayed bonuses.
  • Support reserves remain separate rather than consuming board space.
Prototype component

Lobbying house player boards

The six lobbying house kits each include one player board. The board identifies the house without relying on color, parks its opening marker and two counterbid pieces between rounds, holds one face-down agenda, distinguishes the concealed player reserve from public commitments, and summarizes the Campaign Round.

At two or three players, one human takes two boards and combines their bid pieces. The player still has one reserve, agenda, and score.

Open

Remaining reference needs

The party boards answer the recurring bonus, overture, coalition, Shell, and delayed-marker questions. The lobbying house boards will cover the broad contest sequence and bid ownership. The remaining likely needs are:

  • District adjacency and free Support spots.
  • Detailed Organise, Rally, Smear, and Court legality, including the zero-Support alternatives.
  • Election Day district draws, cumulative score, and the unresolved tie-break order.
Superseded prompts

Ladder-era reference needs

  • Contest sequence and reveal timing.
  • What winning, tying, passing, and losing did.
  • How position changed a bid or result.
  • When bid resources were spent, returned, or refreshed.
  • End trigger, scoring, and tie-break order.

Quality bar

A player aid should answer a timing or consequence question faster than the rulebook. It should not contain strategy advice, hidden clarifications, or rules absent from the current rules document.
