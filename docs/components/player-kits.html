Lobbying firm kits

Player boards and bid ownership for Bellweather’s influence brokers.

Index/Lobbying firm kits

Each lobbying firm has a visually distinct identity kit. The first prototype supports two to six players. At four to six players, each human takes one kit and one scoring card. At two or three players, each human takes two kits and combines their bid components while retaining one player economy, bid-card reserve, compatible two-card agenda, and score.

Required kit

Per lobbying firm

Component Quantity Prototype specification Purpose
Player board 1 200 × 140 mm, two per A4 portrait sheet Identify the firm, hold one face-down scoring card or a stacked low-player pair, summarize operation baselines, and remind players of party powers.
Firm identity card 3 40 × 61 mm, interchangeable, with a large firm emblem Identify one opening bid and cover up to two face-down counterbid stacks. Identity cards stay with their firm.
Score marker 1 25 mm circular, with the firm emblem and number Record one human player’s public point balance on the shared track. At two or three players, choose one of that player’s two firm markers and leave the other unused.

Manufacture: Sleeve every 40 × 61 mm card in an opaque 41 × 63 mm sleeve. Transferable bid cards use front-only artwork, while each firm’s three identical face-up identity cards carry its number, enlarged emblem, name, and pattern.

Player supply

Per human player

  • One concealed reserve shared by every lobbying firm they control.
  • At four to six players: ten one-Leverage cards, four Bluff cards, two Organise, four Rally, two Smear, two Court, and 5 public points.
  • At two or three players: twenty one-Leverage cards, eight Bluff cards, four Organise, eight Rally, four Smear, four Court, and 10 public points.
  • At four to six players, one hidden scoring card. At two or three players, two hidden scoring cards whose six districts are distinct within that player’s pair.
  • One cumulative point balance and one active score marker. At two or three players, leave the second firm’s score marker unused.
  • One privacy screen or equivalent concealment for every uncommitted bid card.

Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards remain neutral rather than firm-colored because the Revolving Door and player gifts transfer them between players. Points are public and may also be given.

Questions before manufacture

  • Do four Bluff cards create enough uncertainty about operation-card counts without making stacks awkward?
  • Does stacking a compatible low-player pair in one exact-size face-down scoring-card space preserve enough privacy, or should every agenda remain entirely behind the player screen?
  • Do two firm identities per low-player seat clarify the larger bid allowance, or imply separate economies and scores?
