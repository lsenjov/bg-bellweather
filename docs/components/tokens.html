Representation rules

  • Saturated prototype colors identify persistent party state: Honeycomb yellow, Old Shell green, Foxglove pink, Riverworks blue, Many Wings orange, and Night Parliament black.
  • Lobbying-firm boards and identity pieces use low-saturation stationery tints with charcoal numbers, emblems, labels, and patterns, so they cannot be mistaken for party state even when a firm reuses a party hue.
  • Every firm identity repeats its number, emblem, and pattern so bid ownership never depends on color.
  • District Support, free spots, overtures, coalitions, the Shell marker, delayed Night Parliament bonuses, and the Pecking Order remain public.
  • The Campaign Round, Election Day number, and point balances remain public.
  • Uncommitted Clout, Bluff, and operation cards remain physically concealed.
  • Opening Clout values and counterbid locations remain readable from across the table; opening operation and Bluff cards remain face down.
  • Transferable bid cards share one back. Firm-specific Counterbid Cover cards sit face up on counterbid stacks and obscure the top card while identifying ownership.
  • Clout, Bluff, and operation cards remain neutral because they transfer through player gifts and the Revolving Door; firm identity belongs on Cover cards and opening-bid markers.
  • Temporary markers leave the play area during cleanup so stale state cannot survive.

Superseded prototype prompt

The initial documentation shell proposed 10 undenominated coins or cubes per player before a bidding loop had been chosen. A later influence prototype used 20 Clout at every player count. The current setup uses 10 Clout at four to six players and doubles it to 20 at two or three.

The initial ladder sketch also specified one player marker per player to show relative ladder position. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced that marker role before a prototype was built.

Questions before manufacture

  • What physical proxy best represents an unlimited Support supply without making the map difficult to read?
  • Can one pointer show an overture and make mutual coalitions immediately legible?
  • Do four Bluff cards per standard player create useful uncertainty without making bids cumbersome?
  • Which state is hard to remember without a marker?
