Representation rules

  • Saturated prototype colors identify persistent party state: Honeycomb yellow, Old Shell green, Foxglove pink, Riverworks blue, Many Wings orange, and Night Parliament black.
  • Lobbying-firm boards and identity pieces use low-saturation stationery tints with charcoal numbers, emblems, labels, and patterns, so they cannot be mistaken for party state even when a firm reuses a party hue.
  • Every firm identity repeats its number, emblem, and pattern so bid ownership never depends on color.
  • Party Support reserves, district Support, free spots, overtures, coalitions, the Shell marker, delayed Night Parliament bonuses, and the Pecking Order remain public.
  • The Campaign Round, Election Day number, and cumulative scores remain public.
  • Clout reserves and uncommitted operation-token reserves remain physically concealed.
  • Opening Clout values, opening operation-token counts, and counterbid locations remain readable from across the table.
  • Counterbid covers must conceal bid value and operation-token information from sight, bulk, and ordinary handling as far as practical.
  • Clout and operation tokens remain neutral because they transfer through behind-screen player trades and the Revolving Door; firm identity belongs on bid markers, not reusable resources.
  • Temporary markers leave the play area during cleanup so stale state cannot survive.

Superseded prototype prompt

The initial documentation shell proposed 10 undenominated coins or cubes per player before a bidding loop had been chosen. The first influence prototype replaces that prompt with 20 Clout per human player in denominations of 1 and 5.

The initial ladder sketch also specified one player marker per player to show relative ladder position. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced that marker role before a prototype was built.

Questions before manufacture

  • How many Support pieces does each party need for the selected district capacities?
  • Can one pointer show an overture and make mutual coalitions immediately legible?
  • What mix of 1- and 5-value pieces permits flexible splitting without frequent change-making?
  • Do the 70 mm covers conceal Clout and the 25 mm operation tokens, including differences in weight, sound, and bulk?
  • Which state is hard to remember without a marker?
