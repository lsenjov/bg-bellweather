Representation rules

  • Shape and color should distinguish both lobbying-house components and party Support without implying that a player owns a party.
  • Every house identity repeats its number, emblem, and pattern so bid ownership never depends on color alone.
  • Party Support reserves, district Support, free spots, overtures, coalitions, the Shell marker, and delayed Night Parliament bonuses remain public.
  • The Campaign Round, Election Day number, and cumulative scores remain public.
  • Clout reserves and uncommitted operative reserves remain physically concealed.
  • Opening Clout values, opening operative counts, and counterbid locations remain readable from across the table.
  • Counterbid covers must conceal bid value and operative information from sight, bulk, and ordinary handling as far as practical.
  • Clout and operatives remain neutral because they transfer between players; house identity belongs on bid markers, not reusable resources.
  • Temporary markers leave the play area during cleanup so stale state cannot survive.

Superseded prototype prompt

The initial documentation shell proposed 10 undenominated coins or cubes per player before a bidding loop had been chosen. The first influence prototype replaces that prompt with 20 Clout per human player in denominations of 1 and 5.

The initial ladder sketch also specified one player marker per player to show relative ladder position. Party targets and the Pecking Order replaced that marker role before a prototype was built.

Questions before manufacture

  • How many Support pieces does each party need for the selected district capacities?
  • Can one pointer show an overture and make mutual coalitions immediately legible?
  • What mix of 1- and 5-value pieces permits flexible splitting without frequent change-making?
  • Do the 70 mm covers conceal the selected Clout and operative formats, including differences in weight, sound, and bulk?
  • Which state is hard to remember without a marker?
