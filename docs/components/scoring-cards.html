Scoring cards

Twenty-four hidden agendas for Election Day.

Index/Scoring cards

Each human player holds one hidden scoring card during a twelve-round campaign period. Every card names three district–party objectives plus one positive and one negative relative-seat reference.

Decided

Card anatomy

  1. The first line pairs one 6-capacity district with one party.
  2. The second line pairs one 4-capacity district with a different party.
  3. The third line pairs one 2-capacity district with a third party.
  4. The three districts are pairwise non-neighboring.
  5. In games with four or more players, gain the positive reference’s base district score and lose the negative reference’s base district score.

Relative-seat references are Left, Right, Second Left, and Second Right. The two references on one card always indicate different players in a four-player game, so Second Left and Second Right never share a card. At two or three players, ignore both references.

When an objective’s party is in a coalition on Election Day, drawn Support belonging to either coalition partner scores for that objective. Coalition state changes how the printed party is matched; it does not change the card inventory or draw size.

Decided

Deck distribution

  • Each 6-capacity district appears eight times.
  • Each 4-capacity and 2-capacity district appears four times.
  • Each party appears four times in each line position and twelve times across the deck.
  • Each relative-seat direction appears six times as a positive reference and six times as a negative reference.
  • All twenty-four district triples are different.

Prototype component

Physical format

Each scoring card is 40 × 61 mm so it fits inside a 41 × 63 mm sleeve. Two A4 portrait sheets hold twelve card fronts each. Compact party names, right-edge party-emblem watermarks, color, capacity numbers, and explicit Gain and Lose labels keep the dense card readable without relying on color alone. Card backs remain open.
