Scoring cards

Twenty-four hidden agendas for Election Day.

Index/Scoring cards

Each human player receives three fixed hidden Election slots at setup: one card per slot at four to six players, or one registered pair per slot at two or three players. Every card names three district–party objectives, three parties for possible Capital scoring, and one positive and one negative relative-seat reference. Ruleset 19 retains the city-spoke assignments introduced in Ruleset 17.

Decided

Card anatomy

  1. The first line pairs one 6-capacity district with one party.
  2. The second line pairs one 4-capacity district with a different party.
  3. The third line pairs one 2-capacity district with a third party.
  4. The three districts are pairwise non-neighboring.
  5. In games with four or more players, gain the positive reference’s base district score and lose the negative reference’s base district score.
  6. Every physical card front prints its card ID and R19 so it cannot be silently mixed with a replaced deck.

Relative-seat references are Left, Right, Second Left, and Second Right. The two references on one card always indicate different players in a four-player game, so Second Left and Second Right never share a card. At two or three players, ignore both references.

At four to six players, shuffle all twenty-four individual cards once, then deal three ordered Election slots with one card per slot. That card supplies both the player’s ordinary objectives and Capital parties for its Election.

At two or three players, first form the twelve registered compatible pairs identified by adjacent odd–even card numbers: SC-01 + SC-02 through SC-23 + SC-24. Keep the odd-numbered card first or on the left. Turn every pair face down as one packet, shuffle the twelve packets once as the full deck, and deal one packet per slot. Both cards score their ordinary objectives; only that pair’s first card supplies the player’s Capital parties. The position is fixed when the pair is formed and cannot be chosen or changed later.

Every registered pair has six distinct district objectives, so the six or nine required pairs always complete without exposing passed-over cards or reshuffling. Compatibility is checked within each pair, not across the player’s six-card game allocation. Players may inspect their complete allocation but reveal only the assigned slot at each Election.

When an objective’s party is in a coalition on Election Day, drawn Support belonging to either coalition partner scores for that objective. Coalition state changes how the printed party is matched; it does not change the card inventory or draw size.

Decided

Capital scoring

After ordinary district scoring and the relative-seat modifier, inspect Bellweather Centre without drawing or removing Support. Count how many of the three different parties printed on the player’s Capital card are present there: score 0 points for zero or one, 1 point for two, or 3 points for all three.

Multiple Support from the same named party count only once. A coalition partner does not stand in for a named party. Bellweather Centre never participates in ordinary Election draws, thinning, or district-objective scoring.

Decided

Deck distribution

  • Each 6-capacity district appears eight times.
  • Each 4-capacity and 2-capacity district appears four times.
  • Each party appears four times in each line position and twelve times across the deck.
  • Each relative-seat direction appears six times as a positive reference and six times as a negative reference.
  • All twenty-four district triples are different.

Current component

Physical format

Each scoring card is 40 × 61 mm so it fits inside a 41 × 63 mm sleeve. Two A4 portrait sheets hold twelve card fronts each. Compact party names, right-edge party-emblem watermarks, color, capacity numbers, and explicit Gain and Lose labels keep the dense card readable without relying on color alone. Card backs remain open. In low-player pairs, preserve the registered order physically by keeping the odd-numbered card first or leftmost.
