Decided

Components

The current mechanic requires reusable currency, reusable command pieces, hidden bid containers, distinct board targets, and a Priority Order area listing those targets. Candidate prototype materials are tracked in the component documents:

  • Board or ladder track
  • Cards
  • Bid and position tokens
  • Player aids
Decided

Private information

Each player physically conceals their total available currency as well as the identities and total count of their uncommitted commands. Players may remember, infer, or track holdings from previously revealed exchanges; there is no rule that makes the information unknowable. Opening bids reveal their currency amount and command count; counterbids conceal their contents until the simultaneous reveal. Every bid must hold the actual tokens and cards committed to it; one component cannot occupy multiple bids.

The total supplies of currency and command cards are fixed after setup. Rules and effects may transfer them but never create, destroy, discard, or bank them.

Decided

Setup

For the first four-player prototype, give each player 20 currency represented in whole-number denominations of 1 and 5, plus an identical set of commands. Place the targets in one fixed starting Priority Order for every initial test. Command types and quantities, target state, exact token mix, and all other setup details remain open.

Decided

Round structure

1. Open auctions

In a two- or three-player game, each player opens two auctions. In a game with four or more players, each player opens one auction. For each auction, its opener chooses a visible board target. Each target may host no more than one auction. The procedure for simultaneous attempts to open the same target is an open question.

The opening player places a bid containing at least one currency and any number of optional commands. A player may commit as many commands as they own; there is no per-bid cap. The bid’s currency amount and number of commands are public, but the commands remain hidden. Opening bids lock before counterbid placement begins and cannot be revised.

A player must open as many of their allotted auctions as they can fund at the one-currency minimum. If they cannot fund the full allotment, they open fewer or none. They still retain their full counterbid allowance and may use those opportunities as empty bids.

2. Place counterbids

In a two- or three-player game, each player may place up to four counterbids. In a game with four or more players, each player may place up to two. Counterbids may target any auction, including their owner’s, and any number may share one auction. A counterbid may be empty. Its target is public, while its currency and commands remain hidden.

All players place counterbids simultaneously during a timed phase. Use 90 seconds for the first four-player prototype. A player may have no more counterbid markers on the board than their player-count allowance. Until the timer expires, players may retrieve a counterbid, change its physically escrowed currency or commands behind their screen, relocate it, or withdraw it. Changes made at the last second are legal. Players may negotiate, lie about bids, coordinate, and break promises, but agreements are never binding. They may not show hidden bid contents or transfer resources outside normal resolution. When time expires, every released counterbid clearly inside one target’s bid area locks with its current contents and location. A held, moving, or ambiguously placed counterbid is withdrawn to its owner’s reserve.

3. Reveal and remove equal counterbids

Reveal the currency and commands in every counterbid simultaneously across the board before resolving any auction. Counterbids with equal currency amounts in the same auction are returned to their owners and do not execute, regardless of ownership or command contents. Zero-value counterbids follow this rule. Equal values in different auctions do not interact. Only the original opening bid is protected from matching another bid: counterbids equal to its currency amount are returned while the opening bid remains active. An additional bid placed by the opener receives no special protection.

4. Resolve auctions

If the Priority Order area has an auction, resolve it first. Each command executed there swaps one adjacent pair of targets. Changes apply immediately for the current round, and the updated list persists across rounds until another command changes it. Resolve every other auction in full, following the current target list.

Within an auction, rank active bids from most currency to least. Bids, not players, occupy ranks. Starting with the highest bid, each bid owner executes that bid’s commands in any order they choose. A command may be used on a target with its matching category; that target defines the command’s concrete effect. A committed command card or type is fixed, but its owner chooses its legal variable parameters when it executes.

Check whether each command is legal when it would execute. An illegal command fails and cannot be replaced, but remains part of its bid for transfer.

If an earlier effect removes or disables an unresolved auction’s target, that auction still resolves. Its newly illegal commands fail, then its active bids transfer normally.

5. Transfer bids

After all commands in the auction have resolved, transfer each complete bid—its currency and reusable commands—to the owner of the bid immediately below it. The lowest bid transfers to the owner of the highest bid. A player occupying multiple ranks receives once for each rank. If only one active bid remains, its owner receives it back.

6. End the round

After the final auction transfers its bids, players collect their received resources into their concealed reserves. Preserve the board and Priority Order state, then begin the next opening phase. There is no automatic income, draw, refresh, or other resource change.

Open

End of game

End trigger, scoring, and tie-breakers are unresolved. Held currency and command cards do not score directly; victory must come from target or board outcomes selected with the theme.
