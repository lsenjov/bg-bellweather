Decided

Identity

Working title: Ladder Bidding.

The title may change. The game is set in the fictional Republic of Bellweather, where One Fell Swoop Public Affairs, Pairliament Partners, TriumviRAT Advisory, IVy League Public Affairs, Vested Interests, and VI.P. Access Group compete to influence animal political parties.

At four to six players, each human controls one lobbying firm and holds one scoring card. At two or three players, each controls two firms but retains one player economy, bid-card reserve, and score while holding two scoring cards.

Decided

Pitch

Rival lobbying firms stack Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards to reorganise Support across crowded districts, rally animal parties, smear their rivals, broker coalitions, and manipulate the media Pecking Order before Election Day.

Objective

Shape party Support toward the district–party objectives on your hidden scoring cards. Score across three Election Days; after the third, every human player tied for the highest cumulative point balance shares the win.

Decided

Private information

Each human player physically conceals their scoring card or cards and every uncommitted bid card. Point balances are public. Players may remember, infer, or track concealed holdings from previously revealed exchanges; there is no rule that makes the information unknowable. The total number of transferable cards in every placed bid is public; it includes Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards but excludes the firm identity card. An opening influence bid places its one-Leverage cards face up, so its Leverage amount is also public, while its operation and Bluff cards remain face down and their family quantities and mixture are concealed. Counterbids conceal all transferable card faces beneath a firm identity card until that contest’s group reveal, but not the total number of cards in the stack. Every bid must hold the actual cards committed to it; one card cannot occupy multiple bids. A player controlling two firms shares one concealed reserve between them.

The total supplies of Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards are fixed after setup. Rules and effects may transfer them but never create, destroy, discard, or bank them.

Decided

Setup

At four to six players, give each human one lobbying firm kit. At two or three players, give each human two kits. Each kit contains one player board, three interchangeable 40 × 61 mm identity cards, and one score marker. A second kit adds identity and bid pieces but does not add a second score.

At four to six players, give each human player ten one-Leverage cards, four Bluff cards, two Organise cards, four Rally cards, two Smear cards, two Court cards, and 5 points. At two or three players, double that entire player supply to twenty one-Leverage cards, eight Bluff cards, four Organise cards, eight Rally cards, four Smear cards, four Court cards, and 10 points. Each firm’s three identity cards supply one opening-bid identifier and two counterbid-stack identifiers; a low-player-count human therefore has six interchangeable identity cards across two firms.

Shuffle all twenty-four scoring cards. At four to six players, deal one face down to each player. At two or three players, complete one player’s hand before moving clockwise to the next: deal their first card face down, then draw a second card. The player privately checks both cards. If the second card shares any district with the first, discard it face down and draw again until the pair names six distinct districts. If the draw pile empties during this deal, shuffle the face-down rejected cards into a new draw pile while keeping every held card out. Place the ring-and-cross district map, campaign and score tracker sheet, six party tracking boards, unlimited party Support, one coalition target token per party, the round marker, the Pecking Order board, and its six party tokens. Put one Support from every party in each of Harbormouth, Grand Market, and Ironwood; each of those three 6-capacity districts therefore begins full. Put every coalition target token on its board’s neutral setup position. Randomize the six party tokens to form the starting Pecking Order. Put the round marker on Campaign Round 1.

Randomly choose one human player as the first opener for the first Campaign Round and place the Early Bird marker beside them. Leave both Ready circles on every party board uncovered. Put one score marker per human player on their starting point balance; at two or three players, each human chooses which controlled firm’s score marker to use and leaves the other unused.

Decided

Gifts and deals

A player may give one other player any mixture of eligible Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards and points at any time. Bid cards are eligible only while physically behind the giver’s screen; cards committed to an opening bid or counterbid are ineligible even before that bid locks. Retrieved or withdrawn counterbid cards become eligible again after returning behind the screen. The giver cannot include more cards than are eligible or more points than their current point balance.

Each gift is one-way and atomic: either its complete stated contents move to the recipient or none do. The recipient does not accept or reject it, and a completed gift cannot be reversed. Players may negotiate exchanges, promises, and other deals, but the game does not enforce any agreement or future behavior. Players enforce deals socially and may keep or break their promises.

Decided

Parties and operations

The six party targets are the Honeycomb Cooperative, Old Shell Union, Foxglove League, Riverworks Party, Many Wings Coalition, and Night Parliament. They are fictional animal institutions distinguished by how they organize, not direct equivalents of current real-world parties.

Party Animals Favored operations
Honeycomb Cooperative Bees Organise and Rally
Old Shell Union Tortoises Smear and Court
Foxglove League Foxes Organise and Smear
Riverworks Party Beavers Rally and Smear
Many Wings Coalition Starlings Organise and Court
Night Parliament Owls Rally and Court

Operation cards belong to the Organise, Rally, Smear, or Court family. Every family has one baseline effect that may be used at every party. Each party favors two families and offers one distinct bonus for each. The six parties retain every unique pair of the four operation families, so every family is favored by three parties.

Districts and Support

The map contains connected political districts. Each district has a limited number of Support spots. A free spot contains no Support; a party is present in a district while at least one of its Support occupies a spot there. Two districts are neighboring when they share a printed black border segment. Districts that meet only at a point or sit across Crownwater are not neighboring.

The first prototype map has sixteen districts and eighteen shared borders. Clockwise from the north, the outer ring is Northreach (2), Cloverfield (4), Harbormouth (6), Millbank (4), Reedwater (2), Sunmeadow (4), Grand Market (6), Red Orchard (4), Westgate (2), Mossfield (4), Ironwood (6), and High Pastures (4). Bellweather Centre (3) connects to Northreach through Crown Road (2), to Reedwater through Canal Ward (2), and to Westgate through Old Quarter (2). The map contains fifty-seven Support spots in total.

Every party has an unlimited Support supply. Added Support is therefore always available, but district placement still requires a legal free spot. Support on a party board is Court Support, not district Support; it does not make a party present on the map and cannot be drawn or scored on Election Day. Removed district Support and cleared Court Support return to their party’s supply. All Support, free spots, shared borders, and Court spaces are public. Each party starts with one Support in each of Harbormouth, Grand Market, and Ironwood.

Operation baselines

Operation Party has Support on the map Party has no Support on the map
Organise Move one of the party’s Support from its district to a free spot in a neighboring district. The final Support may leave its source district. Add one of the party’s Support to any free spot on the map.
Rally Add one of the party’s Support to a free spot in a district where it is already present. Add one of the party’s Support to any free spot on the map.
Smear Remove one rival party’s Support from a district where the acting party is present or from a district neighboring one where it is present. Remove one rival party’s Support from any district on the map.
Court Choose another party and place one Support belonging to the acting party on that party’s Court space. The current Coalition Target remains a legal choice.

Check whether the party has any Support when each operation card executes. Earlier cards in the same bid may therefore change the later cards’ available districts.

Court Support, Coalition Targets, and coalitions

Each party board has one Court space for every other party and one directional Coalition Target token. All Court spaces begin empty and all six Coalition Targets begin unpointed. After any Court Support is placed, compare the five spaces on that acting party’s board. If one space contains strictly more Support than every other space, move the acting party’s Coalition Target to that party. If the highest count is tied, leave the Coalition Target where it is, including on the unpointed space during setup.

When two parties’ Coalition Targets point toward each other, those parties form a coalition. Form or break that coalition immediately whenever either target moves. On Election Day, each coalition partner’s drawn district Support also counts as the other partner’s Support when scoring district–party objectives. After Election Day, return every piece of Court Support to its party’s supply without moving any Coalition Target.

Slip Away ignores adjacency but not district capacity. Murmuration’s second Organise checks whether Many Wings has Support after the first resolution; a recovery placement therefore makes the second resolution a movement. Spin, Public Works, Local Chapters, and all other effects that add Support require the stated free spot; the matching Support itself is always available.

The Pecking Order ignores operation families and does not use party baselines or bonuses. Every operation card executed there swaps one adjacent pair of party tokens; choose the adjacent pair when that operation card executes.

Decided

Campaign Round structure

1. Open influence contests

Every Campaign Round automatically includes one Pecking Order auction. It has no opener, no opening bid, and does not consume any player’s opening-contest allowance.

Starting with the first opener, label the players clockwise A, B, and so on. In a two-player game, take opening turns in ABBA order. In a three-player game, use ABCCBA. The player at the turn-around therefore takes two consecutive turns. In a game with four to six players, each player instead takes one opening turn clockwise from A.

On each opening turn, place exactly one opening bid if the active player has at least one Leverage behind their screen; otherwise, pass that turn. Check the player’s current reserve again on every repeated turn, so a gift received after an earlier turn can make a later opening mandatory. Each opening bid must target a visible party that does not already host a contest, so later turns choose from the parties still available.

The opening lobbying firm places one of its identity cards beside a bid containing at least one one-Leverage card and any number of optional operation and Bluff cards. Place the Leverage cards face up and the operation and Bluff cards face down in one stack. A player may commit as many operation or Bluff cards as they own; there is no per-bid cap. The bid’s Leverage amount and total transferable-card count are public, while the family quantities and mixture of its face-down cards remain hidden. Opening bids lock before counterbid placement begins and cannot be revised.

An unfunded opening turn does not remove or reduce that player’s counterbid allowance. They still retain their full counterbid allowance and may use those opportunities as empty bids.

2. Place counterbids

In a two- or three-player game, each player may place up to four counterbids. In a game with four or more players, each player may place up to two. Counterbids may target the automatic Pecking Order auction or any open party contest, including their owner’s, and any number may share one contest. A counterbid may be empty. Its target and total transferable-card count are public, while the identities and family quantities of its transferable cards remain hidden.

All players place counterbids simultaneously. Before the game, the host either disables the counterbid timer or chooses its duration. For each counterbid, stack its physically escrowed Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards face down, then place one of the firm’s identity cards face up on top. An identity card by itself represents an empty counterbid. A player may have no more identity cards covering counterbids than their player-count allowance. Until they mark themselves ready, players may retrieve a counterbid, change its physically escrowed cards behind their screen, relocate it, or withdraw it. A ready player may become unready and resume editing unless every player has already become ready.

The phase locks as soon as every player is ready. When the timer is enabled, it also locks when time expires, whichever happens first; when disabled, only unanimous readiness locks it. Changes made immediately before the lock are legal. Players may negotiate, lie about bids, coordinate, and make gifts or promises under the deal rules, but may not show hidden bid contents. At the lock, every placed counterbid keeps its current contents and target.

3. Reveal each contest and remove equal counterbids

When a contest becomes the next one to resolve, reveal every bid in that contest as a group. Turn its opening bid’s face-down cards face up and lift each identity card covering a counterbid, revealing the transferable cards beneath it while leaving the identity card beside them so ownership remains visible. Keep every future contest covered until its own resolution begins. Bluff cards have no effect. At this reveal, return counterbids with equal Leverage amounts in that contest to their owners; they do not execute, regardless of ownership or other card contents. Zero-value counterbids follow this rule. Equal values in different contests do not interact. Only the original opening bid in a party contest is protected from matching another bid: counterbids equal to its Leverage amount are returned while the opening bid remains active. Every Pecking Order bid is an ordinary counterbid, so none receives opening-bid protection.

4. Resolve influence contests

Resolve the automatic Pecking Order auction first. Rank its active bids from most Leverage to least, but execute bids in reverse rank order, from the lowest active bid to the highest; the highest-Leverage bid therefore acts last. The human player who placed that highest active bid becomes the first opener for the next Campaign Round; move the Early Bird marker beside them. Receiving the bid through the Revolving Door does not transfer this role. If no active bid remains, the first opener and marker do not change. Each operation card swaps one adjacent pair of party tokens, with each change applying before the next card chooses its pair. Bluff cards do nothing. If no active bid remains, the order does not change. The updated order applies immediately and persists across rounds. After the Pecking Order Revolving Door, resolve every party contest in full following the current order.

Within every contest, rank active bids from most Leverage to least. Bids, not players, occupy ranks. The reverse execution order at the Pecking Order does not change this ranking or its later Revolving Door. At the start of a party contest, uncover both Ready circles on that party’s board; both operation bonuses are available. Each bonus corresponds to one of the party’s favored families and may be applied once during that contest.

Starting with the highest bid in a party contest, each bid owner executes that bid’s operation cards in any order they choose. Every operation card performs its family’s baseline effect. As a matching operation card executes, its owner may also apply the corresponding party bonus if that bonus is still available and its claim conditions are met; applying it makes the bonus unavailable for the rest of the contest, so cover that bonus’s Ready circle with a marker. Immediate bonuses require both the baseline and bonus effect to resolve. A bid containing both favored families may claim both bonuses. When a bid finishes, any bonus it could not or chose not to claim remains available to lower-ranked bids. Each bid receives its own opportunity, even when one player owns multiple ranks. The family printed on a committed card is fixed, but its owner chooses all legal variable parameters when it executes.

Closing Argument and After-Hours Deal are delayed bonuses. Claim either when its matching Night Parliament baseline resolves, then mark the claiming bid; future legality is not required when claiming. Complete Night Parliament’s contest normally, including its Revolving Door, and keep every claim marked while the remaining party contests resolve. After the final contest completes its Revolving Door, resolve the delayed bonus operations from highest claiming bid to lowest, then finish the Campaign Round. One bid that claimed both chooses their order. Check legality when each delayed baseline would resolve. If at least one legal choice exists, its owner chooses legal parameters; an illegal selection must be corrected and does not consume the claim. If no legal choice exists, the delayed baseline fails automatically.

Before each bid owner acts, identify its remaining operation families with at least one legal baseline choice against the current board, including Slip Away while that bonus remains available. The owner chooses one such family and legal parameters. An illegal selection must be corrected and does not consume a card. If none of the bid’s remaining operation cards has any legal choice, every remaining operation card in that bid fails automatically and the contest advances. Failed cards cannot be replaced and remain part of their bid for transfer.

If an earlier effect removes or disables an unresolved contest’s party, that contest still resolves. Its newly illegal operation cards fail, then its active bids transfer normally.

5. Resolve the Revolving Door

After all operation cards in the contest have resolved, transfer each complete bid—its Leverage, Bluff, and reusable operation cards—to the owner of the bid immediately below it. Firm identity cards never transfer. The lowest bid transfers to the owner of the highest bid. This normal high-to-low transfer direction also applies at the Pecking Order despite its reversed operation-card execution. A player occupying multiple ranks receives once for each rank. If only one active bid remains, its owner receives it back.

6. End the Campaign Round

After the final contest resolves its Revolving Door, players collect their received cards into their concealed reserves. Return every identity card to its firm. Preserve party state and the Pecking Order, then move the round marker to the next Campaign Round or Election Day. This completes one Campaign Round regardless of player count, the number of contests opened, or any player’s ability to fund an opening bid. There is no automatic income, draw, refresh, or other resource change.

Hold an Election Day after Campaign Rounds 4, 8, and 12. After any other round, begin the next opening phase.

Decided

Election Day

The game has twelve total Campaign Rounds in three sets of four. Hold an Election Day after rounds 4, 8, and 12. This clock is the same at every player count and does not depend on how many contests were opened or funded.

  1. Reveal agendas: Every player reveals their scoring card or cards. At two or three players, each player reveals their compatible pair; at four to six, each reveals one card. Every card has one 6-capacity, one 4-capacity, and one 2-capacity district–party objective. Its three districts do not neighbor one another and its three parties are different.
  2. Draw district results: Gather the Support currently in each district named by at least one revealed card, randomize it out of sight, and draw without replacement. Draw three Support from a 6-capacity district, two from a 4-capacity district, and one from a 2-capacity district. If the district contains fewer Support than required, draw all of them. Empty Support spots are not part of the draw.
  3. Share and retain each result: Draw only once for each named district, even when several scoring cards name it. Use that same result for every objective concerning the district. Keep the drawn Support in that district and return every undrawn Support there to its party’s supply. Districts absent from every revealed scoring card are unchanged; Bellweather Centre can never appear on a scoring card and is therefore never thinned by Election Day.
  4. Calculate base district scores: For each objective, a drawn Support scores one point if it belongs to the party paired with that district or to that party’s current coalition partner. Coalition scoring is reciprocal: while Honeycomb and Foxglove are in coalition, drawn Honeycomb Support scores for a Foxglove objective and drawn Foxglove Support scores for a Honeycomb objective. A party outside a coalition scores only its own Support. Add the results from every objective on the player’s card or compatible pair. This is the player’s base district score for this Election Day.
  5. Apply seat references: In a game with four or more players, each card names a positive and negative relative seat. Add the positive player’s base district score and subtract the negative player’s base district score. Use only base district scores; seat-reference adjustments never copy one another. At two or three players, ignore both references.
  6. Record points: Add the result to the player’s public point balance. The balance may fall below zero.
  7. Clear Court Support: Return all Support from every party board’s Court spaces to its party’s supply. Leave every Coalition Target where it is.

After the first and second Election Days, collect every scoring card, including rejected second draws and undealt cards, and shuffle all twenty-four together. Deal new hidden cards for the next four-round set using the same player-count procedure. After the third Election Day, the game ends. Every player tied for the highest cumulative point balance shares the win.
