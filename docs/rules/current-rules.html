Decided

Identity

Working title: Ladder Bidding.

The title may change. The game is set in the fictional Republic of Bellwether, where rival lobbying houses influence animal political parties.

Decided

Pitch

Rival lobbying houses commit Clout and operatives to expand animal political parties, reshape their platforms, form coalitions, and manipulate the media Pecking Order before Election Day.

Objective

Parties will compete for electoral success while lobbying houses compete to influence the resulting government. The exact constituency model, Election Day procedure, government-formation procedure, victory condition, and end trigger are unresolved.

Decided

Components

The current mechanic requires reusable Clout, reusable operative cards, hidden influence containers, six party targets, and a Press Perch listing the parties in Pecking Order. Candidate prototype materials are tracked in the component documents:

  • Parties and Pecking Order
  • Cards
  • Clout, bid, and state tokens
  • Player aids
Decided

Private information

Each lobbying house physically conceals its total available Clout as well as the identities and total count of its uncommitted operatives. Players may remember, infer, or track holdings from previously revealed exchanges; there is no rule that makes the information unknowable. Opening influence bids reveal their Clout amount and operative count; counterbids conceal their contents until the simultaneous reveal. Every bid must hold the actual tokens and cards committed to it; one component cannot occupy multiple bids.

The total supplies of Clout and operative cards are fixed after setup. Rules and effects may transfer them but never create, destroy, discard, or bank them.

Decided

Setup

For the first four-player prototype, give each lobbying house 20 Clout represented in whole-number denominations of 1 and 5, plus an identical set of operatives. Place the six parties in one fixed starting Pecking Order for every initial test. Operative quantities, party effects and state, exact token mix, and all other setup details remain open.

Decided

Parties and operations

The six party targets are the Honeycomb Cooperative, Old Shell Union, Foxglove League, Riverworks Party, Many Wings Coalition, and Night Parliament. They are fictional animal institutions distinguished by how they organize, not direct equivalents of current real-world parties.

Decided

Round structure

1. Open influence contests

In a two- or three-player game, each player opens two contests. In a game with four or more players, each player opens one contest. For each contest, its opener chooses a visible party or the Press Perch. Each target may host no more than one contest. The procedure for simultaneous attempts to open the same target is an open question.

The opening lobbying house places a bid containing at least one Clout and any number of optional operatives. A player may commit as many operatives as they own; there is no per-bid cap. The bid’s Clout amount and number of operatives are public, but the operative identities remain hidden. Opening bids lock before counterbid placement begins and cannot be revised.

A player must open as many of their allotted contests as they can fund at the one-Clout minimum. If they cannot fund the full allotment, they open fewer or none. They still retain their full counterbid allowance and may use those opportunities as empty bids.

2. Place counterbids

In a two- or three-player game, each player may place up to four counterbids. In a game with four or more players, each player may place up to two. Counterbids may target any contest, including their owner’s, and any number may share one contest. A counterbid may be empty. Its target is public, while its Clout and operatives remain hidden.

All players place counterbids simultaneously during a timed phase. Use 90 seconds for the first four-player prototype. A player may have no more counterbid markers on the board than their player-count allowance. Until the timer expires, players may retrieve a counterbid, change its physically escrowed Clout or operatives behind their screen, relocate it, or withdraw it. Changes made at the last second are legal. Players may negotiate, lie about bids, coordinate, and break promises, but agreements are never binding. They may not show hidden bid contents or transfer resources outside normal resolution. When time expires, every released counterbid clearly inside one target’s bid area locks with its current contents and location. A held, moving, or ambiguously placed counterbid is withdrawn to its owner’s reserve.

3. Reveal and remove equal counterbids

Reveal the Clout and operatives in every counterbid simultaneously across the board before resolving any contest. Counterbids with equal Clout amounts in the same contest are returned to their owners and do not execute, regardless of ownership or operative contents. Zero-value counterbids follow this rule. Equal values in different contests do not interact. Only the original opening bid is protected from matching another bid: counterbids equal to its Clout amount are returned while the opening bid remains active. An additional bid placed by the opener receives no special protection.

4. Resolve influence contests

If the Press Perch has a contest, resolve it first. Each operative executed there swaps one adjacent pair of parties in the Pecking Order. Changes apply immediately for the current round, and the updated list persists across rounds until another operative changes it. Resolve every party contest in full, following the current Pecking Order.

Within a contest, rank active bids from most Clout to least. Bids, not players, occupy ranks. Starting with the highest bid, each bid owner executes that bid’s operatives in any order they choose. An operative may be used at a party with its matching operation icon; that party defines the operative’s concrete effect. A committed operative card or family is fixed, but its owner chooses its legal variable parameters when it executes.

Check whether each operative is legal when it would execute. An illegal operative fails and cannot be replaced, but remains part of its bid for transfer.

If an earlier effect removes or disables an unresolved contest’s party, that contest still resolves. Its newly illegal operatives fail, then its active bids transfer normally.

5. Resolve the Revolving Door

After all operatives in the contest have resolved, transfer each complete bid—its Clout and reusable operatives—to the owner of the bid immediately below it. The lowest bid transfers to the owner of the highest bid. A player occupying multiple ranks receives once for each rank. If only one active bid remains, its owner receives it back.

6. End the round

After the final contest resolves its Revolving Door, players collect their received resources into their concealed reserves. Preserve party state and the Pecking Order, then begin the next opening phase. There is no automatic income, draw, refresh, or other resource change.

Open

End of game

End trigger, Election Day, government formation, scoring, and tie-breakers are unresolved. Held Clout and operative cards do not score directly; victory must come from party, constituency, platform, coalition, or other board outcomes.
