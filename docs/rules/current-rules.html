Index/Current rules

The influence and Revolving Door sequence, ring-and-cross district map, Support actions, all twelve party bonuses, and the first Election Day scoring system form the current playable core. Starting Support and several physical procedures remain unresolved.

Decided

Identity

Working title: Ladder Bidding.

The title may change. The game is set in the fictional Republic of Bellwether, where One Fell Swoop Public Affairs, Pairliament Partners, TriumviRAT Advisory, IVy League Public Affairs, Vested Interests, and VI.P. Access Group compete to influence animal political parties.

At four or more players, each human controls one lobbying house. At two or three players, each controls two houses but retains one player economy, operative reserve, scoring card, and score.

Decided

Pitch

Rival lobbying houses commit Clout and operatives to reorganise Support across crowded districts, rally animal parties, smear their rivals, broker coalitions, and manipulate the media Pecking Order before Election Day.

Objective

Shape party Support toward the three district–party objectives on each of your hidden scoring cards. Score across three Election Days; after the third, the human player with the highest cumulative score wins. Tie-breakers remain open.

Components

The current mechanic requires reusable Clout, reusable operative cards, hidden influence containers, six party targets, and a Press Perch listing the parties in Pecking Order. Candidate prototype materials are tracked in the component documents:

Decided

Private information

Each human player physically conceals their scoring card, total available Clout, and the identities and total count of their uncommitted operatives. Players may remember, infer, or track holdings from previously revealed exchanges; there is no rule that makes the information unknowable. Opening influence bids reveal their Clout amount and operative count; counterbids conceal their contents until the simultaneous reveal. Every bid must hold the actual tokens and cards committed to it; one component cannot occupy multiple bids. A player controlling two houses shares one concealed reserve between them.

The total supplies of Clout and operative cards are fixed after setup. Rules and effects may transfer them but never create, destroy, discard, or bank them.

Decided

Setup

At four or more players, give each human one lobbying house kit. At two or three players, give each human two kits. Each kit contains one player board, two circular 70 mm counterbid covers, and one opening-bid marker. A second kit adds identity and bid pieces only.

For the first prototype, give each human player 20 Clout represented in whole-number denominations of 1 and 5, plus one identical set of operatives. Shuffle the twenty-four scoring cards and deal one face down to each player. Place the ring-and-cross district map, six party tracking boards, party Support, party overture markers, a Campaign Round and Election Day tracker, and the six parties in one fixed starting Pecking Order. Leave both Ready circles on every party board uncovered. Set every player’s cumulative score to zero. Operative quantities, Support quantities and starting positions, starting overtures, exact token mix, and all other setup details remain open.

Decided

Parties and operations

The six party targets are the Honeycomb Cooperative, Old Shell Union, Foxglove League, Riverworks Party, Many Wings Coalition, and Night Parliament. They are fictional animal institutions distinguished by how they organize, not direct equivalents of current real-world parties.

Party Animals Favored operations
Honeycomb Cooperative Bees Organise and Rally
Old Shell Union Tortoises Smear and Court
Foxglove League Foxes Organise and Smear
Riverworks Party Beavers Rally and Smear
Many Wings Coalition Starlings Organise and Court
Night Parliament Owls Rally and Court

Operatives belong to the Organise, Rally, Smear, or Court family. Every family has one baseline effect that may be used at every party. Each party favors two families and offers one distinct bonus for each. The six parties retain every unique pair of the four operation families, so every family is favored by three parties.

Districts and Support

The map contains connected political districts. Each district has a limited number of Support spots. A free spot contains no Support; a party is present in a district while at least one of its Support occupies a spot there. Two districts are neighboring when they share a printed black border segment. Districts that meet only at a point or sit across Crownwater are not neighboring.

The first prototype map has sixteen districts and eighteen shared borders. Clockwise from the north, the outer ring is Northreach (2), Cloverfield (4), Harbormouth (6), Millbank (4), Reedwater (2), Sunmeadow (4), Grand Market (6), Red Orchard (4), Westgate (2), Mossfield (4), Ironwood (6), and High Pastures (4). Bellwether Centre (3) connects to Northreach through Crown Road (2), to Reedwater through Canal Ward (2), and to Westgate through Old Quarter (2). The map contains fifty-seven Support spots in total.

Added Support comes from that party’s reserve. Removed Support returns to its party’s reserve. All Support, free spots, shared borders, and reserves are public. Exact Support quantities and starting positions remain open.

Operation baselines

Operation Party has Support on the map Party has no Support on the map
Organise Move one of the party’s Support from its district to a free spot in a neighboring district. The final Support may leave its source district. Add one of the party’s Support to any free spot on the map.
Rally Add one of the party’s Support to a free spot in a district where it is already present. Add one of the party’s Support to any free spot on the map.
Smear Remove one rival party’s Support from a district where the acting party is present or from a district neighboring one where it is present. Remove one rival party’s Support from any district on the map.
Court Choose another party that is not the overture’s current target, if any, and point the overture toward it.

Check whether the party has any Support when each operative executes. Earlier operatives in the same bid may therefore change the later operatives’ available districts.

Overtures and coalitions

Each party has one directional overture that may point toward one other party. When two parties’ overtures point toward each other, those parties form a coalition. Court must change an overture’s target; redirecting either overture immediately breaks its existing coalition. Starting targets—including whether any overtures begin unpointed—and the consequences of coalition membership remain open.

Murmuration’s second Organise checks whether Many Wings has Support after the first resolution; a recovery placement therefore makes the second resolution a movement. Spin, Public Works, Local Chapters, and all other effects that add Support require both a matching Support in the party reserve and the stated free spot.

The Press Perch accepts all four families but does not use their party baselines or bonuses. Every operative executed there instead swaps one adjacent pair in the Pecking Order.

Decided

Campaign Round structure

1. Open influence contests

In a two- or three-player game, each player opens two contests. In a game with four or more players, each player opens one contest. For each contest, its opener chooses a visible party or the Press Perch. Each target may host no more than one contest. The procedure for simultaneous attempts to open the same target is an open question.

The opening lobbying house places its opening-bid marker and a bid containing at least one Clout and any number of optional operatives. A player may commit as many operatives as they own; there is no per-bid cap. The bid’s Clout amount and number of operatives are public, but the operative identities remain hidden. Opening bids lock before counterbid placement begins and cannot be revised.

A player must open as many of their allotted contests as they can fund at the one-Clout minimum. If they cannot fund the full allotment, they open fewer or none. They still retain their full counterbid allowance and may use those opportunities as empty bids.

2. Place counterbids

In a two- or three-player game, each player may place up to four counterbids. In a game with four or more players, each player may place up to two. Counterbids may target any contest, including their owner’s, and any number may share one contest. A counterbid may be empty. Its target is public, while its Clout and operatives remain hidden.

All players place counterbids simultaneously during a timed phase. Use 90 seconds for the first four-player prototype. Each counterbid places its house’s A or B reveal seal with the escrowed contents and covers them with the matching 70 mm cover. A player may have no more counterbid covers on the board than their player-count allowance. Until the timer expires, players may retrieve a covered counterbid, change its physically escrowed Clout or operatives behind their screen, relocate it, or withdraw it. Changes made at the last second are legal. Players may negotiate, lie about bids, coordinate, and break promises, but agreements are never binding. They may not show hidden bid contents or transfer resources outside normal resolution. When time expires, every released counterbid clearly inside one target’s bid area locks with its current contents and location. A held, moving, or ambiguously placed counterbid is withdrawn to its owner’s reserve.

3. Reveal all bids and remove equal counterbids

Reveal the operative identities in every opening bid and lift every counterbid cover simultaneously across the board before resolving any contest. Leave each A or B reveal seal with its Clout and operatives so ownership remains visible. Counterbids with equal Clout amounts in the same contest are returned to their owners and do not execute, regardless of ownership or operative contents. Zero-value counterbids follow this rule. Equal values in different contests do not interact. Only the original opening bid is protected from matching another bid: counterbids equal to its Clout amount are returned while the opening bid remains active. An additional bid placed by the opener receives no special protection.

4. Resolve influence contests

If the Press Perch has a contest, resolve it first. Each operative executed there swaps one adjacent pair of parties in the Pecking Order. Changes apply immediately for the current round, and the updated list persists across rounds until another operative changes it. Resolve every party contest in full, following the current Pecking Order.

Within a contest, rank active bids from most Clout to least. Bids, not players, occupy ranks. At the start of a party contest, uncover both Ready circles on that party’s board; both operation bonuses are available. Each bonus corresponds to one of the party’s favored families and may be applied once during that contest.

Starting with the highest bid, each bid owner executes that bid’s operatives in any order they choose. In a party contest, every operative performs its family’s baseline effect. As a matching operative executes, its owner may also apply the corresponding party bonus if that bonus is still available and its claim conditions are met; applying it makes the bonus unavailable for the rest of the contest, so cover that bonus’s Ready circle with a marker. Immediate bonuses require both the baseline and bonus effect to resolve. A bid containing both favored families may claim both bonuses. When a bid finishes, any bonus it could not or chose not to claim remains available to lower-ranked bids. Each bid receives its own opportunity, even when one player owns multiple ranks. A committed operative card or family is fixed, but its owner chooses its legal variable parameters when it executes.

Closing Argument and After-Hours Deal are delayed bonuses. Claim either when its matching Night Parliament baseline resolves, then mark the claiming bid; future legality is not required when claiming. After every active bid has finished its ordinary operatives, resolve the delayed bonus operations from highest claiming bid to lowest, immediately before the Revolving Door. One bid that claimed both chooses their order. Check legality and choose all parameters when each delayed baseline would resolve. An illegal delayed baseline fails.

Check whether each operative is legal when it would execute. An illegal operative fails and cannot be replaced, but remains part of its bid for transfer.

If an earlier effect removes or disables an unresolved contest’s party, that contest still resolves. Its newly illegal operatives fail, then its active bids transfer normally.

5. Resolve the Revolving Door

After all operatives in the contest have resolved, transfer each complete bid—its Clout and reusable operatives—to the owner of the bid immediately below it. The lowest bid transfers to the owner of the highest bid. A player occupying multiple ranks receives once for each rank. If only one active bid remains, its owner receives it back.

6. End the Campaign Round

After the final contest resolves its Revolving Door, players collect their received resources into their concealed reserves. Preserve party state and the Pecking Order. This completes one Campaign Round regardless of player count, the number of contests opened, or any player’s ability to fund an opening bid. There is no automatic income, draw, refresh, or other resource change.

If this is the twelfth Campaign Round since the last Election Day, hold an Election Day. Otherwise, begin the next opening phase.

Decided

Election Day

For the first prototype, hold an Election Day after every twelve Campaign Rounds. The interval is the same at every player count and does not depend on how many contests were opened or funded. Twelve rounds is a working playtest value.

  1. Reveal agendas: Every player reveals their scoring card. Each card has one 6-capacity, one 4-capacity, and one 2-capacity district–party objective. Its three districts do not neighbor one another and its three parties are different.
  2. Draw district results: Gather the Support currently in each district named by at least one revealed card, randomize it out of sight, and draw without replacement. Draw three Support from a 6-capacity district, two from a 4-capacity district, and one from a 2-capacity district. If the district contains fewer Support than required, draw all of them. Empty Support spots are not part of the draw.
  3. Share each result: Draw only once for each named district, even when several scoring cards name it. Use that same result for every objective concerning the district, then return every drawn Support to the district it came from. Election Day never changes district Support.
  4. Calculate base district scores: Each player gains one point for every drawn Support matching the party paired with that district on their card. Add the results from the card’s three objective lines. This is the player’s base district score for this Election Day.
  5. Apply seat references: In a game with four or more players, each card names a positive and negative relative seat. Add the positive player’s base district score and subtract the negative player’s base district score. Use only base district scores; seat-reference adjustments never copy one another. At two or three players, ignore both references.
  6. Record scores: Add the result to the player’s cumulative score. Scores may fall below zero.

After the first and second Election Days, discard every revealed scoring card, deal each player a new hidden card from the remaining deck, reset the Campaign Round count, and begin the next opening phase. If the deck cannot deal every player a card, shuffle the discards to form a new deck before dealing. After the third Election Day, the game ends. The player with the highest cumulative score wins.

Prototype values: Twelve Campaign Rounds between Election Days and three Election Days per game are selected for testing, not treated as settled balance. Tie-breakers remain open.
