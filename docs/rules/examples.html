Worked examples

Supportless Foxglove Spins back onto the map

State: Foxglove has no Support on the map. One bid contains two Smear cards, and Spin is available.

Resolution: The first Smear may remove rival Support from any district and claim Spin, adding Foxglove Support to the vacated spot. Foxglove is now present, so the second Smear may target only its district or a district neighboring one where it is present.

Why: Check whether a party has Support separately when each operation card executes.

Binding Pact within one contest

State: Old Shell has two Court Support on Night Parliament and one on Foxglove, so its Coalition Target points to Night Parliament. The highest bid and the next bid each contain Court.

Resolution: The highest bid Courts Foxglove and claims Binding Pact. The baseline produces a 2–2 tie, so Old Shell still targets Night Parliament. Binding Pact places a second Support on Foxglove, making Foxglove the unique 3–2 leader and moving the target there. The next bid may resolve another Court baseline, but Binding Pact is already unavailable for the contest.

Why: Recheck the unique leader after every Court Support placement, while each party bonus may be applied only once per contest.

A tied Court leaves the coalition intact

State: Old Shell and Night Parliament target each other. On Night Parliament’s board, Old Shell leads Foxglove by one Court Support.

Resolution: A Court card adds one Night Parliament Support to Foxglove’s Court space. The two spaces are now tied, so Night Parliament’s Coalition Target stays on Old Shell and the coalition remains. Another Court Support on Foxglove would give it the unique lead, move the target, and break the coalition immediately.

Why: A tied high count never moves the existing Coalition Target. A coalition exists only while both targets remain reciprocal.

Election cleanup preserves the target

State: Honeycomb’s Coalition Target points to Foxglove when Election Day finishes.

Resolution: Return all Honeycomb Court Support to the supply, but leave its Coalition Target on Foxglove. If Honeycomb’s first later Court places Support on Riverworks, Riverworks becomes the unique 1–0 leader and the target moves there.

Why: Election Day cleanup clears Court Support without recalculating targets; later placements resume the unique-leader check.

Night Parliament gets the final word

State: The highest bid at Night Parliament claims Closing Argument with Rally. A bid in a later party contest uses Smear and creates a new free spot.

Resolution: Finish Night Parliament’s Revolving Door and every later party contest. After the final Revolving Door, the highest claiming bid’s original owner resolves the delayed Rally using the current map. They may choose the newly opened spot if Night Parliament is present in that district.

Why: Closing Argument’s legality and parameters are checked when its delayed baseline resolves.

Coalition partners share an election result

State: Honeycomb and Foxglove are in a coalition when Election Day begins. A shared district draw reveals one Honeycomb Support, one Foxglove Support, and one Riverworks Support. One scoring card names Honeycomb in that district and another names Foxglove.

Resolution: The Honeycomb objective scores two points, one for the Honeycomb Support and one for the Foxglove Support. The Foxglove objective also scores two points. The Riverworks Support scores for neither objective.

Why: Each drawn Support belonging to either coalition partner matches an objective naming either party. One Support still contributes at most one point to a single objective.

A mixed gift is atomic

State: One player chooses to give another player three eligible Leverage, one Rally card, and two points.

Resolution: All six resources move as one gift. If the giver lacks any stated resource or has fewer than two points, none of the gift moves. The recipient has no acceptance step.

Why: A gift is one-way and atomic, and cannot include more points than the giver’s current balance.

Committed bid components cannot be given

State: A player has placed a covered counterbid containing two Leverage and a Rally card. Before the counterbid phase locks, another player offers a Smear card for that Rally card.

Resolution: The Rally card cannot be given while it remains in the counterbid, even though the bid is still revisable. Its owner may retrieve the counterbid and return its contents behind their screen; only then does the card become eligible for a gift.

Why: Gift eligibility depends on the component being physically behind a player’s screen, not on whether its bid has locked.

A deal is socially enforced

State: One player gives two Leverage after another player promises not to counterbid at Honeycomb later in the round.

Resolution: The completed gift stands. The recipient may still counterbid at Honeycomb, because the game neither enforces the promise nor reverses the gift.

Why: Players enforce agreements socially; the rules provide no acceptance, reversal, or contract enforcement.
