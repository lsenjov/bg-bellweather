Glossary

Stable words reduce accidental rule changes.

Index/Glossary

The working vocabulary distinguishes the auction loop, public party state, hidden scoring agendas, and Election Day results.

Working vocabulary
Term Current use Status Avoid confusing with
Base district score The points a player earns from the three district–party objectives on one scoring card before applying its positive and negative seat references. Rules term Cumulative score or a score copied through a seat reference
Bid A physically escrowed commitment of Clout and any optional operation tokens to one influence contest. Rules term A payment to the targeted party
Campaign Round One complete sequence of creating the automatic Pecking Order auction, choosing party-contest targets, placing opening bids and counterbids, revealing all bids, resolving every contest, and completing every Revolving Door. Rules term One contest, one opening bid, or Election Day
Clout Reusable political leverage that ranks bids and may transfer through the Revolving Door or, while behind a player’s screen, through gifts. Themed rules term Literal money, points, or party support
Coalition The mutual state formed while two parties’ overtures point toward each other. On Election Day, drawn Support belonging to either partner matches objectives naming either party. Themed rules term The Many Wings Coalition party or a one-way overture
Contest One automatic Pecking Order auction or one player-opened party opportunity in which lobbying firms may place bids. Rules term Round or turn
Counterbid A concealed, revisable bid placed after opening contests are locked. Rules term The public opening bid
Deal An agreement between players. The game does not enforce it; players may keep or break promises and enforce deals socially. Rules term A completed gift or Night Parliament’s After-Hours Deal bonus
District One named map area containing a limited number of spots for party Support. Two districts are neighboring only when they share a black border segment; meeting at a point or across Crownwater does not count. Rules term A party or contest target
Election Day The scoring procedure after a campaign period in which scoring cards are revealed and current district Support is sampled. Themed rules term A Campaign Round or an action that changes Support
First opener The human player who takes the first opening turn in a Campaign Round. Choose one randomly for the first round; thereafter, use the player who placed the previous round’s highest active Pecking Order bid. If no active bid remained, keep the existing first opener. The Revolving Door does not transfer this role. Rules term The first party in the Pecking Order or the recipient of the highest bid through the Revolving Door
Lobbying firm One of six political influence organizations whose identity kit marks a player’s bids. One human controls one firm at four to six players and two firms at two or three players, while retaining one player economy and score. Themed rules term A party
Operation token A neutral reusable 25 mm square token that may be given while behind a player’s screen or committed to a bid and executed at a party or the Pecking Order. Themed rules term A scoring card, player marker, or party Support
Operation family Organise, Rally, Smear, or Court; each has one baseline effect available at every party. Rules term A party bonus
Overture A party’s one-way pointer, which Court may set to a different party. Two reciprocal overtures form a coalition. Themed rules term A coalition or a lobbying-firm agreement
Party bonus One of two favored-operation effects offered by a party. Each may be claimed with one matching operation token per contest, with bids receiving first refusal from highest rank to lowest; most resolve immediately, while Night Parliament’s resolve after ordinary operation tokens. Rules term An operation’s universal baseline effect
Party One of six animal political institutions that serves as an ordinary influence target. Themed rules term A player or lobbying firm
Pecking Order The board and persistent party sequence that determine party-contest resolution, as well as the mandatory no-opening-bid auction that changes that sequence before the parties resolve. Its operation tokens execute from the lowest active bid to the highest, then its bids Revolve normally. Themed rules term Bid rank, player order, electoral support, or score
Points A player’s public point balance. Players begin with 5 points, doubled to 10 at two or three players; gifts and Election Day results change the balance, which may become negative. Rules term Clout, Support, or a base district score
Revolving Door The transfer after a contest in which each complete bid moves to the owner of the bid immediately below it and the lowest moves to the highest. Themed rules term Party recruitment or a reward paid by the board
Scoring card A hidden player card containing three district–party objectives plus positive and negative relative-seat references. Rules term An operation token, party board, or public objective
Support Public party pieces from an unlimited supply that occupy limited district spots and leave the map when removed. Rules term Clout, score, a lobbying firm, or votes already cast
Archived vocabulary

Ladder-era terms

Ladder, position, and rung described an early shared-track hypothesis. Press Perch was the former name for the Pecking Order board and auction. These terms remain in historical documents but have no current rules meaning.

Style rule

Use one term for one game concept. If a component and an action share a name, change one before writing more rules.
