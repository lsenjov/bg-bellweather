Game vision

The political contest that gives the auction core a purpose.

Index/Game vision

In the Republic of Bellweather, animal political parties compete for votes while rival lobbying firms pursue hidden interests across the electoral map.

Decided

One-sentence pitch

Rival lobbying firms stack Leverage, Bluff, and operation cards to reorganise Support across crowded districts, rally animal parties, smear their rivals, broker coalitions, and manipulate the media Pecking Order before Election Day.

Decided

The theme’s promise

  • Parties are targets, not players: lobbying firms pursue private district–party agendas while every player can move every party’s public Support.
  • Access has a price: committing more Leverage secures earlier access to a party but exposes a richer network to the Revolving Door.
  • Political position is mutable: district Support, Court Support, and Coalition Targets change without mapping any party directly to a current real-world party.
  • The satire stays institutional: animal parties create distance while preserving recognizable lobbying, media, and coalition incentives.

Provisional

Desired table moments

  • A lobbying firm visibly supports one party while secretly positioning operation cards elsewhere.
  • A player commits one more Leverage to act first, knowing the next-ranked rival may inherit the entire package.
  • A late Pecking Order bid changes which party resolves next and invalidates a plan elsewhere.
  • A Smear opens the only free spot in a crucial district, changing where later Support can move.
  • A weak or empty influence campaign does little immediately but captures a valuable exposed network through the Revolving Door.
  • A party succeeds electorally while advancing an agenda its apparent supporters did not expect.

Keep only moments that survive the first prototype.

Boundaries for the first prototype

  • Prove the repeated influence and Revolving Door loop before adding a detailed election simulation.
  • Use a paper district map, party sheets, cubes, coins, pointers, and one Pecking Order track wherever possible.
  • Keep the parties structurally distinct without assigning them direct real-world equivalents.
  • Do not design exceptions to fix outcomes that have not happened in play.
  • Do not mistake an auction format for a complete game.

First proof

The theme earns its place when players can explain why district capacity, a party’s district or Court Support, its Coalition Target, its favored-operation bonuses, or its position in the Pecking Order changed an influence bid—and why acting first was worth exposing the committed network.
