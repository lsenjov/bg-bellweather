Game vision

A political contest built around access, public card piles, and timely exits.

Index/Game vision

In the Republic of Bellweather, animal political parties compete for votes while rival lobbying firms pursue hidden interests across the electoral map.

Decided

One-sentence pitch

Rival lobbying firms open access to animal parties, play Operations into tempting public piles, and decide when to collect or close those contacts while shaping Bellweather for three Elections.

Decided

The theme’s promise

  • Parties are targets, not players: lobbying firms pursue private district–party agendas while every player can move every party’s public Support.
  • Access creates a claim: a Firm marker secures a party’s eventual Closure pile, while any rival can still Collect the pile first.
  • Political position is mutable: district Support, Court Support, and Coalition Targets change without mapping any party directly to a current real-world party.
  • The satire stays institutional: animal parties create distance while preserving recognizable lobbying, media, and coalition incentives.

Provisional

Desired table moments

  • A player feeds cards into a party they did not open, hoping to Collect the pile before its opener closes it.
  • An opener waits for one more valuable Operation, knowing a rival can harvest the pile first.
  • A mid-orbit Closure ends the year and makes its owner next year’s Early Bird.
  • A Smear opens the only free spot in a crucial district, changing where later Support can move.
  • A player empties one party’s pile, only for it to become valuable again before the year ends.
  • A party succeeds electorally while advancing an agenda its apparent supporters did not expect.

Keep only moments that survive the first prototype.

Boundaries for the first prototype

  • Prove the repeated Operate, Collect, and Close loop before adding more exceptions or economies.
  • Use a paper district map, party sheets, cubes, cards, counters, and Firm markers wherever possible.
  • Keep the parties structurally distinct without assigning them direct real-world equivalents.
  • Do not design exceptions to fix outcomes that have not happened in play.
  • Keep the annual boundary uniform, including at thematic midterms, before Elections, and after Year 6.

First proof

The theme earns its place when players can explain why they opened a party, fed its Operation pile, harvested another player’s pile, or closed their own—and how that timing changed the map they carried into an Election.
