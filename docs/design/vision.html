Game vision

The political contest that gives the auction core a purpose.

Index/Game vision

In the Republic of Bellwether, animal political parties compete for votes while rival lobbying houses compete to control what those parties will do with power.

Decided

One-sentence pitch

Rival lobbying houses commit Clout and operatives to expand animal political parties, reshape their platforms, form coalitions, and manipulate the media Pecking Order before Election Day.

Decided

The theme’s promise

  • Parties are targets, not players: the parties seek electoral success, while lobbying houses seek influence over the resulting government.
  • Access has a price: committing more Clout secures earlier access to a party but exposes a richer network to the Revolving Door.
  • Political identity is mutable: campaigning, organization, platforms, and coalitions can change without mapping any party directly to a current real-world party.
  • The satire stays institutional: animal parties create distance while preserving recognizable lobbying, media, and coalition incentives.

Provisional

Desired table moments

  • A lobbying house visibly supports one party while secretly positioning operatives elsewhere.
  • A player commits one more Clout to act first, knowing the next-ranked rival may inherit the entire package.
  • A late move to the Press Perch changes the Pecking Order and invalidates a plan elsewhere.
  • A weak or empty influence campaign does little immediately but captures a valuable exposed network through the Revolving Door.
  • A party succeeds electorally while advancing an agenda its apparent supporters did not expect.

Keep only moments that survive the first prototype.

Boundaries for the first prototype

  • Prove the repeated influence and Revolving Door loop before adding a detailed election simulation.
  • Use paper party sheets, cubes, coins, and one Pecking Order track wherever possible.
  • Keep the parties structurally distinct without assigning them direct real-world equivalents.
  • Do not design exceptions to fix outcomes that have not happened in play.
  • Do not mistake an auction format for a complete game.

First proof

The theme earns its place when players can explain why a party, its compatible operations, or its position in the Pecking Order changed an influence bid—and why acting first was worth exposing the committed network.
