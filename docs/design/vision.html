“Ladder Bidding” is a productive constraint, not yet a design: it suggests rising stakes, visible position, and contested value. This page exists to discover which of those ideas deserves to become the game.

Hypothesis

The title’s promise

The working name points toward three testable expectations. None is a committed mechanic.

  • A visible climb: players can read relative progress or escalating value at a glance.
  • A meaningful bid: offering more should hurt, reveal intent, or close off a future option.
  • Pressure from position: where a player sits on the ladder should change what a bid means.

Desired table moments

  • A player pauses because one more step is valuable and visibly dangerous.
  • A bid communicates something without making the outcome automatic.
  • The table understands why a result happened without consulting a flowchart.
  • A losing bid leaves a player with a changed problem, not a wasted turn.

These are starting prompts. Keep only moments that survive the first prototype.

Boundaries for the first prototype

  • Prove one repeated decision loop before adding theme or content variety.
  • Use paper, cubes, coins, and a single track wherever possible.
  • Do not design exceptions to fix outcomes that have not happened in play.
  • Do not mistake an auction format for a complete game.

First proof

The idea earns a second prototype when players can explain why they changed a bid because of ladder position—and want to face that decision again.
