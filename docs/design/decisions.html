2026-08-19 Print each Bonus card with its home party’s emblem as a pale, oversized watermark behind the rules text, using the same six emblem drawings as the party workspaces and scoring cards. The repeated emblem makes a card’s home party recognizable at a glance and keeps the new collectible deck aligned with the established print-component identity system without taking space from its rules. The watermark competes with rules text in print, party colour alone identifies cards reliably enough, or the shared emblem artwork changes.
2026-08-19 After ordinary Election 3 scoring, award a final card rank bonus using every ordinary Operation card and held Bonus card in each human player’s hand after Year 6 Cleanup. Exclude Bonus cards at party homes. A player scores one point for every other player whose count is less than or equal to theirs; equivalently, sort counts into slots from 0 through one less than the player count and give ties the highest occupied slot. Rank each low-player human’s combined hand once, then determine winners after adding the bonus. Apply the breaking change as Ruleset 21 without saved-game migration. The terminal rank gives collected cards value beyond future actions and turns late card use into a visible tradeoff. The comparison formula handles every tie pattern without a separate tie-breaker or fractional points. Card hoarding dominates late board play, Bonus-card ownership overwhelms ordinary collection, the low-to-high rank spread is too small or too large, or players cannot verify final counts cleanly.
2026-08-19 Replace the two once-per-year bonus states on each party board with twelve unique collectible Bonus cards, two per party. Put every card face up at its home party during setup. A player who receives a party pile by Collect, voluntary Close, or automatic Closure may optionally take at most one available card from that party into New Year; Closure offers this choice even when the pile is empty. Bonus cards move to private hands during Cleanup, may be held indefinitely, and play as atomic standalone actions during Operate at their home party or its current reciprocal coalition partner. A successful play returns the card home immediately. Remove claimed-bonus state and bonus-used markers, and apply the breaking change as Ruleset 20 without saved-game migration. A physical card gives each power visible location, ownership, circulation, and opportunity cost. Tying acquisition to pile transfer reinforces the central Operate–Collect–Close decision, while immediate return allows contested reuse without a yearly reset subsystem. Cards remain held too long, early collection creates runaway access, automatic Closure choices interrupt the year ending, reciprocal play makes party identity unclear, immediate recollection loops dominate, or the twelve-card physical footprint is not worth the added circulation.
2026-08-19 Shorten the campaign from twelve years to six while retaining three Elections after Years 2, 4, and 6. Treat Years 1, 3, and 5 as thematic midterm boundaries with ordinary Cleanup and no additional mechanical effect. Keep the three fixed agenda slots and all Election scoring unchanged. Apply the breaking schedule as Ruleset 19 without saved-game migration. Two-year cycles preserve the three-act Election arc while removing half of the repeated annual procedure. The thematic midpoint gives each cycle a recognizable first and second half without adding an untested subsystem. Agendas cannot develop in time, cards circulate too slowly, Capital positions become overly dependent on setup, year-ending tempo dominates the shorter cycle, or the game becomes too brief to create meaningful pile tension.
2026-08-19 Increase each standard player's Organise supply from two cards to three and preserve complete low-player doubling with six Organise cards at two or three players. Retain four Rally, two Smear, and two Court cards per standard player. Expand the six-player print supply from sixty to sixty-six Operation cards across five sheets and apply the breaking setup change as Ruleset 18 without saved-game migration. More Organise access makes relocation and multi-year route preparation less dependent on recovering a small shared supply while leaving reinforcement, removal, and coalition influence unchanged for a focused balance test. Organise dominates play, players routinely finish years with unused Organise cards, the larger hand weakens Collection timing, or the fifth print sheet is not worth the six added cards.
2026-08-16 Use each Firm opening marker as its party’s complete open-state indicator. A marker in the opening well means open; every voluntary or automatic Closure returns it face up to its player, where returned markers show the public Closure count and remain unavailable until the next year. Remove the separate Closed wells and all six Closed markers from the physical suite. The opening marker already answers whether a party accepts actions. Returning it also makes Closure tactile and provides the majority count without maintaining a second piece or preserving ownership that no longer affects play. Players confuse a closed party with a party that was never opened, accidentally reuse a returned marker during the same year, or cannot count Closures reliably across player areas.
2026-08-16 Connect each capacity-two inner spoke directly to a capacity-six city: Crown Road to Harbormouth, Canal Ward to Grand Market, and Old Quarter to Ironwood. Remove the spokes’ former connections to Northreach, Reedwater, and Westgate while retaining the outer ring, all sixteen districts and capacities, eighteen connections, and fifty-seven Support spaces. Re-register all twenty-four scoring cards with new district objectives so every card remains a unique pairwise non-neighboring 6/4/2 triple and every odd–even low-player pair still contains six distinct districts; retain card IDs, parties, seat references, and all frequency balances. Apply the breaking change as Ruleset 17 without saved-game migration. The three full starting cities become the explicit gateways to Bellweather Centre, while the capacity-two outer districts return to the same two-neighbor role as the rest of the ring. Rebuilding every district assignment preserves the deck’s global structure and avoids treating the new adjacency rule as three isolated card repairs. The full starting cities make their spokes too difficult to enter, direct Centre access compresses the map too far, city adjacency over-amplifies neighboring bonuses, or the replacement scoring pairs create materially uneven routes or Capital prospects.
2026-08-16 Replace Night Parliament’s provisional Night Shift Double Rally with Quiet Hours: after Rally, add one Night Support to a player-selected otherwise empty district. Bellweather Centre is a legal target while empty. Apply the breaking change as Ruleset 16 without saved-game migration. Global placement into unattended territory gives the owls a memorable after-hours identity and a Rally expression distinct from Riverworks’ neighboring growth and Many Wings’ scattering. The empty-district restriction preserves immediate resolution, prevents reinforcement, and reduces the available targets as the map fills. Remote objective seeding is too reliable, early access to Bellweather Centre is too strong, the bonus becomes unusable too early, or players frequently misread “empty” after resolving the Rally.
2026-08-16 Adopt one clean Ruleset 15 print suite: six exact party workspaces with card-sized Firm-opening and Operation-pile wells; one shared sheet containing the annual sequence, all public state, and all six Firm piece kits; and one 200 × 120 mm player folio per human with three ordered agenda wells, a card-sized New Year well, four exact 14 mm Collection wells, and the Lobby reference. Each Firm kit on the shared sheet contains one annual opening marker, two Collection counters, and one score marker. Use saturated colour only for party state and muted stationery treatments for Firms. Name the card source and PDF for Operations, and keep retired auction materials out of the active print bundle without compatibility aliases. The physical zones now mirror the yearly loop directly: party ownership and piles live with parties, hidden hands and fixed agendas live with players, and public timing state lives on one shared sheet. Exact card wells reduce ambiguous piles, while distinct shapes and visual systems keep party state, Firm ownership, Collection actions, and scoring from being confused. The separate party workspaces consume too much table area, stacked New Year or Operation piles become unreadable, low-player players cannot associate two Firm kits with one folio, or print-and-play cutting reveals a simpler component format.

Keep the 40 × 61 mm zero-gutter format and visible family indices, and identify Ruleset 15 in every printed sheet label. The temporary legacy path was removed on 2026-08-16. The Operation economy changes, duplex backs become necessary, or the compact faces prove hard to read.
2026-08-13 When observer admission is enabled, let a new observer use the invitation code after the game ends to open the completed state and replay. Persist only the observer credential needed for authentication; do not append or broadcast a post-completion event, so the canonical replay sequence remains fixed. A person who receives the invitation code after play should not need a participant token saved before completion. Treating this as replay admission preserves the completed game as immutable history rather than pretending that live observation continued after play. Invitation codes are shared too broadly for full-information replay access, completed replays need separate host-controlled publication, or retained replay-viewer credentials require an expiry policy.
2026-08-13 Partially superseded: Resolve an Operate action one card at a time. After each of the first two cards resolves immediately, the acting player may finish or choose another card at the same locked party against the current board; a third card finishes automatically. Ruleset 20 removed the one-bonus limit when it replaced recurring bonuses with standalone cards. In the app, retain select controls and also make legal party files, districts, and rival Support directly clickable. Party Openings use click-to-select followed by explicit confirmation. Sequential choice makes board changes meaningful without requiring players to precommit now-invalid targets. Direct table targeting restores the map as the primary play surface while confirmation protects the consequential Opening placement. Operate sequences become too slow, updated-state choices make three cards routinely dominant, players lose track of whether they are still operating, or table targeting creates accidental selections.
2026-08-13 Partially superseded: Replace the auction game outright with a twelve-year party-access loop. Each year uses Party Openings, clockwise Lobby actions, and Cleanup; Elections follow Cleanup in Years 4, 8, and 12. Firm markers establish who receives a party at Closure and are not bids. Lobby actions are Operate with one to three cards at one party, Collect a whole nonempty pile by spending one of two annual counters, Close one’s own opened party after one’s first Lobby turn, or Pass. A strict majority of opened parties closed or a complete uninterrupted orbit of passes ends the year immediately; remaining piles go to their openers. Remove Leverage, Bluff, counterbids, the Pecking Order, Revolving Door, gifts, and auction timers. Apply the clean replacement as Ruleset 15 without executable compatibility paths. Public piles put the strategic question directly on when to contribute, harvest, or secure party access. Removing the auction economy also removes the timing and component machinery that no longer serves that question. Ruleset 19 shortened the schedule to six years and moved Elections to Years 2, 4, and 6. Revisit the retained annual loop when players cannot identify meaningful Operate–Collect–Close timing, openings feel like guaranteed ownership, Collections empty every useful pile, or mid-orbit endings routinely deny more play than they create tension.
2026-08-13 Give each standard player one Firm opening and two Collection counters per year; at two or three players double Firms, openings, counters, Operation cards, and starting points. Use ABBA at two players and ABCCBA at three only for Party Openings; Lobby actions always proceed clockwise from Early Bird. The player whose action or final pass ends the year becomes next year’s Early Bird. Keep collected and closed cards visibly in a New Year area, unusable for the current year, then release them during uniform Cleanup before any scheduled Election. The doubled low-player economy preserves the intended quantity of party access. A single ordinary action orbit is easier to follow, while awarding next Early Bird to the year-ender connects the exit decision to the following year without requiring a separate calculation. The same player chains Early Bird too easily, later seats lose too many actions, low-player doubling makes the table state excessive, or remembering New Year contents creates disputes.
2026-08-13 Partially superseded: Limit each complete Operate action to one party bonus even when it plays three cards. Ruleset 20 removed that limit with collectible standalone Bonus cards. Replace delayed Night Shift provisionally with an immediate second Rally in the same district. Add Capital scoring after the relative-seat modifier: a scoring card’s three parties score 0 for zero or one present in Bellweather Centre, 1 for two, and 3 for all three; duplicates count once and coalition partners do not count. At two or three players, both cards in a registered pair score ordinary objectives, but its fixed first odd-numbered card alone supplies Capital parties. One bonus keeps a three-card action from multiplying exceptions, immediate Night Shift removes delayed yearly state, and modest fixed Capital scoring makes the unchanged Centre relevant without giving low-player seats a scoring-time card choice. Three-card actions are still too explosive, Night Shift loses its identity, Centre occupation dominates ordinary agendas, or registered low-player pairs have materially unequal Capital prospects.
2026-08-12 Prepare the complete scoring deck once at setup and deal every player three fixed, ordered agenda slots for Elections 1, 2, and 3. At four to six players, shuffle the twenty-four individual cards once and deal one per slot. At two or three players, assemble adjacent odd–even card IDs as twelve registered compatible pair packets, shuffle all twelve face-down packets once, and deal one per slot; this guarantees all six or nine pairs without exposing passed-over cards or reshuffling. Let players inspect all of their own slots immediately, reveal only the assigned slot at each Election, leave scored slots face up and public, and never reassign or redeal them. Replace the 200 × 140 mm single-well firm board with a compact 200 × 120 mm board whose three overlapping 40 × 61 mm wells preserve the Election order and accept stacked pairs. Apply the breaking state and projection change as ruleset version 14 with no saved-game migration. Full-game foresight supports long-range map planning without turning agendas into selectable options. Explicit slot boundaries preserve the low-player six-objective balance and keep future agendas private from opponents. Advance knowledge causes excessive analysis, players routinely sacrifice the current Election for later slots, setup sequences decide too much of the game, or three ordered physical wells make the private play area cumbersome.
2026-08-12 Replace the party powers with the theme-first matrix: Honeycomb Organise/Court with Waggle Route and Common Cause; Old Shell Organise/Smear with Dig In and Stonewall; Foxglove Smear/Court with Spin and Whisper Network; Riverworks Organise/Rally with Canal Network and Public Works; Many Wings Rally/Court with Scatter the Flock and Joint Campaign; and Night Parliament Rally/Smear with Night Shift and Midnight Leak. 2026-08-10 Allow observers to join an active web game by invitation code when the host enabled observer admission, and display that code to every authenticated participant throughout the game. Continue to close new player seats once play starts and close all new admission after the game ends. Observation does not alter the player roster or reveal seat-private state, so requiring observers to arrive before the host starts needlessly prevents late viewers and playtest facilitators from following an ongoing game. Keeping the code visible lets any participant invite those observers without relying on the host’s local browser state. Late observer joins disrupt active players, moderation requires host-only invitation control, or completed games need invitation-based observer access in addition to authenticated replay access.
2026-08-05 Reveal each contest as a whole when it becomes the next contest to resolve. At that boundary, cancel and return equal counterbids in that contest before ranking its survivors. Matching the reveal, cancellation, and refund boundary also prevents concealed outcomes from leaking through crossed-out filings or unexpectedly restored reserves. Players cannot prepare adequately for later contests, reserve changes still imply a hidden outcome, or staging the repeated reveal and cancellation procedure makes resolution too slow.
2026-08-05 During operation resolution, offer only remaining families with at least one legal current choice. Reject an illegal player-selected parameter without consuming the decision or card.
