2026-07-28 Use a fixed starting Priority Order in the first prototype. A stable initial sequence makes sessions comparable and exposes structural target-order advantages before adding setup variance. The ordering effects are understood well enough that random setup would improve replayability without obscuring balance.
2026-07-28 Do not score held currency or command cards directly. Closed-economy resources should provide future agency; scoring currency would turn empty low bids into a direct point engine and make acting early cost points. A theme requires reserve value to contribute to victory without overwhelming target outcomes.
2026-07-28 After the final auction, begin the next round without automatic income, draws, refreshes, or other resource changes. The transferred bids already define the next round’s reserves, and the fully closed economy should not need a hidden cleanup exception. The loop needs a theme-driven between-round procedure to prevent stagnation or maintain board state.
2026-07-28 At timer expiry, withdraw any counterbid that is held, in transit, or not clearly released inside one target’s bid area. Legal last-second movement needs an objective lock condition that does not depend on stated intent. The bright-line placement rule creates frequent accidental withdrawals or physical disputes.
2026-07-28 Allow unrestricted nonbinding table talk during counterbidding, but forbid showing hidden bid contents or transferring resources outside resolution. Claims, threats, coordination, and broken promises reinforce the live bluffing phase without adding enforceable contracts. Negotiation overwhelms the timed decisions, produces chronic kingmaking, or creates unacceptable social pressure.
2026-07-28 Use a 90-second counterbid timer in the first four-player prototype. Players need time to assess four openings and physically construct and revise two concealed bids before component handling becomes familiar. Players consistently lock early or the phase lacks meaningful time pressure.
2026-07-28 Each command executed on the Priority Order area swaps one adjacent pair of targets. Larger reorders require several commands, and lower-ranked bids retain the ability to contest earlier changes. Adjacent swaps are too weak to justify opening or bidding on the Priority Order area.
2026-07-28 Make commands usable by a category of board targets, with each target defining the command’s concrete effect. Categories preserve meaningful differences in command value without creating universal or single-target assets in a closed economy. Category matching adds lookup cost or still leaves too many command cards unusable.
2026-07-28 Give players identical command sets in the first prototype. The initial test should isolate the bidding and transfer loop; capability asymmetry should emerge from player choices rather than setup variance. The symmetric setup produces scripted openings or fails to exercise command valuation.
2026-07-28 Use a fully closed economy for currency and command cards. The mechanism is about wagering and redistributing a fixed supply rather than generating income or paying a bank. The fixed supply causes unrecoverable stagnation or leaves target effects without enough economic vocabulary.
2026-07-28 Represent prototype currency in whole-number denominations of 1 and 5, using identical opaque containers for hidden bids. Denominations reduce bulk and handling time while the containers prevent value from being read from the physical size of a bid. Making change leaks too much information or token weight and sound still reveal useful bid information.
2026-07-28 Give each player 20 currency in the first four-player prototype. The first test needs enough range to split resources across three possible bids while still making large commitments consequential. Players rarely approach insolvency, ties are too rare, or bids cluster in a narrow part of the range.
2026-07-28 At two or three players, give each player two opening auctions and four additional bids; at four or more, give each player one opening auction and two additional bids. Prototype first at four players. Both brackets supply an average of two additional bids per opened auction while low player counts retain enough simultaneous contests. The low-count game becomes too crowded or the same ratios produce meaningfully different incentives across player counts.
2026-07-28 Require each player to fund as many opening auctions as they can, up to their player-count allotment, while preserving their full additional-bid allotment. Players cannot voluntarily claim insolvency’s protection, but partial insolvency does not require an artificial bailout or remove recovery bids. Mandatory opening forces strategically empty choices or makes recovery from a weak position harder.
2026-07-28 A player unable to fund every allotted opening retains their full additional-bid allowance, including the option to make empty bids. Bankruptcy temporarily removes initiative while preserving the low-bid collection route back into the currency economy. Currency-poor players cannot recover reliably or skipping their auction destabilizes the round.
2026-07-28 Require every bid to contain the actual currency tokens and command cards being committed. Physical escrow prevents duplicate allocation and makes concealed reserves and empty bids self-enforcing. The containers or handling time make live bid revision impractical.
2026-07-28 An auction still resolves and transfers its bids if an earlier effect removes or disables its target. Target removal may invalidate commands, but it should not rewrite the locked resource exchange after the global reveal. Resolving targetless auctions creates excessive procedure or unintuitive transfers.
2026-07-28 Compare currency equality only among bids in the same auction, ignoring ownership and command contents; zero-value counterbids tie normally. Tie cancellation should affect the contest being ranked, and competing empty bids should cancel rather than create several risk-free ranks. Cross-auction cancellation proves necessary to create meaningful interaction between otherwise separate auctions.
2026-07-28 Limit each player to their player-count counterbid allowance throughout the timed phase, not merely when the timer expires. Visible feints should require relocating a scarce bid slot rather than adding unlimited temporary decoys. The live-marker allowance does not create enough movement or misdirection during the timed phase.
2026-07-28 Lock opening bids before the counterbid timer begins, and allow players to use either additional bid on their own auction. The public opening offer remains reliable while self-bidding permits intentional multi-rank, collection, and insurance tactics. Self-bidding routinely eliminates the opening bid’s transfer risk or becomes compulsory rather than situational.
2026-07-28 Do not impose a per-bid command limit; a player may commit any number of commands they own. Each additional command increases both the bid’s immediate action and the value at risk in the circular transfer. Large bundles create excessive resolution time, overwhelming bursts, or a dominant all-in pattern.
2026-07-28 Physically conceal both the identities and total count of each player’s uncommitted commands. Opponents should not be able to exploit a player merely because that player is visibly short of commands; revealed transfers remain available to memory and inference. Concealed counts make component verification difficult or memory tracking becomes a practical expectation.
2026-07-28 Keep each player’s available currency physically concealed, while allowing opponents to infer or remember it from revealed exchanges. Opponents should not be able to safely underbid a currency-poor player or suppress that player’s ability to collect richer bids from below. Tracking totals becomes a practical expectation rather than an exceptional feat of memory, or concealed wealth creates unacceptable verification problems.
2026-07-28 A bid commits command cards or types, not their variable parameters. The bid owner chooses legal parameters and command order during execution. Players retain tactical agency after the global reveal while still wagering specific capabilities. Late parameter choices cause excessive analysis or make hidden command identities insufficiently binding.
2026-07-28 Reveal every bid on the board simultaneously before resolving the Priority Order auction or any other auction. The Priority Order contest should reorder known commitments, and no auction should gain an information advantage from being resolved later. The complete reveal creates excessive calculation or removes useful suspense from later auctions.
2026-07-28 A command that is illegal when its turn arrives fails without substitution and still transfers with its bid. Earlier commands and auction ordering remain consequential without destroying the reusable command asset. Failed commands are too hard to anticipate or leave players without meaningful bidding choices.
2026-07-28 Run counterbid placement simultaneously under a timer. Players may change a counterbid’s hidden contents, relocate it, or withdraw it until time expires, including at the last second. Fully revisable placement makes commitment, feints, attention, and timing part of the bidding game. Late moves routinely deny meaningful reactions, reward dexterity over judgment, or create accessibility problems.
2026-07-28 Resolve the Priority Order area first, apply its changes immediately, and preserve the updated list until a later command changes it. Players can contest the current resolution sequence and create persistent plans around the order of interacting board areas. Priority contests become compulsory or add procedure without producing consequential choices.
2026-07-28 Rank bids rather than players. A player may place multiple additional bids in one auction and occupy multiple ranks. Players can combine a high bid for early commands with lower bids intended to collect wagers above them. Self-transfer patterns remove too much risk or consistently dominate distributing bids across auctions.
2026-07-28 Protect only the original opening bid from equal-value cancellation and require it to contain at least one currency. All additional bids are ordinary counterbids and may be empty. The protection prevents a public opening amount from being cancelled at no resource risk, while empty counterbids make a limited bid slot itself a wager. Opener protection is too strong or empty counterbids collect value without a meaningful opportunity cost.
2026-07-28 Use bid amount to buy command priority, then transfer the complete reusable bids around the ranking. Every distinct active bid acts, and the circular transfer makes currency and commands a wager rather than a payment to a bank. Players cannot predict the exchange well enough to make deliberate bids or uncontested commands produce excessive value.
2026-07-28 Use “Ladder Bidding” as the working title. It is compact and provides two useful design prompts without committing to a theme. The core loop suggests a clearer promise or the title constrains exploration.
2026-07-28 Keep mechanics explicitly unresolved in the initial archive. The name alone is not enough evidence to present an auction or track system as a rule. The first playable loop is chosen.
2026-07-28 Use plain HTML for human-facing game documents. The idea needs durable, browsable notes without a build or dependency toolchain. A generated rulebook, public site, or component database becomes useful.
