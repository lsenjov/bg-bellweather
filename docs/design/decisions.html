2026-07-31 Remove the large “Classified” word from the shared transferable bid-card back. Retain the Republic of Bellweather register line, central LB Influence Filing seal, patterned navy field, nested borders, and Revolving Door registry footer. The seal already communicates a concealed filing. Removing the redundant label gives the back a cleaner focal hierarchy without changing card identity or hidden-information handling. 2026-07-31 Rename the setting from Bellwether to Bellweather, Clout to Leverage, the Court operation to Coalition, and each party’s Overture to its Coalition Target. Apply the same breaking rename to package scopes, state, protocol fields, operation IDs, environment variables, and saved-session keys with no legacy aliases. Give the six transferable card families unique L/B/O/R/S/C indices in the upper-left and rotated lower-right corners. Bellweather is the intended place-name. The Coalition vocabulary directly connects the action, directional target, and reciprocal state. Leverage gives every card family a distinct starting letter, and paired corner indices keep the family recognizable in either orientation and in a fanned hand. Players confuse the Coalition operation with an established coalition, Coalition Target is too long for components, any two card indices remain difficult to distinguish at a glance, or a future public release requires save migration.

Restore the map diagram from 76% to 88% scale without changing its geometry or Support layout. A separate tracker sheet gives the score track enough range and physical marker space while returning the map surface to district play. The extra sheet creates excessive table spread, the six-row score snake is difficult to follow, scores regularly exceed 40, or the enlarged map still does not suit the chosen Support pieces.

Periodic thinning creates room for the increased Rally supply without erasing all map development. Random survivors preserve some campaign work, while Bellwether Centre’s immunity gives the map a persistent central chokepoint. Random survival makes pre-Election play feel futile, too few districts are named to relieve crowding, or the permanent Bellwether Centre state becomes stagnant rather than strategically tense.

A 2:1 Rally-to-Smear ratio should produce more persistent Support and more competition for district capacity. Rally dominates bid composition, the map remains permanently full despite Election thinning, or the added cards make physical reserves and bid stacks cumbersome.

2026-07-30 Revise the printable boards from playtest feedback. Use one source-coloured overture token on target-coloured party positions; remove the opening-area container instruction. Give firm boards an exact 40 × 61 mm agenda well, operation baselines, and terse reminders for both powers of all six parties, removing issued-piece and round-overview panels. Consolidating shared trackers and tokens reduces setup sheets without changing rules. Target colours are still ambiguous, party-power reminders become too terse to use, cutting tokens compromises the Pecking Order board, or the map trackers crowd district state.
2026-07-30 Make the simultaneous counterbid timer configurable and optional. The host either disables it or chooses its duration before play. Lock the phase when every player is ready or, when enabled, when the timer expires, whichever happens first. A ready player may become unready until unanimous readiness locks the phase. A configurable clock supports both time-pressured human play and deterministic automated playtests. Unanimous readiness avoids dead time without forcing a clock on groups that prefer deliberate negotiation. Players use readiness to pressure others, repeated ready changes create confusion, untimed games stall, or different timer settings make playtest results difficult to compare.
2026-07-30 Play twelve total Campaign Rounds in three sets of four, with Election Days after rounds 4, 8, and 12. Begin each player with 5 public points, doubled to 10 at two or three players. Election scoring changes that public balance and may take it below zero. After the third Election Day, every player tied for the highest point balance shares the win; use no tie-breaker. Three shorter scoring intervals keep every agenda active for four rounds while preserving three Election Days. Starting points create a transferable score resource before the first election, and shared victory makes an equal final balance a complete result. Four rounds do not give players enough opportunity to pursue an agenda, transferable starting points overwhelm Election Day scoring, or shared victories make final-round choices less consequential.
2026-07-30 Partially superseded: At four to six players, give each human 10 Clout, two Organise, two Rally, two Smear, one Court, one opening-bid marker, and two counterbids. At two or three players, double the complete supply to 20 Clout, four Organise, four Rally, four Smear, two Court, two opening-bid markers, and four counterbids; also double the starting point balance. The low-player game gives each human two firm kits and twice the bidding responsibility, so doubling their economy, bid pieces, and initial points preserves the intended resources per controlled firm. The doubled low-player structure remains current. On 2026-07-31, Rally increased to four per standard player and eight per doubled player, and firm identity cards replaced specialized opening and counterbid pieces.
2026-07-30 Give every party an unlimited Support supply. Begin every party with one Support in each of the three 6-capacity districts: Harbormouth, Grand Market, and Ironwood. Begin every overture unpointed. Randomize the six party tokens to create the starting Pecking Order. District capacity remains the only limit on adding Support. The three large districts begin symmetrically occupied by all six parties, coalitions must be created during play, and changing the initial Pecking Order avoids a permanent scripted setup. Unlimited Support makes Rally growth too easy, full large districts make early Rally actions too brittle, an unpointed start delays Court relevance, or random order creates excessive setup variance.
2026-07-30 Partially superseded: Allow a player to give one other player any mixture of eligible Clout, operation tokens, and public points as a one-way atomic gift. Clout and operation tokens are eligible only behind the giver’s screen; committed bid contents remain ineligible. A gift cannot exceed any eligible holding or the giver’s current point balance. It has no recipient acceptance step and cannot be reversed. The game does not enforce agreements; players may keep or break promises and enforce deals socially. One-way gifts express negotiated settlements without requiring the rules or application to model contracts, exchanges, acceptance, rollback, or future conditions. Atomic validation prevents partial transfers when any stated resource is unavailable. The gift structure remains current. On 2026-07-30, cards replaced Clout and operation tokens, and eligible Bluff cards joined the transferable supply.
2026-07-30 Partially superseded: Place opening bids in clockwise player order. Randomly choose the first opener at setup and track that role with one shared opening-priority marker. In later rounds, the player who placed the previous round’s highest active Pecking Order bid opens first; if no active bid remained, keep the existing first opener. Each player completes their full opening allocation during one turn, choosing only parties that have not already received an opening bid. Sequential placement gives the Pecking Order auction a consequence in the following round and resolves competing attempts to open the same party without a separate collision procedure. Completing a player’s allocation on one turn keeps the sequence readable at two and three players. The procedure remains current. On 2026-07-30, the shared component was named the Early Bird marker.
2026-07-29 On Election Day, treat drawn Support belonging to either member of a coalition as matching both parties for district–party objective scoring. A party outside a coalition matches only its own Support. Reciprocal overtures now create a direct electoral benefit: each partner broadens the Support that can score for objectives naming it, making Court actions and coalition timing relevant to the existing victory system. Coalitions make objectives too easy to score, create abrupt swings immediately before Election Day, or make helping a coalition partner strategically indistinguishable from helping the named party.
2026-07-29 Superseded: Allow players to trade Clout and operation tokens at any time, but only while those components are physically behind a player’s screen. Components committed to opening bids or counterbids cannot be traded, even before a bid locks; retrieved or withdrawn components become eligible again after returning behind the screen. Make a deal binding only when every term can be satisfied immediately; any promise depending on a later action, choice, transfer, state, or outcome remains nonbinding. Do not reverse completed transfers when a related future promise is broken. Immediate exchanges create enforceable resource negotiation without requiring the game to police future behavior or resolve conditional contracts. The screen boundary gives trade eligibility a visible physical test and preserves bid escrow throughout placement and resolution. Replaced on 2026-07-30 by atomic one-way gifts of Clout, operation tokens, and points with no rules-enforced agreements.
2026-07-29 Call the player organizations lobbying firms, shortened to firms on components. Replace “House” kit labels with “Firm” while retaining each firm’s number, emblem, and name. Remove “70 mm” from counterbid-cover faces and keep that dimension only in print and manufacturing instructions. The six organizations are public-affairs consultancies, so “firm” describes them more naturally and avoids confusion with a legislative chamber or political dynasty. Cover dimensions help fabrication but do not identify ownership or affect play. Players mistake firms for the animal parties, “firm” conflicts with later client or employer terminology, or fabricators cannot find the cover diameter in the surrounding component instructions.
2026-07-29 Remove fixed party numbers from the movable Pecking Order tokens. Removing it makes the component’s visual language unambiguous: numbers are positions and tokens are parties. Party identity proves difficult to read across the table without a fourth identifier, or the names and emblems fail to distinguish tokens for players who cannot rely on colour.

2026-07-29 Print the Pecking Order as a two-sheet A4 landscape component: one shared board with six numbered 44 mm party-token positions, low-to-high operative guidance, normal-direction Revolving Door guidance, and a long edge for covered counterbids; plus one sheet containing six movable 44 mm party tokens that repeat the prototype colours, party numbers, emblems, and short names. 2026-07-29 Use Pecking Order as the single name for the ordered party track, its board, and its auction. Include one automatic Pecking Order auction in every Campaign Round. It has no opener or opening bid and accepts ordinary counterbids from each player’s existing allowance. Cancel equal-Clout bids normally, rank the survivors from most Clout to least, then execute their operation tokens from the lowest bid to the highest so the highest bid acts last. Every operation token swaps one adjacent pair of party tokens. Resolve the Pecking Order first, then perform its Revolving Door in the normal high-to-low ranking direction before resolving party contests in the resulting order. Guaranteed access makes the shared resolution order an active system every round without charging one player an opening opportunity. Reversed execution lets larger commitments answer the changes below them, while the unchanged Revolving Door preserves the resource-transfer incentives used by every other contest. The auction feels compulsory without being interesting, reverse execution is routinely confused with reverse transfer, the lack of an opening bid produces too many cancellations, the combined name creates ambiguity, or the highest bidder’s final say makes lower bids irrelevant.
2026-07-29 Use saturated prototype colours for persistent party identity and state: Honeycomb yellow, Old Shell green, Foxglove pink, Riverworks blue, Many Wings orange, and Night Parliament black. House tints may reuse party hues, but lobbying-house components must not use large saturated identity fields. Party Support and related state are distributed across the map and need immediate colour recognition. Only a limited number of each player’s bid identifiers and one score marker need ownership recognition, so lobbying houses can rely on their repeated numbers, emblems, and patterns. Saturation and treatment separate the two systems more reliably than trying to reserve twelve unrelated hues. Physical prototype colours differ too much from their printed references, pale house pieces become difficult to associate across the table, black Support merges with map linework, or any house component is mistaken for party state.

2026-07-28 Use a sixteen-district ring-and-cross map for the first prototype. Twelve districts form an outer ring.
