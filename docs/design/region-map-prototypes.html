Three regions.
Three possible maps.

Each region holds 18 Support and elects up to 9 votes. Elections still resolve district by district. Each scoring card has three region–party objectives; the exact deck assignments remain open.

Concept studies, not adopted rules. Bellweather Centre shares a real border with every region in all three maps. Its current three Support spaces and separate scoring are retained here as a prototype assumption.

Compact hub

Three cities occupy the west, mixed districts the northeast, and small districts fan across the southeast.

Play hypothesis. Short routes and several borders between regions could make it easy to redirect a campaign. The Centre meets five districts.

Watch in playtesting. The western cities could become a self-contained contest if the eastern routes offer too little reason to cross.

Centre neighbors: Grand Market, Crown Road, Meadow, Westfield, Marsh. Total shared district borders: 32.

Rural fringe

A northern city belt sits above the Centre, with mixed districts to the southwest and a broad rural fringe to the southeast.

Play hypothesis. The outlying region reaches from the city belt to the southern edge, offering several possible entry routes.

Watch in playtesting. The far southern districts might still be divided peacefully; test how often players actually travel between regions.

Centre neighbors: Grand Market, Canal Ward, Westfield, Marsh. Total shared district borders: 33.

City corridor

Cities cluster at the western end. The Centre sits at the junction of a long mixed southern corridor and an expansive outlying region.

Play hypothesis. The large rural territory makes distance visible. Its southern border provides access from several mixed districts.

Watch in playtesting. Coast has a long border and little capacity: it could become a powerful movement route despite electing only one vote.

Centre neighbors: Grand Market, Ironwood, Northgate, Meadow, Heath. Total shared district borders: 32.

What to compare

Start with the compact hub for a direct test of regional scoring. Compare whether the rural fringe offers useful alternative approaches, and whether the city corridor creates contested routes or merely gives players somewhere quiet to expand.

All three studies have more shared borders than the current map's 18. That is an exploratory topology choice: extra routes might encourage interference, but could also make it easier to bypass opponents.
