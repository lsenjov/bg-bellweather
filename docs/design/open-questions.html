Open questions

Resolve the questions that unlock a prototype before the ones that decorate it.

Index/Open questions

The initial ladder-era question queue remains as design history. Current unresolved rules and playtest watches follow it in a separate list.

Archived initial queue / draft 00
Priority Question Why it matters Smallest useful test
01 What does the ladder represent? Position could be progress, turn order, price, status, or eligibility; each creates a different game. Sketch one loop for each interpretation using the same six-space track.
02 What are players bidding to gain or change? The prize determines whether bids express valuation, timing, denial, or prediction. Run five contests with one recurring prize and no scoring layer.
03 What does a bid cost? Reusable, spent, and future-punishing bids produce very different incentives. Compare finite coins with a bid that moves a player backward.
04 How are bids made and revealed? Open, sealed, simultaneous, and once-around bidding create different information games. Play the same contest three times using open, sealed, and once-around bids.
05 Why does a player want to climb? The ladder needs an intrinsic incentive or a clear relationship to winning. Test immediate privileges against end-game position scoring.
06 Can players fall, block, pass, or share a rung? Movement constraints define the game’s sharpest interaction. Prototype exclusive rungs first, then allow sharing and compare.
07 What happens to a losing bidder? Repeated non-results can create runaway leaders or disengagement. Compare keeping the bid, gaining priority, and moving on another track.
08 What ends the game? The end trigger determines whether climbing is a race, a timer, or a positional contest. Test reaching the top against a fixed number of auctions.
09 What theme makes these incentives intuitive? Theme should explain the loop after the loop proves interesting. Retheme one proven prototype, not the whole possibility space.

Current mechanic questions

Status Question Why it matters Smallest useful test
Open rule What concrete effect does each compatible operation have at each party? The six parties and four operation families define compatibility, but the board has no outcomes for Campaign, Expansion, Platform, or Coalition operatives to change. Give each party one state and define one effect for each of its two compatible operations, then repeat the influence loop without Election Day scoring.
Open rule How do constituencies, Election Day, government formation, and lobbying-house victory work? The theme separates party success from player success, but there is no end trigger or board outcome that determines a winner. Compare simple influence in successful parties with public policy objectives across a fixed four-round test.
Open component How many operatives of each family does a lobbying house begin with? The fixed identical supply controls how often each party can be influenced and how quickly operative compatibility becomes public through transfers. Start with two operatives from each family only as a test matrix, then record unused cards and concentrated bundles.
Open rule What happens if players attempt to open an influence contest on the same party or the Press Perch? Targets may host only one contest, but opening choices are made simultaneously and remain visible. Try the physical selection procedure with three players and deliberately contest one party.
Playtest watch Do legal last-second counterbid moves improve bluffing or merely prevent informed reactions? The timed phase deliberately permits sniping, but it may reward speed, vigilance, or reach more than judgment. Record every move made in the final five seconds, when each player stops revising, and whether opponents felt they had a meaningful response.
Playtest watch Do uncapped operative bundles create worthwhile risk or overwhelming action bursts? Operative supply is the only limit, so one bid may produce a long resolution before transferring a highly concentrated asset. Record the largest bundle, its resolution time, and whether another player would willingly make the same wager.
Playtest watch Does placing an additional bid on one’s own influence contest create varied tactics or compulsory insurance? An opener can occupy several ranks and may be able to route valuable opening resources back to themselves. Track how often players self-bid, whether the choice protects value, and what they gave up elsewhere to do it.
Playtest watch Does unrestricted nonbinding negotiation deepen the live phase or overwhelm it? Bluffs and threats may enrich visible bid movement, but social pressure and kingmaking may dominate under time pressure. Record which agreements affected placements, which were broken, and whether quieter players could participate meaningfully.
Playtest watch Does the Revolving Door make circular transfer easier to predict without implying that a party receives the bid? The theme should clarify that lobbying houses exchange complete influence packages after every contest, even though the party was the target. Ask players to predict each transfer before moving any Clout or operatives and record every mistaken destination.
