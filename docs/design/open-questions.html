Archived initial queue / draft 00
Priority Question Why it matters Smallest useful test
01 What does the ladder represent? Position could be progress, turn order, price, status, or eligibility; each creates a different game. Sketch one loop for each interpretation using the same six-space track.
02 What are players bidding to gain or change? The prize determines whether bids express valuation, timing, denial, or prediction. Run five contests with one recurring prize and no scoring layer.
03 What does a bid cost? Reusable, spent, and future-punishing bids produce very different incentives. Compare finite coins with a bid that moves a player backward.
04 How are bids made and revealed? Open, sealed, simultaneous, and once-around bidding create different information games. Play the same contest three times using open, sealed, and once-around bids.
05 Why does a player want to climb? The ladder needs an intrinsic incentive or a clear relationship to winning. Test immediate privileges against end-game position scoring.
06 Can players fall, block, pass, or share a rung? Movement constraints define the game’s sharpest interaction. Prototype exclusive rungs first, then allow sharing and compare.
07 What happens to a losing bidder? Repeated non-results can create runaway leaders or disengagement. Compare keeping the bid, gaining priority, and moving on another track.
08 What ends the game? The end trigger determines whether climbing is a race, a timer, or a positional contest. Test reaching the top against a fixed number of auctions.
09 What theme makes these incentives intuitive? Theme should explain the loop after the loop proves interesting. Retheme one proven prototype, not the whole possibility space.

Current mechanic questions

Status Question Why it matters Smallest useful test
Playtest watch Do the twelve party bonuses remain distinct and worth first refusal without overshadowing their baselines? Every operation has three party expressions, but global movement, conversion, double effects, persistent protection, and delayed actions may differ sharply in practical value. Record every claimed, declined, and unusable bonus, the bid rank that received it, and whether the baseline alone would have justified the operation card.
Playtest watch Do hidden district–party objectives and partial Support draws reward deliberate map play? Each card can still score at most six base points, but a coalition broadens which parties’ Support matches while shared random draws may either create useful election uncertainty or conceal the effect of four Campaign Rounds of Support movement. At each Election Day, record every objective’s own-party and coalition-partner Support before the draw, points actually drawn, and whether its holder knowingly influenced that district, party, or coalition.
Playtest watch Are four Campaign Rounds per Election Day and twelve total Campaign Rounds the right clock? The fixed 4/4/4 structure produces twelve complete Campaign Rounds regardless of player count, contest count, or insolvency. Record elapsed time and meaningful board changes in each four-round period, then ask whether each Election Day arrived too early, too late, or at a useful turning point.
Playtest watch Does reciprocal coalition scoring make Court actions and overtures strategically valuable without overpowering district control? Each drawn Support belonging to either coalition partner matches objectives for both, so a coalition can broaden scoring immediately before Election Day. Record every coalition at each Election Day, the points each partner’s Support contributed to the other, and which Court actions formed or broke those coalitions.
Open component Do the sleeved scoring cards need printed backs? The 40 × 61 mm fronts fit the selected sleeves, but clear sleeves and thin paper may expose hidden agendas without a common back or opaque insert. Test the front-only cards in the intended 41 × 63 mm sleeves under ordinary table lighting before designing backs.
Playtest watch Does the sixteen-district map make both outer-ring distance and Crownwater causeway movement matter? The three large outer districts should attract Support while the low-capacity causeways provide alternate routes between sectors; either structure could dominate and flatten spatial choices. Track every Organise origin and destination, whether its shortest route uses a causeway, and the free spots remaining at each capacity after every contest.
Playtest watch Do the long party boards keep bids, bonuses, and overtures readable without consuming too much table space? The boards combine the contest target with persistent state, but their opening-bid areas must fit a 40 × 61 mm firm identity card and six boards occupy more space than one central track. Print all three sheets at actual size, use the intended identity cards, and run one contest at every party on an ordinary table.
Playtest watch Does unlimited Support with all three 6-capacity districts full at setup create useful pressure? District capacity is the only placement limit. The three largest districts begin full, so early Rally effects there depend on a move or removal creating a vacancy. Record every failed placement, every vacancy created and claimed in a 6-capacity district, and whether unlimited supply makes Support growth feel automatic elsewhere.
Playtest watch Does the player-count-scaled 2 Organise / 4 Rally / 2 Smear / 1 Court supply create enough growth without making Rally bids routine? Rally is twice as common as Smear so board development should outpace direct removal. Players at two or three begin with double the standard supply, and every card can concentrate in another player’s reserve through gifts. Record each card’s bids and gifts, every round in which a family is unavailable to a player, and whether the additional Rally cards create more Support or merely taller bid stacks.
Playtest watch Does having no Support create a useful recovery state or a desirable global-Smear strategy? A supportless party may Smear anywhere until it re-enters through Organise or Rally, so absence may provide offensive flexibility rather than merely prevent elimination. Record every voluntary or forced loss of final Support, all global Smears, and how soon the party re-enters.
Playtest watch Do limited district spots create tactical vacancies or frequent failed actions? Organise and Rally require free spots while Smear creates them, but a crowded map may make operation legality too brittle. Record each failed spatial operation, each contested vacancy, and the number of free spots after every contest.
Playtest watch Do legal last-second counterbid moves improve bluffing or merely prevent informed reactions? A timed phase deliberately permits sniping, but it may reward speed, vigilance, or reach more than judgment. When using a timer, record every move made in the final five seconds, when each player stops revising, and whether opponents felt they had a meaningful response.
Playtest watch Do uncapped operation-card bundles create worthwhile risk or overwhelming action bursts? Operation-card supply is the only limit, so one bid may produce a long resolution before transferring a highly concentrated asset. Record the largest bundle, its resolution time, and whether another player would willingly make the same wager.
Playtest watch Does placing an additional bid on one’s own influence contest create varied tactics or compulsory insurance? An opener can occupy several ranks and may be able to route valuable opening resources back to themselves. Track how often players self-bid, whether the choice protects value, and what they gave up elsewhere to do it.
Playtest watch Do atomic gifts and socially enforced deals deepen negotiation without overwhelming play? Gifts can redistribute Clout, Bluff, and operation cards and points outside the Revolving Door. One-way transfers and unenforced agreements may create social pressure, kingmaking, or misunderstandings about reciprocity. Record every gift and promise, the resources and points moved, any broken promise or rules dispute, and whether quieter players could participate meaningfully.
Playtest watch Does the Revolving Door make circular transfer easier to predict without implying that a party receives the bid? The theme should clarify that lobbying firms exchange complete influence packages after every contest, even though the party was the target. Ask players to predict each transfer before moving any bid cards and record every mistaken destination.
