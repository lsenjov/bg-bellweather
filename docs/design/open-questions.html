Questions for testing the replacement yearly loop.

Index/Open questions

The rules below are playable. These questions identify balance, pacing, and component choices still requiring evidence; they are not hidden rules.

First testDoes a third Organise card improve movement without crowding out the other Operations?The added card should make routes and relocation more available, but an eleven-card standard hand may make Organise-heavy piles less valuable or let movement dominate objective play.Track unused Organise cards, Organise cards played and collected, failed route plans, and turns where another Operation family was unavailable.
First testDoes the Operate–Collect–Close loop create meaningful timing pressure?Players need reasons to feed a party pile, harvest it, wait for more cards, or secure it through their opening marker.Play two years and record pile size at every Collect and Closure, plus every turn a player wanted to wait but acted from fear of losing the pile.
First testDoes the strict-majority Closure ending arrive at a satisfying time?Ending mid-orbit is deliberately sharp, but it may cut off too many plans or make third and fourth Closures automatic.Record who closes each party, the turn count, which players lose expected actions, and whether the ending player had a real alternative.
First testAre two Collections per year enough at four to six players and four enough at two or three?Too few makes openings deterministic ownership; too many drains piles before Closure matters.Track unused counters, desired-but-unavailable Collections, repeated Collections at one party, and cards gained by Collect versus Closure.
First testDo collectible Bonus cards improve the Operate–Collect–Close tension?Cards can be held indefinitely, played through reciprocal partners, and return home after use, creating a second circulation loop beside public Operation piles.Record every award, decline, play, return, recollection, reciprocal-partner use, and turn where a Bonus card changes a Collect or Closure choice.
BalanceDo the three city spokes create useful Centre access without making the full starting cities compulsory gateways?Every party begins in each capacity-six city, but those cities begin full. Their direct spokes may create productive movement and Smear pressure or simply relocate congestion from the outer capacity-two junctions.Record every move, bonus, and Smear across a city–spoke border; note when city capacity blocks a desired route and compare Centre entries through each arm.
BalanceDoes Quiet Hours give Night Parliament too much unrestricted expansion?Its empty-district restriction is thematic and self-limiting, but global reach can establish remote objectives or occupy Bellweather Centre without a supporting route.Record every opportunity, claim, unavailable claim, target distance, new objective presence, and Quiet Hours placement in Bellweather Centre.
BalanceDoes Capital scoring at 0/0/1/3 reward Centre presence without dominating ordinary agendas?The Centre persists through Elections and has only three spaces, so modest points may still create strong blocking incentives.Record Capital points, ordinary points, who occupied each Centre space, and actions taken primarily to deny the third named party.
BalanceDoes the fixed first Capital card in low-player pairs feel fair?The lock removes a scoring-time choice but particular registered pairs may differ in Centre feasibility.Compare Capital opportunities by pair across repeated setups and ask whether players could remember which first card mattered.
PacingDoes the player who ends the year make a healthy next Early Bird?The reward is tempo next year, while players later in the current orbit may lose actions.Record deliberate year endings, the following ABBA/ABCCBA openings, and whether one player can chain the role too easily.
PacingDo six years give each agenda enough development time?Two-year cycles halve openings, Lobby turns, Collection-counter refreshes, and card-circulation opportunities before each Election.Complete all six years; time every stage and record unfinished agenda plans, stranded New Year cards, and Operation cards that never returned to a useful hand.
IdentityShould the working title change now that bidding has been removed?“Ladder Bidding” describes neither the current action loop nor its strongest table moment.Wait until players consistently name the game’s central tension in their own words.
